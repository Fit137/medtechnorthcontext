# ADR-012 — Monetise the clinician room rather than chase physicians

**Status:** Accepted

## Context
The first room drew 10+ practising clinicians (dentists, clinical pharmacists, physiotherapists) and **zero physicians**. Two MDs withdrew for structural reasons: one over the residential venue, one over low physician count.

## The question
Chase MDs with an external venue and enterprise convening first, or double down on the clinicians who actually showed up?

## Scoring
Weights: speed to revenue 0.30 · uses the existing asset 0.25 · cost and risk 0.20 · builds toward the bigger business 0.15 · defensibility 0.10

| Path | Score |
|---|---|
| **Monetise the clinician room, let it fund the MD build** | **9.40** |
| Double down on clinicians as an end in itself | 8.95 |
| Chase MDs, external venue, enterprise convening first | 4.50 |

## The premise that was wrong
"Convening requires MDs as a higher authority than clinicians." **There is no universal clinical hierarchy for convening.** Authority is category-specific. For dental implants, imaging, materials and practice software, dentists are the entire market and there is no MD above them in the purchase decision. For vaccines and minor ailments, pharmacists are the channel.

## Supporting timing
Both groups had a commercial earthquake on 1 July 2026: Ontario pharmacy scope expansion and CDCP expansion.

## Also
Dentists and pharmacists may be the *stronger* commercial room. A dentist owns a practice and can buy in three weeks. A hospital MD sits inside the slowest procurement machinery in the country.

## Language constraint
Never let "MDs are a higher authority than clinicians" enter how this is described, even internally. Dentists, pharmacists and physiotherapists are regulated health professionals providing direct patient care. They are clinicians by any definition that matters, including their own.

## MD strategy
Pursue as a later chapter, not a precondition. Cheapest channel is nomination from the clinicians already in the room. Both dropouts become winnable once paid sessions run in professional venues with a published brief behind them.
