# Growth Flywheel

## The core reframe

**Ask guests for things that raise their own standing, not things that help you.**

A nomination is not a favour to MedTech North. "Who should be at this table?" is a question about their judgement, and being asked it is flattering. The moment it becomes "refer a colleague and get a perk", it converts a status act into a transaction and makes free feel like it had strings.

**The test for every ask: would they want to do this even if you got nothing from it?**

---

## The mechanics, scored

Weights: preserves guest status 0.30 · multiplier per guest 0.25 · composition control 0.20 · operating effort 0.15 · brings paying-side people 0.10

| Mechanic | Status | Multiplier | Control | Effort | Paying-side | **Score** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Panel and cohort moderation** | 10 | 8 | 9 | 8 | 6 | **8.60** |
| **Chapter clinical lead** | 10 | 9 | 9 | 5 | 7 | **8.50** |
| **Institutional host session** | 10 | 10 | 7 | 6 | 5 | **8.30** |
| **Nomination rights** | 10 | 5 | 9 | 9 | 7 | **8.10** |
| Advisory contribution to the brief | 9 | 4 | 9 | 7 | 5 | 7.05 |
| Asking them to post on LinkedIn | 4 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 5 | 6.40 |
| Guest-of-guest passes | 8 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 5 | 6.10 |
| **Referral rewards and incentives** | 2 | 6 | 6 | 7 | 4 | **4.75** |

**The finding: the top four are status roles, not referral mechanics.** Referral rewards score worst, and counterintuitively the more you incentivise it the less it works, because payment reframes a judgement call as a job.

## The loop

1. A guest arrives **nominated**, not applied. Higher status from the first second
2. The room is good. Do nothing else at this stage
3. They get a **status role**: moderate a cohort, speak on a recorded panel, host at their institution
4. That role is **visible to their network** without you asking for anything
5. The role naturally raises the question of who else belongs
6. They nominate
7. Back to step one, their judgement now publicly validated

**The elegant move is step four.** Do not ask them to share your content. Manufacture the artifact *they* want to share. A recorded panel with their name on it gets posted by them, tagged by their institution, and seen by exactly the peers you want. No ask was made, and the distribution beats any referral scheme.

## The three asks that multiply

**Institutional host.** One clinician hosting at their hospital brings 8 to 12 colleagues at once, in their own building, at near-zero cost. The single largest multiplier available. A department head bringing trainees adds the student pipeline.

**Chapter clinical lead.** A named ongoing role for one respected clinician per city. Status, real influence, and it makes them a co-owner of the room's quality. This is also how curation eventually gets delegated, which the financial model flags as the binding operational constraint.

**Moderation and panels.** Cheapest to run, highest status per unit of effort, produces the shareable artifact.

---

## The nomination question, worded correctly

**Do not ask a clinician to nominate another clinician.** That only grows the free side, and the model needs roughly 1.5 guests per paying member.

**Ask it open: "Who should be at this table?"**

A clinician's network is not only clinicians. It contains the device rep who calls on them, the startup that spun out of their department, the industry partner funding their lab, the procurement lead they argue with. Asked openly, the question surfaces all four groups.

**High-value variant: "Which company should be in this room?"** A clinician naming a company is a warm path to a paying member or sponsor, and being asked which companies matter is flattering rather than extractive. You are monetising their judgement, never their contacts.

## Sequencing

| When | The ask |
|---|---|
| First room | Nothing. Host them well. That is the whole job |
| After first room | "Who should be at this table?" One question, personal follow-up, not a form |
| Second or third room | A status role. Panel, moderation, or a topic they want discussed |
| Ongoing | Institutional host, chapter lead |

**Asking on night one is the most common way to cheapen this.** The nomination is worth more after they have something to nominate people into.

---

## Language

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Refer a colleague" | "Who should be at this table?" |
| "Help us grow" | "Your nomination carries weight here" |
| "Share this post" | Record them, and let them share it |
| "You get X for every referral" | Nothing. No rewards, ever |
| "Bring a guest" | "Is there someone whose absence you noticed?" |

That last one gets better answers than the direct question, because it makes them scan the room rather than their contact list.

---

## Metrics

- Nominations per guest per year
- Nomination-to-attendance conversion
- People per institutional host session
- **Guardrail: guest-to-payer ratio near 1.5:1.** Past 2:1, pause guest recruitment and push builder acquisition

---

## What breaks it

The moment a guest suspects the nomination request is a growth tactic rather than a genuine question, the mechanic dies and takes trust with it.

**Therefore:** keep volume low, one or two nominations a year per person · always personal, always from the founder · never automated, never a form email · **reject nominations that do not fit.** A nomination that is always accepted is not an honour.
