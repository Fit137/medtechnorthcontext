# Clinician vertical selection

> **STATUS: DEFERRED, NOT WITHDRAWN.** `ADR-025` moved the restaurant format to a builders room with no clinicians in it, because practice owners are the hardest audience in Canadian outpatient health to reach cold and four weeks is not enough. **The analysis below stays valid and unchanged for whenever a clinician-led room is attempted.** Dentistry remains the correct first vertical when that happens.
>
> **INTERNAL.** Which profession a clinician-led room should be built for, and why.

---

## The criterion that actually decides it

Not which room is most interesting. **Which room has the deepest ecosystem of companies willing to pay to be in it**, because the paying side carries the event and the invited side never will.

Everything else is secondary to that.

## Scoring

Six criteria, scored 1 to 10.

| Criterion | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Density** | Can you find 500 of them inside a 45 minute drive |
| **Owner rate** | Do they control a budget, or does head office |
| **Vendor depth** | How many companies would pay $650 to $1,500 to sit in that room |
| **Reachability** | Public register, named owners, findable and messageable |
| **Evening viability** | Income, schedule, and whether a weeknight out is realistic |
| **Brand fit** | Does the resulting room look like the brand, in photographs |

| | Dentistry | Community pharmacy | Physiotherapy and rehab |
|---|---|---|---|
| Density | **10** | 9 | 8 |
| Owner rate | **9** | 6 | 8 |
| Vendor depth | **10** | 8 | 6 |
| Reachability | **9** | 8 | 7 |
| Evening viability | **8** | 6 | 6 |
| Brand fit | 8 | **8** | 7 |
| **Total /60** | **54** | 45 | 42 |
| **Score** | **90** | 75 | 70 |

## 1. Dentistry. 90. Pick this one

**Vendor depth is the deciding number and dentistry wins it outright.** Practice management software, imaging and CBCT, dental labs, practice-transition brokers and consolidators, dental-specific accountants and lawyers, equipment finance, supply distribution. Every one of those categories already spends money on trade events, already understands paying to be in a room with practice owners, and already has a budget line for it. No education is required to make the sale.

Owner rate is the second reason. A dentist in the GTA is usually an owner or a co-owner, which means the person at the table is the person who signs. Reachability is strong: the RCDSO register is public, and practices publish their owners' names on their own websites.

The single weakness is fatigue. Dentists are heavily courted, sit through a dense continuing education circuit, and get invited to vendor evenings constantly. **That is exactly why the paid tier is an object rather than access, and why nothing is asked of the invited side.** An evening where a dentist is given professional portraits and asked for nothing is not the invitation they are used to deleting.

## 2. Community pharmacy. 75. The best second, and the better topical hook

Pharmacy has the stronger reason to meet **right now**. As of 1 July 2026 Ontario pharmacists prescribe for 28 minor ailments, up from 19, with five more planned for early 2027, plus six additional publicly funded vaccines. Over 99% of Ontario pharmacies participate and 2.4 million assessments have already been delivered. All verified in `data/verified-stats.md`. That is a live scope-of-practice expansion, and a room about what it actually changes writes its own invitation.

It loses on two things that matter more.

**Owner rate.** Chains dominate. Independent owners exist but franchisees run on constrained budgets and often cannot commit an evening.

**Vendor constraints.** The pharmaceutical side operates under the Innovative Medicines Canada code and provincial rules governing interactions with pharmacists, including limits on hospitality. That does not make the room unsellable, it makes every sale slower and every hospitality decision a compliance question. Wrong for an MVP with a six week clock.

**Run it second, in the new year, once the format is proven and the hospitality question can be answered properly.**

## 3. Physiotherapy and rehab. 70. Right ecosystem, thinner wallet

High owner rate and a genuine appetite to meet. The vendor ecosystem is real but shallow and much less cash-rich: practice software, equipment, insurance billing support. Fewer companies clear $1,500 for an evening without a long conversation first. Income is meaningfully lower than dentistry, so a weeknight out competes harder against a clinic shift.

**Good third dinner. Wrong first one.**

## The wildcard, named and dismissed

**Veterinary practice owners** score higher than physiotherapy on every commercial measure. Enormous vendor spend, very high owner rate, dense in the GTA, chronically underserved by good events. It is dismissed because it is not Canadian health innovation for patients, and the first Practice Dinner is also the first thing many people in the sector will see under this name. Revisit once the brand can afford an adjacency.

## Single vertical, and when two is allowed

**One profession per dinner.** The reason is commercial, not philosophical: a vendor buys a defined audience, and stacking professions divides the value of the room by the number of professions in it.

Stacking is correct for a $12 open mixer, where volume at the top of the funnel is the only goal and nobody is buying a defined audience. Copying it here would be copying the mechanics of a different business.

**The one permitted widening is along the vertical's own referral chain.** For dentistry, that means general practice owners, plus the specialists they refer to, plus dental lab owners. Same ecosystem, same commercial category, no dilution. Use it if volume is short at day −14. Never widen across unrelated professions.
