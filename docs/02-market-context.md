# Market Context — The Argument

The pitch has three parts: a large asset, a chronic failure, and a recent change that makes the failure urgent. Every figure here traces to `../data/verified-stats.md`.

---

## 1. The asset is real

- **8th largest medical device market in the world**
- **1,500+ medtech companies**
- **6,000+ life sciences companies**, 4th most active biotech sector in the OECD
- **~US$10 billion** medical device market (2024)
- **$344 billion** in annual health spending, **12.1% of GDP** (2023)
- **Over one third** of Canadian patients used virtual care in 2023
- **Lowest-cost G7 country** for biotech and clinical trials
- Ontario holds roughly **46.8%** of Canada's medical device companies; Quebec **22.3%**

## 2. The chronic failure

**More than a year, best case, from lab to bedside.** Many devices take several years. Some never arrive.

**76% of Ontario's academic hospitals** name policies, directives and procurement regulations as a major hurdle to adopting innovation. The system describing itself.

> ⚠️ **Age warning.** That 76% figure comes from a 2016 CAHO survey. It is still the most-cited number on this, but always attribute it as "a survey of Ontario's academic hospitals found" rather than implying it is current. If challenged, concede the vintage immediately and pivot to the 2026 policy changes below.

**The loop nobody breaks:** without pilots there is no data, without data there is no adoption, without adoption there are no pilots.

**Thirteen jurisdictions**, each with different priorities, privacy legislation and procurement rules. There is no single door to knock on.

**This is not an invention problem. It is a proximity problem, and proximity can be designed.**

## 3. Why now — the moving part

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **11 Dec 2025** | Buy Ontario Act receives royal assent |
| **16 Dec 2025** | Federal Buy Canadian policy takes effect. **Health and pharmaceuticals is one of five named strategic sectors** |
| **15 Jun 2026** | Threshold for Canadian preference drops from $25M to $5M |
| **1 Jul 2026** | Ontario pharmacists gain 9 additional minor ailments (19 → 28) plus 6 newly publicly funded vaccines including RSV, shingles and pneumococcal |
| **1 Jul 2026** | CDCP 2026-27 benefit year begins, expanded to all eligible Canadians |

**The argument:** policy has created preference for Canadian health technology faster than the relationships that would serve it. A hospital can prefer a Canadian supplier and still not know who they are.

### Know the counterargument

A June 2026 Policy Options piece argued Buy Canadian will not cost or deliver much, since it grants only a modest price advantage on a narrow band of large procurements.

**Concede this cheerfully. It strengthens the case:** the policy signals intent, and intent does not create pathways. Relationships do.

## The "why did you build this" answer

> "Canada is the eighth largest medical device market in the world. Fifteen hundred medtech companies. Three hundred and forty-four billion dollars a year on health.
>
> And a Canadian device, best case, takes more than a year to get from a lab to a patient's bedside. In practice, many take several years.
>
> That is not an invention problem. Three quarters of Ontario's academic hospitals say procurement rules are a major hurdle to adopting innovation. The founder cannot reach the clinician. The clinician sees the product after the design is frozen. The buyer will not move without evidence, there is no evidence without a pilot, and there is no pilot without someone in the room who can say yes.
>
> That has been true for twenty years. [PAUSE]
>
> What changed is that in December, health became one of five strategic sectors under Buy Canadian. In June, the threshold dropped to five million dollars. For the first time there is real preference for buying Canadian health technology.
>
> So the demand is arriving faster than the relationships that would serve it.
>
> That is what I built. Not because I wanted to run events. Because an introduction that takes eighteen months should take one dinner."

**Change only the last beat by audience:**

| Audience | Closing line |
|---|---|
| Clinician | "...and because you should see what's being built while your opinion can still change it." |
| Founder | "...and because you should not spend a year of runway chasing one meeting." |
| Policy | "...and because the policy you passed needs somewhere for those conversations to happen." |
| Corporate | "...and because you cannot assemble that room, and we can, in two weeks." |
| Investor | "...and because the companies that clear this gap are the ones worth backing." |
