# Financial Model

Basis for a full model, not the full model. Built as an 11-tab XLSX with 246 live formulas. This document carries the findings and the drivers.

**Framing for any external reader:** this projects a business that has held one room. Every number is a hypothesis with arithmetic attached. Presented that way it is credible.

---

## The three findings

### 1. Each chapter has a hard ceiling of ~35 paying members

```
40 seats per dinner
× 50% reserved for hosted guests (1:1 composition rule)
= 20 paying seats per dinner
× 4 dinners per year (quarterly)
= 80 paying seat-slots
÷ 2.3 dinners attended by a typical member
= 34.8 paying members per chapter
```

Five chapters at quarterly cadence cap membership revenue at roughly **$136,000/year.**

**Cadence is the lever inside a city:**

| Dinners/city/year | Ceiling per chapter | 5 chapters |
|---|---|---|
| 4 (quarterly) | 35 | $136,000 |
| 6 | 52 | $205,000 |
| 8 | 70 | $273,000 |
| 12 (monthly) | 104 | $410,000 |

**Three consequences:** growth comes from cities and cadence not from selling harder into a full chapter · membership can never be the main revenue line · a waitlist is a feature, because it is the scarcity proof that prices sponsorship.

**Counter-intuitive corollary:** higher attendance *lowers* the ceiling, because engaged members consume more of a fixed seat supply. Success on engagement constrains membership growth.

### 2. Membership is under 30% of revenue at every stage

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Membership share | 29.1% | 27.7% | 24.9% |
| Sponsorship share | 50.1% | 45.8% | 50.1% |

Membership is not the product being sold. It is the mechanism that makes the room provable enough to sell sponsorship against.

### 3. The dinner programme is exactly self-funding at base, and swings $160K

At year-two base: 20 dinners × 40 seats × $80 blended = $64,000 delivery, against 20 × $4,000 × 80% attach = $64,000 revenue. **Exactly zero.**

Annual dinner programme net, 20 dinners:

| Partner-hosted share ↓ / Sponsor → | $0 | $2,000 | $4,000 | $6,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% (all restaurant) | −$84,000 | −$44,000 | −$4,000 | +$36,000 |
| 50% (base) | −$64,000 | −$24,000 | **+$16,000** | +$56,000 |
| 100% (all partner-hosted) | −$44,000 | +$4,000 | +$36,000 | +$76,000 |

**A $160,000 annual swing. No pricing decision is worth as much as venue partnerships and sponsor attach.** That is where founder time goes.

**The operating rule that follows: no dinner goes on the calendar without either a per-dinner sponsor at $3,000–7,500 or a donated partner venue.**

## Unit economics per member per year

| Tier | Fee | Contribution, unsponsored | Contribution, sponsored | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guest | $0 | ~$209 cost | the magnet | n/a |
| Chapter | $290 | $36 | $220 | 76% |
| National | $790 | $348 | $580 | 73% |
| Circle | $2,950 | $1,625 | $2,105 | 71% |

**Chapter at $36 unsponsored** is the tier-level expression of finding 3. **Guest is not a loss line**, it is customer acquisition cost for the sponsorship business. **Twelve Circle members contribute more than eighty Chapter members.**

**Cost floor:** at typical redemption with unsponsored dinners, servicing a National member costs $442 and the 60% margin floor is $1,105, above the $790 price. Sponsored, cost drops to $210 and the floor to $525, cleared at 73.4%.

## Three-year build

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapters live | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| Dinners / summits | 12 / 1 | 20 / 2 | 24 / 2 |
| Guest members | 90 | 200 | 280 |
| Paying members | 83 | 150 | 180 |
| Capacity utilisation | 80% | 86% | 86% |
| **Total revenue** | **$207,150** | **$423,380** | **$561,460** |
| Total costs | $137,520 | $300,950 | $413,675 |
| **Net contribution** | **$69,630** | **$122,430** | **$147,785** |
| Net margin | 33.6% | 28.9% | 26.3% |
| Revenue per paying member | $2,496 | $2,823 | $3,119 |

**Margin declines because founder compensation enters** at $60,000 in year two and $130,000 in year three. Intended, not deterioration. Most models of this type hide the founder cost.

**Revenue per member rises without any price increase.** Sponsorship per member is why.

| **Net contribution** | **$31,800** | **$147,200** | **$236,840** |

**The bear case clears zero.** It assumes half the dinners unsponsored, one category partner, two underfilled chapters and summit revenue 39% below plan.

**Price is not flexed in any scenario**, because the risk is filling rooms and closing sponsors, not whether $790 is right.

## Break-even

At base sponsorship, sponsorship and summit contribution ($208,000) exceeds the entire fixed and compensation base ($122,000) by $86,000.

**Break-even paying members: zero.** Every membership dollar is contribution. Sponsorship alone covers the fixed base.

## What a full model still needs

1. **Monthly cohort engine.** Rolling join dates, deferred revenue on annual memberships, per-cohort retention.
2. **Cash timing.** Sponsorship arrives in lumps, dinner costs are paid on the night, memberships collected up front. Build a 13-week view.
3. **Seat-by-seat summit build.** Replace the blended line with pass types, booth tiers and sponsor cheques.
4. **Chapter unit model.** One chapter as a repeatable P&L so expansion is adding units.
5. **Actuals capture.** Three dinners of real data will move this model more than any further modelling.
