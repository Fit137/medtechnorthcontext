# Buyer-Led Room Design

> **INTERNAL. Never published.**

A method for designing a room backwards from what a specific buyer needs, plus the guardrail that stops the method destroying the asset, plus one worked case.

---

## The method

Normal sequence: compose a room, then find someone to fund it. Buyer-led sequence: start from what a named buyer's function is measured on, work out which room produces that, then check whether the room is one MedTech North would want to run anyway.

The order matters because it inverts the usual failure. Most convening businesses build a room and then contort the pitch to fit whoever answers the phone. Working backwards produces a sharper room and a much shorter sales cycle, and it produces one specific danger that has to be handled explicitly.

### The four questions, in order

1. **What is this person measured on?** Not the company. The person holding the budget. A partnerships manager, a field marketing lead and a country manager are measured on different things and will buy different rooms.
2. **Which room produces that outcome?** Composition first, topic second.
3. **Would we run this room with no funder at all?** The gate. See below.
4. **Who else would fund the same room?** If the answer is nobody, the room is a bespoke service and should be priced and named as one.

---

## The gate: the no-funder test

`docs/05-monetization.md` kills one idea permanently: a single builder who both funds a room and curates its guest list, because that delivers clinicians as an audience and trades the asset for a cheque. Buyer-led design sits one step away from that, so the gate has to be explicit rather than assumed.

**The test: state the room's topic and composition with no company attached. Is it a room worth running?**

If yes, the funder shaped the theme, which is allowed and is what a co-host is for. If no, the funder is curating, and the room is a lead generation event wearing a network's clothes. Clinicians detect the difference in one evening, and it is not recoverable.

**Three rules that keep a buyer-led room on the right side of the line:**

- The funder influences the **theme**, never the **list**. They may nominate. MedTech North composes.
- **Clinicians are the authority in the room, not the audience.** If the design has clinicians listening while commercial people talk, it has already failed.
- **No pitching, no demo, no stage time beyond a welcome.** Unchanged in every format.

## Worked case: an AI customer support platform with a partnerships function

Used here because it starts from a company that failed the co-host screen in `data/cohost-targets.md` and asks whether a different room changes the answer. It does, and the reason it does is instructive.

### What changed in the facts

Two corrections to the earlier assessment, both material.

**They have real Toronto presence.** Sources conflict: some list San Francisco as headquarters, while Tracxn lists the company as Toronto-based, founded 2023, around 30 people in 2026 ✅. Their company and staff LinkedIn pages sit on the Canadian subdomain, and they have posted a Toronto-based growth role ✅. **Verify this directly before the meeting**, but the earlier statement that no Canadian office was found was wrong, and it was load-bearing in the previous conclusion.

**A partnerships function exists, with a person in Toronto and a budget.** This was not findable in public research and comes from direct observation of their activity. It changes the buyer entirely.

### Question 1: what is a partnerships manager measured on?

Not leads. Not brand. Partnership functions report on qualified partner conversations, partner agreements signed, partner-sourced or partner-influenced pipeline, and activated partners meaning those who have deployed at least one client.

For a bootstrapped, profitable, roughly 30-person company at about $10M ARR ✅, the partnership bet that matters is **borrowed distribution**: access to somebody else's installed base. Not logos, not co-marketing volume. Their product supports white-labelling on embeddable widgets as a paid add-on, with white-labelling included at the enterprise tier ✅, which is the signature of an agency and embed motion rather than a pure direct one.

**So the thing they cannot buy anywhere is a concentration of Canadian health software companies that each own an installed base of practices.** One practice-management vendor embedding their product reaches every practice on that vendor's platform at once. That is the asymmetric outcome their function exists to find, and there is no conference in Canada that assembles those specific people in a room small enough to have a real conversation.

### Question 2: which room produces that?

Not a room of dentists. A room where the demand side and the supply side of practice software are both present, with the demand side holding the authority.

**Topic: the front desk.** What actually happens at the front desk of Canadian dental practices and community pharmacies since July 2026. CDCP eligibility and claims questions after the April 2026 grid revision and the benefit year that opened 1 July. Booking load from the pharmacy scope expansion that took minor ailments from 19 to 28. Refill and vaccine scheduling against six newly funded vaccines. Where automation helps, where it fails, and what practices and patients will not tolerate.

**Format:** the Patient Journey Jam that has already run twice, pointed at the administrative journey rather than the clinical one. A live board mapping the path from "I need an appointment" to "my claim was rejected," annotated by the people who live it while the people building for them watch and ask.

**Composition target, room of roughly 32:**

| Group | Seats | Role in the room |
|---|---|---|
| Practising clinicians, practice and pharmacy owners, office and practice managers | 14 | **The authority.** They map the journey and say where it breaks |
| Health software founders, CTOs and product leads (practice management, patient engagement, booking, claims, virtual care) | 12 | The partner prospects. They are there to listen |
| Multi-site operations leaders (dental groups, pharmacy banners, clinic groups) | 3 | Both. They own support volume and they buy |
| Agencies and development shops serving healthcare | 3 | Channel prospects. **Hard cap**, per the service provider rule |
| Students and researchers | 2 | |
| Co-host seats | 2 | |

That lands close to the 1:1 builders-to-validators rule, counting practice owners and operations leaders as validators, which is what they are here. The agency cap is the number that will be under pressure and it is the one that must not move.

### Question 3: would we run this with no funder?

**Yes, comfortably.** The front desk is drowning in both professions for dated, verifiable reasons, clinicians have strong and specific views about it, and builders have no structured way to hear them. It passes the gate on its own merits, which is what makes it safe to sell.

### Question 4: who else would fund it?

Booking and scheduling platforms, patient communication vendors, claims and insurance verification companies, virtual care operators, telephony and voice companies, and several of the practice management vendors already on the Tier A list in `data/corporate-targets.md`. **The room is not bespoke**, which means it can be sold again and the first funder is not load-bearing.

**The compounding argument, which is the real reason to run this room regardless of who pays:** twelve health software companies in a room is twelve of MedTech North's own future buyers, experiencing the format from the inside. The room a partnerships budget funds this quarter is the pipeline for the next three.

## What the buyer receives, and what they never do

**Receives:** a written composition target before the room and a composition report after. A co-produced written report from the session, which for a content-led company is often worth more than the room itself, because primary clinician research in a vertical is exactly what they cannot manufacture. Recorded assets they may share on their own channels. Consented individual introductions afterwards, made personally.

**Never receives:** the attendee list, contact data, or any export. Guest list visibility before the room. Stage time beyond a welcome. A demo slot. Editorial control over the report. Any promised partnership, pipeline or commercial outcome, per `docs/08-legal-and-compliance.md`.

## Pricing and the budget-line move

They almost certainly cannot host thirty people at around thirty employees, so this is not the co-host product and should not carry co-host pricing.

| Line | Band |
|---|---|
| Session partner | $7,500 |
| Co-produced front desk report | $12,000 to $18,000 |
| **Bundle** | **$18,000 to $22,000** |

**The tactical move that matters more than the price: a partnerships manager does not buy sponsorships, they buy partnerships.** The same money is far easier to approve as partner activation, market development or co-marketing than as an event sponsorship, because the first two are lines their function already owns and reports against. Structure it as a partnership between the two organisations with the room and the report as the activation. Never use the word sponsorship in the conversation, which is also `ADR-007` behaviour applied to a private setting.

**Expect to be asked for measurability, and welcome it.** A company that reached this scale without outside funding did so by refusing spend it could not measure. That discipline is a reason to be unusually concrete about deliverables, not a reason to soften them. Composition is measurable and should be guaranteed in writing with a make-good. Outcomes are not measurable and must never be promised.

## The meeting

Since the buyer is reachable in Toronto, this is a conversation rather than a campaign.

**First third, their questions.** What kinds of partners are working now, agency, embed, referral or integration. What their Canadian healthcare distribution looks like today, which is the hypothesis to test and the answer is probably none. How they currently source a qualified partner conversation in a vertical they have no presence in, and what that costs. That last question sets the price without a figure being quoted, which matters because no comparison figure exists in `data/verified-stats.md`.

**Middle, the room.** Composition first, topic second, the report third. The never list stated plainly and early, because it protects them as much as it protects the room and it lands better as an opening than as a defence.

**Last, one rung and one date.** A single session with a stated composition and a make-good. Not a programme, not a season.

**The disqualifying answer to listen for:** if their partner motion is purely affiliate and volume-based rather than embed and enterprise, the room's value to them drops sharply and the honest move is to say so. A partnerships function chasing affiliate signups does not need a room of twelve vendors, and selling them one would be the kind of deal that produces a bad reference.
