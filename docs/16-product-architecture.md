# Product Architecture

> **INTERNAL.** Supersedes the pricing in `docs/14-dinner-programme.md` section 3a and the tier stack in the partner deck. Decision record: `ADR-022`.

Three products. They differ by **what is promised**, and price follows that, not headcount.

| | **Monthly Meetup** | **Calendar Dinner** | **Convening** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composition promised | **None** | **None** | **Yes, to their brief** |
| Room size | ~30 | 50 to 100 | Target 50, floor 25 |
| Cadence | Monthly, already running | Quarterly, planned | On demand |
| Venue | Our GTA venue | Our venue | Their HQ, or Toronto for US buyers |
| Attendee list | **Yes, opted-in only** | Never | Never |
| Presentation | **Yes** | Welcome only | No deck, no demo |
| Entry price | **$2,500** | **$4,500** | **$25,000** |

**The line that keeps this honest: the meetup sells exposure, the dinner and the convening sell composition.** Both are legitimate. They are not the same purchase and must never be described as if they were.

---

## 1. Monthly Meetup

Already running monthly in Mississauga, described as Toronto for buyers outside Canada. Roughly 30 people, a mix of builders, clinicians, researchers and students. Physicians are invited and have accepted; none has yet attended. **No composition is briefed and none is promised.**

**The first two of this series were co-hosted and paid for by Miro.** That is the product already sold twice, at the co-host end of the ladder.

**Sponsor: $2,500 per meetup. Annual, twelve meetups: $24,000 ($2,000 each).**

| They get | Detail |
|---|---|
| Presentation slot | 10 to 15 minutes on the venue's screen |
| **Opted-in attendee list** | Name, role, organisation, email, for attendees who ticked the consent box |
| Two seats | |
| Category exclusivity for that night | One sponsor per meetup |
| Named on the registration page | |

**Floor: 20 attending. Below that, the next meetup is free.**

### The list, and the only way it is legal

CASL requires **express** consent: an unchecked box, the sponsor named, at the point of registration. Pre-ticked boxes are not consent in Canada, and making consent a condition of attending is not meaningful consent under PIPEDA.

So the box is unchecked, the sponsor is named, and people who decline still attend.

**Sell "the attendees who opted in", never "the attendee list".** Expect 60 to 80% opt-in when the sponsor is named and relevant. Say that number honestly rather than implying everyone.

### Why this does not break the never list

Because the never list belongs to the dinner and the convening, and those promises are unchanged.

**Two rooms, two data promises, stated plainly to everyone in both.** The meetup is open, transactional and consent-based. The dinner and the convening are closed, and no contact data ever leaves them. Separating the two makes the premium clearer rather than weaker: a company paying $25,000 is buying the room where nobody is being harvested.

**The rule that protects it: a meetup attendee is never treated as a dinner or convening attendee for any purpose.** Separate registration, separate consent, separate list.

## 2. Calendar Dinner

Planned in advance, published on a calendar, 50 to 100 people, mixed. **Not tailored, and no composition promised.**

**Dinner Partner: $4,500 per dinner. Annual, four dinners: $14,400 ($3,600 each).**

Priced below the $7,500 tailored session deliberately. A buyer whose ideal customer profile only partly matches the room should still find this an easy yes.

| They get | Detail |
|---|---|
| A welcome | Two minutes. Not a keynote, not a deck |
| Two seats | As peers, not as a stand |
| Category exclusivity for that dinner | Up to three partners per dinner, one per category |
| Composition report | Who was there, by role, after the fact |
| Three consented introductions | |
| No attendee list | |

**Floor: 50% of the stated target. A target of 60 means a floor of 30. Below that, the next calendar dinner is free.**

Three partners at $4,500 is $13,500 a dinner, which comfortably clears the food cost on a room of 75.

## 3. Convening

The premium product. **Composition is the promise.** The buyer gives a brief, we compose to it.

Two venue options, same product:
- **Their headquarters.** Canadian companies with space
- **A Toronto venue we arrange.** US and international companies, who cannot host here. Travel to the US is not currently possible, so the room comes to Toronto and they fly in

| Step | Price |
|---|---|
| **The Brief** | **$4,500**, credited in full against the first convening |
| Single convening | **$25,000** |
| Four in a year | **$72,000** ($18,000 each, 28% off) |
| Six in a year | **$96,000** ($16,000 each, 36% off) |

**Pay per convening is always available.** Nobody is asked to commit to a year before seeing one. The annual discount is the reward for committing, not the price of entry.

### The Brief is the most important $4,500 in the business

We come back with exactly who we would put in the room and whether we can get them. **If we cannot fill it, we say so at brief stage, and they have spent $4,500 instead of $25,000.**

This is also our own protection. **We only sign a convening we have already shown we can fill**, which is what stops the guarantee below from ever being triggered.

### What a convening includes

Composition built to their brief, category exclusivity, a composition report, an insight brief, five consented introductions, and the four rules travelling with us: no presentation, no deck, no demo, no pitching. Guests are told who is hosting before they accept.

**They set the brief. We set the names. They see neither names nor the list before the room.**

## The guarantee ladder

Replaces the deck's "thirty conversations or the year is free, and you keep the dinners." That version refunded revenue while we had already spent the costs, which is a loss larger than the margin. **The principle here: we will return margin, we will not eat capital.**

### The floor is 50%

Free-attendance events run 40 to 60% no-show. We do not pretend otherwise. **We state a target and guarantee half of it.**

| Product | Target | Guaranteed floor |
|---|---|---|
| Convening | 50 invited to brief | **25 attending and matching the brief** |
| Calendar Dinner | Stated per dinner | **50% of it** |
| Monthly Meetup | 30 | **20** |

Counted in the composition report, against the brief, by role.

### What happens if we miss

**Step 1. Miss the floor once.** The next convening is free. We absorb the delivery cost. No cash leaves.

**Step 2. Miss the floor again.** Their choice, not ours:

| Option | What happens |
|---|---|
| **A. Full refund and exit** | Every fee paid under the contract is returned, including convenings already delivered. The relationship ends. If we cannot assemble half of what they asked for twice, either the brief has no market or we are the wrong convener. Keeping their money for that is indefensible |
| **B. Stay, switch to pay-per-attendee** | $700 per verified attendee matching the brief. No minimum, no commitment. They keep the room, they only pay for who shows |

Option B exists because some buyers are genuinely happy with 18 of the right people and would rather keep going than argue about a number.

### What caps our exposure

1. **The paid brief.** We do not sign what we cannot fill. This is the real protection and everything else is backup
2. **Refund is capped at fees paid.** Never consequential loss, never their travel, never their staff time
3. **Their brief arrives within 14 days of signing**, or the clock does not start
4. **Their own seat-holders attend.** A room they did not come to is not a room we failed to fill
5. **No pitching by them.** A breach voids the guarantee, and we will say so in writing when it happens
6. **The floor counts people matching the brief.** Not headcount

### The arithmetic, stated plainly

A $25,000 convening carries roughly $6,000 to $8,000 of hard cost. Step 1 costs us a second delivery, so about $14,000 of cost against $25,000 held. **Still positive.** Step 2A is a real loss of what has been spent, and it is accepted deliberately as the price of a promise worth making, bounded by the fact that the paid brief should stop us ever reaching it.

## What this resolves

`docs/15-partner-deck-audit.md` flagged an unresolved trade: the deck ran 15% commercial seats and the dinner programme ran 50%. **The three-product split resolves it.** Composition ratio is only a promise in the convening, where it is tight and briefed. The meetup and the calendar dinner make no ratio promise at all, so there is nothing to reconcile.
