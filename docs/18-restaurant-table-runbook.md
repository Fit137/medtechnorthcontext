## 1. The shape of the night

Tuesday, 6:30 to 9:30 pm, one restaurant, one profession, 24 to 48 people.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 6:30 | Arrival. Portrait station open and lit from the first minute |
| 7:00 | Seated. Two minutes of welcome, no deck |
| 7:15 | **The number.** One finding from the survey, revealed here first, three minutes |
| 7:20 | **The conversation.** One named guest, interviewed by Ali, 30 minutes, no slides |
| 7:50 | Dinner. Portrait and film station runs continuously alongside |
| 9:15 | Stated finish announced at the start. Honour it |

**No panel, no round-the-table, no icebreaker, no pitching from the floor.** A named guest in conversation is the draw and the only programmed content.

## 2. The draw, and why it is two things

An open paid event needs a named reason to attend. A composition room does not, because the room is the reason. This one needs both halves:

**The named guest.** One recognised figure in the vertical, interviewed for 30 minutes. No fee, dinner and courtesy only. The ask is easy because it is a conversation, not a talk, and nothing has to be prepared.

**The number.** One question, asked to the vertical by form and direct message over three weeks, 60 to 150 responses, revealed on the night. This is the highest-return item in the whole runbook and it costs a form and six hours of messaging.

It does four things at once: it gives cold outreach a reason to contact 300 strangers before the event, it makes the evening the place a real finding was first said, it produces an asset that outlives the night and can be posted for months, and it is the seed of the insight brief line in `ADR-011`.

**Ask the same question at the 26 August dinner first.** If it is dull with eight people it will be dull with three hundred.

### Why the paid tier is priced where it is

$650 is not a seat price, it is a film price with a dinner attached. A freelance videographer quotes $800 to $1,200 for a 60 second edited piece. The second, fifth and twelfth film on a night the crew is already hired costs close to nothing. **That gap is the margin, and it is also why the buyer says yes to a brand they have not heard of.**

## 4. Go or no-go

The media team is the cost committed earliest, so the gates are set around it.

> **Gate 1: $4,500 cleared by day −14.** Covers food and media for a 24 person room. Below it, postpone and refund in full within 48 hours.
>
> **Gate 2: $8,000 cleared by day −7.** Expand the guarantee from 24 to 45. Below it, run at 24 and stop selling.

Book the restaurant at a **guaranteed 24 with a written option to expand to 48 up to 10 days out**. On a Tuesday most GTA restaurants will agree to this. It means the event cannot lose money regardless of how sales go, which is the entire point of running an MVP.

## 5. Contracting the restaurant

Five terms, in order of what they are worth.

1. **Guaranteed minimum 24, option to 48 at day −10, in writing.** The most valuable line in the agreement
2. **Food and beverage minimum, not a room rental fee.** Spend counts toward it, so the risk sits with the restaurant
3. **Final count at 72 hours**
4. **A held per-head rate across a recurring booking.** Six Tuesdays is real negotiating power on a slow night and moves $105 toward $85
5. **Written answer on whether they require a certificate of insurance.** If yes, insurance is a hard blocker, not a background task

Ask for long tables or a private room with a wall the media team can light. Never scattered rounds. Confirm there is a corner that can hold a lit portrait station without blocking service.

## 7. Media operation

One photographer and one videographer, four hours. One lit corner, one backdrop that is not a step-and-repeat, one wireless mic for the conversation.

**Everyone leaves with three edited portraits, paid or not.** This is what makes the camera welcome rather than extractive, and it is why nobody at the table is being photographed for someone else's benefit.

**Paid attendees additionally get a 60 second film**, shot at the same station, same question for everyone, no preparation. Same discipline as `ADR-023`: a single identical question produces a consistent series and removes preparation anxiety.

### Consent, on paper, before the first frame

One paragraph, signed at check-in, collected by whoever is on the door. Covers the attendee's own image for MedTech North's use, names the edition and date, and is refusable without any effect on attendance. **A red dot on the name card marks anyone who declined, and the media team is briefed to frame them out.**

Per `ADR-025` section 6: depicting is allowed, tallying is not. No headcounts, no totals, no counters, no image used to imply endorsement.
