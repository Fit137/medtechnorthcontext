# Host Incentives and the Media Floor

> **INTERNAL. Never published, never quoted, never shown to a guest.** Named list: `data/venue-host-targets.md`. Campaigns: `docs/20-outreach-campaign-sequence.md`. Decision: `ADR-025`.

Why an organisation gives up its building for a day, what it is actually being paid in, and why media is the right floor rather than a consolation prize.

---

## 1. Three asks that keep getting treated as one

The single largest speed gain available is refusing to bundle these. They have different owners, different approval paths and different clocks, and bundling them means the slowest one sets the date.

| | **Venue host** | **Room funder** | **Co-host** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What they give | The space, its AV, its staff for the day | Money for one composed room | Money and their premises |
| What they receive | Co-branding, presence, media shot in their building, their category held for the day | Composition to a brief, category exclusivity, composition report, introductions | Both, plus the co-host position |
| Cash from them | **None** | $7,500 to $25,000 | $25,000 and up |
| Cash to them | **None** | | |
| Who signs | Facilities, community, marketing, or a director with a mandate to convene | Field marketing, industry marketing, partnerships | Same, plus procurement above the committee line |
| Approval path | Often **no purchase, therefore no procurement** | Discretionary or one approval | Committee, vendor onboarding, sometimes legal |
| Realistic time to yes | **2 to 4 weeks** | 4 to 8 weeks | 8 to 16 weeks |
| Tests | H4, H5 | H1, H2 | H1, H2, H9 |

**The venue host ask closes fastest because it is not a purchase.** Nothing has to be bought, so nothing has to clear a threshold. That single property is why an October or November date is reachable at all, and why the first campaign asks for a building and not for money.

The trap to avoid: a host who starts asking what it costs has moved themselves into column two, which is slower. Answer honestly and keep the two asks separate in the same conversation. The space is not contingent on the cheque and the cheque is not contingent on the space, per `ADR-021`.

## 2. The ten currencies, ranked by how fast they convert to a yes

Money is one of ten things an organisation can be paid in, and it is not the fastest.

| Rank | Currency | Who holds it | Why it moves fast |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Documented activity against a reporting deadline** | Regional innovation centres, economic development offices, industry associations, university and college partnership offices, hospital innovation units | Their funding is renewed against activity, not against revenue. Events hosted and organisations engaged are the units they report in. A day of rooms is a line in an annual report that writes itself |
| **2** | **Utilisation of space that costs the same whether it is used** | Innovation hubs, briefing centres, experience centres, incubators, corporate auditoriums | The facility is already a line item under review. Filling it is a defensible internal win at zero marginal cost, and it is approved by whoever owns the calendar rather than a budget |
| **3** | **A budget with a clock on it** | Calendar-year corporates in Q4, subsidiaries approaching a fiscal year end | October and November is the single best-timed window in the year for unspent field marketing money. See the calendar in section 6 |
| **4** | **A new leader or new mandate needing visible proof** | Anyone who hired a Canadian healthcare lead, opened a Canadian office, or launched a Canadian health vertical in the last twelve months | Already recorded in `data/cohost-targets.md` as the highest single trigger. A new leader has a number, no local community, and a short window to show motion |
| **5** | **Media they cannot commission** | Everyone. This is the floor. See section 3 | It is a deliverable rather than an outcome, so it can be promised in writing without breaching `docs/08-legal-and-compliance.md` |
| **6** | **Access under conditions their own logo cannot create** | Any commercial organisation selling into health | The neutrality argument, which is H2 in `docs/18-hypothesis-risk-and-falsification.md`. They can rent a room and cater it. They cannot make the room read as a conversation instead of a sales meeting |
| **7** | **Recruitment and talent visibility** | Universities, colleges, employers with open clinical or technical roles | A room of practising clinicians and operators is a hiring surface, and it is almost never priced |
| **8** | **National interest, stated carefully** | Canadian-owned organisations and public bodies, far more than subsidiaries | Real and currently dated. Handled in section 5, including where it backfires |
| **9** | **Category held for the day** | Any commercial host | `ADR-020`. The only thing in the room exactly one organisation can have, and it costs us nothing to give |
| **10** | **Their own executives sitting with clinicians** | Country managers, Canadian leadership | Internally valuable in a way that is hard to buy. A Canadian team that has never met a practising customer face to face gets an afternoon of it |

**Read the ranking honestly.** The top two are held almost entirely by institutions and public bodies, which is why the fast venue yes lives there and the money lives further down the list with corporates. Those are two different campaigns to two different people, and running them as one is what makes a venue search feel slow.

## 3. The media floor, and why it holds

The floor is this: **from hosting alone, before a single lead is counted, the host receives co-branded material shot in their building that they could not have produced without us.**

Three reasons it is a floor rather than a consolation.

**1. It is the only deliverable entirely inside our control on the night.** Attendance is not. Composition is only partly. A commercial outcome is not at all, and promising one is forbidden. `ADR-024` already established the principle for guests: sell the format, which we control, never the room, which we do not. The same discipline applied to a host produces media as the consideration, and it is the honest version rather than the modest one.

**2. It is legally clean.** Media is a produced object. It can be specified, delivered, and written into a one-page agreement with a make-good. A lead number cannot be promised at all under `docs/08-legal-and-compliance.md`, so a deal that requires one is a deal we are not allowed to close.

**3. The scarce input is the people, not the production.** This is the argument that makes the floor defensible rather than thin, and it should be said plainly in the room:

> A crew is a purchase. Twelve practising clinicians willing to sit on camera and talk about their work is not. Any organisation can book the first. The second is the thing we are actually supplying, and the camera is the cheap half of it.

A marketing team at a health software company can commission a film unit tomorrow. It cannot commission the participants, because the participants will not do it for a vendor, and every one of them has been asked. That asymmetry is the product. The footage is the container it ships in.

### What the media package actually contains

Specified so it can be promised, and so nobody improvises a bigger promise under pressure.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| On-record interviews | Shot in the host's space, lit, lav mic, an interviewer who has read about the subject. Per Format D in `docs/17-event-formats.md` |
| Co-branded cuts | Long form plus vertical cuts, carrying both marks, cleared for the host's own channels |
| Stills | Of the space in use, with people who have consented to be filmed |
| A written summary | What the day covered, publishable by both |
| Category held | For the day, per `ADR-020` |

### The dependency that is currently unmet

**There is no sample reel.** `HANDOFF.md` records that production quotes have not been obtained and no footage has been shot. A facilities director or a marketing lead will ask what the output looks like, and "trust me" is a weak answer against a free building.

**Room 003 on 26 August is the asset shoot for this entire campaign, not only an event.** Shoot it properly, cut a two minute reel and three verticals by the first week of September, and the host campaign gains its single most persuasive attachment before it goes to volume. Below a quality bar the gift becomes an embarrassment, which `docs/17-event-formats.md` already flags as the way Format D fails.

## 4. One object, several internal justifications

The same media package is approved for different reasons depending on who holds the building. Write the message to the reason, not to the object.

| Host archetype | What the footage is to them internally |
|---|---|
| University or college | Recruitment content, industry-engagement evidence, and material for a partnership report |
| Regional innovation centre or economic development office | Proof of ecosystem activity for a funder, and investment-attraction material |
| Hospital innovation unit or research institute | Public-facing evidence that clinicians are consulted early, which is what the unit exists to demonstrate |
| Professional services firm | Thought-leadership positioning, which is what they buy instead of leads |
| Corporate with a briefing centre | Field marketing content plus utilisation of a facility that has a target |
| Bank or insurer with a healthcare practice | Acquisition-adjacent brand material aimed at exactly the practice owners they want as customers |
| Industry association | Member value, demonstrated, in a year when members ask what they get |

## 5. National interest, and where it stops working

The argument is genuine and it is currently dated, which is rare. The Buy Canadian policy took effect 16 December 2025 with health and pharmaceuticals named a strategic sector. The threshold for Canadian preference fell from $25M to $5M on 15 June 2026. The Buy Ontario Act received royal assent on 11 December 2025. Canada is the eighth largest medical device market in the world and a new device still takes more than a year, best case, to reach a Canadian patient.

**How to use it.** As the permission argument, never the purchase argument. It gives the individual signing a reason they are comfortable saying yes, and it turns the request into participation rather than a vendor asking for something. It belongs in the third paragraph, after the specific ask and the specific consideration. A message that opens on patriotism reads as a charity appeal and gets the reply a charity appeal gets.

**Where it works hardest.** Canadian-owned organisations, public bodies, universities, colleges, associations, and Canadian executives inside foreign subsidiaries who have to justify local spend to a head office. For that last group it is unusually useful, because it hands them the internal argument they were going to have to make anyway.

**Where it backfires.** Aimed at a US parent it reads as pressure or as a hint that they are outsiders, which is the opposite of the position we want with the inbound line in `docs/12-convening-as-a-service.md`. For US targets the same energy goes into the market-entry frame instead: they are not being asked to support Canada, they are being shown the shortest route into it.

**The counterargument, conceded cheerfully.** A June 2026 Policy Options piece argued Buy Canadian will not deliver much, granting only a modest advantage on a narrow band of large procurements. Concede it immediately when raised. It strengthens the case rather than weakening it: policy signals intent, and intent does not create a pathway. Building the pathway is the thing being convened.

## 6. Timing, which is an incentive in its own right

| Fiscal year end | Who | What it means for an October or November ask |
|---|---|---|
| 31 December | Most Canadian corporates and Canadian subsidiaries of US firms | **The best window in the year.** Unspent field marketing budget, a hard December deadline, and Q4 event plans that are frequently under-filled |
| 31 March | Federal and provincial bodies, most public agencies, many associations | Mid-year. Q3 activity is what they report in Q4, so an autumn event lands inside the reporting period they are already worried about |
| 30 April | Most Canadian universities | Autumn is term time and the building is staffed. Spring is worse on both counts |
| 30 June, 31 July, 30 September | Various US parents | Check per company. A year end within four months is already a scoring signal in `data/cohost-targets.md` |

**The line this justifies, and it is true rather than manufactured:** the room is being built now because the calendar makes it cheap for them to say yes now. That is a legitimate reason and it is not urgency theatre, which `docs/07-brand-and-voice.md` forbids.

## 7. When they ask how many leads

They will. The answer is prepared, short, and does not apologise.

> We do not promise leads and we would not believe anyone who did. What is in the agreement is who is in the room against a written brief, the material shot on the day, and introductions afterwards to the people who agree to them individually. If the room is right, the rest follows from being in it. If it is not right, we would rather refund than argue about attribution.

Three things that sentence does. It refuses an unpromisable outcome, per `docs/08-legal-and-compliance.md`. It substitutes three promises that are all deliverable and all measurable. And it introduces the guarantee ladder in `ADR-022` as a strength rather than as a defence under pressure.

**If the lead question comes first, before anything else, twice or more across a segment**, that is the falsifier for H5 and it goes in the campaign log rather than into a workaround.

## 8. What a host never receives

Unchanged from `ADR-016`, `ADR-020` and `ADR-021`, restated here because a free building creates pressure to give something back.

- The attendee list, contact data, an export, or badge scanning, in any form, at any price
- Guest list visibility before the room
- Approval over individuals. They may nominate. We compose
- Stage time beyond a welcome, a deck, a demo, or pitching from the floor
- Editorial control over the insight brief or the written summary
- Any claim that clinicians, institutions or regulators endorse them
- Any promised commercial outcome

**The clause that matters most for a donated venue.** A host offering the building will often expect to fill some of it with their own invitees. One or two nominated seats is co-hosting. Eight is curation, and it converts the room into their event held in their building with our name on it. Cap it in writing in the first message, not in the negotiation, and treat resistance on that point as a qualification signal rather than an obstacle.
