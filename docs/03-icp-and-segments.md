# ICPs and Segments

Four groups. Each is named in full because a pharmacist, a dental hygiene student or a lab technologist must be able to find themselves by name rather than guess whether "clinicians" includes them.

**Critical framing note:** never rank these groups against each other in public. The internal logic about who creates versus captures value stays internal. Every segment reads the same site.

---

## 1. Clinicians, researchers and academics

**Access:** by nomination or invitation, reviewed individually. Ticketed, unless a co-host has funded the room, in which case the seat is free to the guest (`ADR-026`).

**Vocabulary for this audience:** point of care, workflow, care pathway, evidence generation, scope of practice, translational, multidisciplinary.

**Sub-segments, named in full:**

*Practising clinicians:* dentists · clinical and community pharmacists · physiotherapists · occupational therapists · respiratory therapists · radiation therapists · speech-language pathologists · dietitians · nurses and nurse practitioners · physicians and surgeons across every specialty · medical laboratory technologists · diagnostic imaging professionals · dental hygienists · denturists

*Research and academic:* clinical trialists · translational researchers · biomedical scientists · academic scientists · health services researchers · epidemiologists · health economists

*Leadership:* hospital chiefs · clinical program directors · medical affairs · quality and safety · practice owners

> **Composition reality, as of July 2026.** The room that actually assembles is dentists, clinical pharmacists, physiotherapists and researchers. Zero physicians have attended. Do not write clinician-facing material as physician-first. See `ADR-012`.

**Why they come:** to see technology at the stage where clinical input still changes it, and to meet peers from institutions and disciplines they rarely share a table with.

---

## 2. Builders — founders and operators

**Access:** by application. Paid membership.

**Vocabulary:** runway, traction, pilot, clinical champion, procurement cycle, reimbursement pathway, regulatory strategy, commercialisation.

**Sectors:** medical devices and diagnostics · digital health and health IT · biotech and therapeutics · pharmaceuticals · clinical AI and decision support · remote monitoring and virtual care · laboratory tools and life science instrumentation · dental technology · mental and behavioural health · femtech, agetech and paediatric health

**Models:** B2B · B2B2C · provider-facing · payer-facing · direct to consumer · hybrids

**Stages:** pre-seed and seed · Series A and B · growth and scale-up · established companies opening a Canadian operation · international companies entering the market

**Roles:** founders and co-founders · CEOs, COOs and commercial leads · heads of clinical, regulatory and market access · business development

**Why they come:** a clinical champion, someone who knows how their organisation actually buys, and an investor who has seen the pathway before.

---

## 3. Policy and government

**Access:** by invitation. Ticketed, unless a co-host has funded the room (`ADR-026`).

**Vocabulary:** capacity, procurement pathways, health technology assessment, value-based procurement, interoperability, jurisdictional, evidence-informed, domestic capacity.

**Sub-segments:**

*Federal:* Health Canada · Public Health Agency · ISED · CIHR · federal procurement
*Provincial:* ministries of health · Ontario Health and regional structures · provincial agencies and Crown bodies
*Regional and municipal:* public health units · Ontario Health Teams · economic development offices
*Health system:* hospital procurement and supply chain organisations · shared services · clinical program leadership
*Assessment and regulation:* health technology assessment · regulatory affairs · privacy and data governance

**Why they come:** to hear procurement and regulatory friction directly from the people living it, months before it appears in a report.

> **Note.** Provincial policy is more relevant to early-stage founders than federal. Queen's Park, Ontario Health and regional procurement are local to the GTA and should be recruited first. Federal engagement carries conflict-of-interest and hospitality rules that need legal review before invitations go out.

---

## 4. Students and graduates

**Access:** ticketed, unless a co-host has funded the room (`ADR-026`). Seats limited per room to protect balance.

**Vocabulary:** training, supervisor, cohort, translational, career pathway. Warmer than other pages, never junior in tone.

**Sub-segments:** dentistry, pharmacy, nursing and medical students · allied health trainees across therapy, diagnostics and laboratory science · graduate students and postdoctoral researchers in biomedical science, health sciences, biomedical and software engineering, public health and health policy · MBA, MHA and health management students · recent graduates and early career professionals

**Why they come:** to watch how decisions actually get made, fifteen years before habit would allow it.

---

## Service providers — the fifth group

Agencies, consultants, law firms, recruiters and vendors selling into the sector.

**They are a paid segment, not a guest segment.** They may not attend free.

**Cap them hard.** Six in a room of forty is fine. Eight in a room of sixteen is a vendor floor with a few clinicians standing in it, and the clinical side will notice. At the first event a significant number were turned away, which was correct.

---

## Composition rules

- Target **1:1 builders to validators** in every room.
- Target roughly **1.5 guests per paying member** across the network.
- If the guest-to-payer ratio climbs past 2:1, pause guest recruitment and push builder acquisition.
- Every seat at every tier is subject to composition. No membership tier overrides the balance of the room.
