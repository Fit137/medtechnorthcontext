## How to record these

**Setup, once.** Phone on a tripod at eye height, camera at arm's length or slightly further. Window light in front of you, never behind. 4K if the phone offers it, 30fps. Film 9:16 natively rather than cropping later.

**Vary the location.** The source material makes the point that going outside measurably lifts engagement, because there is more visual variability and the brain registers it. Rotate: the venue lounge, a car, a coffee shop, walking outside, the kitchen table. Do not film all fourteen against the same wall on the same afternoon, which is the obvious way to run a batch day and the wrong one. Two locations per batch, minimum.

**The first two seconds carry 80% of the outcome.** Three rules for them, all from the source material.

1. **Start mid-thought.** No greeting, no name, no "so I wanted to talk about". The first word of the script is the first word out of your mouth.
2. **Your face is in frame at frame one.** Not a title card, not a logo, not a slow push in. Faces earn the stop.
3. **Say it the way you would say it to one person.** Simple enough for someone with no context. If a sentence needs the listener to know what a convening is, rewrite it.

**Do two takes of every script and keep both.** The second is almost always better and takes ninety seconds.

**Do not memorise.** Read the hook until it is exact, then talk the rest. A slightly rougher delivery outperforms a polished recitation on this platform, and the source material is explicit that reducing the distance between idea and execution is what authenticity actually means.

**Captions are burned in, always.** Most of these are watched muted. Specs are in the design file under `T-G`.

**Length target: 25 to 40 seconds.** Every script below is written to land inside that at a normal speaking pace. If you overrun, cut the third sentence, not the last one.

## SHARE, as a checklist

Run this on anything you write yourself. From video 2.

| Letter | Test |
|---|---|
| **S**imple | Would someone with no health sector background follow it |
| **H**ook | Is the first line a complete, interesting statement on its own |
| **A**uthentic | Is it a thing that actually happened, this month, to you |
| **R**elatable | Is it specific. Vague is not relatable, specific is |
| **E**ntertainment | Would someone watch it who has no intention of ever attending |

The last one is the one to be honest about. Meta's own position, as relayed in video 2, is that entertainment has moved from a consideration to the primary driving factor. For this brand entertainment is not comedy. It is disclosure: showing the mechanics of something people only ever see the outside of, and admitting the parts that went badly.

## S-01. I took the agenda off my dinners
**Post:** `IG-R-01`, Monday 17 August

**Hook, verbatim:**
> I took the agenda off my dinners.

**Body:**
> The first two had a format. People mapped things out, worked through exercises, answered questions going round the table. It went fine, and I've cut all of it.
>
> Everyone I invite has already had a long week. The last thing they need on a Wednesday is a third obligation that asks something of them the second they walk in.
>
> So now there's nothing scheduled. You turn up, you talk to people, you leave when you want to.

**Close:**
> Third one's the 26th, in Mississauga.

**On-screen text:** `I cut the agenda` at 0:00. `Nothing to prepare` at 0:18.
**Shot:** Sitting, indoors, natural light. Calm delivery. This is the account's first post and it sets the register.
**SHARE:** the refusal hook, and the specificity of "a third obligation on a Wednesday".

## S-02. Nobody buys a microphone
**Post:** `IG-R-04`, Friday 21 August

**Hook:**
> Nobody gets stage time at my dinners. Not founders, not anyone paying me, not me.

**Body:**
> Every event in this sector eventually sells a speaking slot. Someone pays, they get fifteen minutes, and everyone in the room knows why they're on their feet.
>
> The moment you sell that, the person in seat four stops being a guest and starts being an audience. And they can feel it, usually before the second slide.
>
> So it's a flat rule. Nobody buys a microphone. There isn't one in the building.

**Close:**
> Costs me money. It's the reason people come back.

**On-screen text:** `Nobody buys a microphone` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Standing, outdoors if possible. Slightly more energy than S-01.
**SHARE:** counterintuitive for a business with no revenue, which is what makes it worth watching.

## S-03. The hardest part is the pitching
**Post:** `IG-R-07`, Tuesday 25 August

**Hook:**
> The hardest part of running these dinners isn't the food or the venue. It's the pitching.

**Body:**
> Not the obvious kind. Nobody stands up and opens a deck. It's the quiet version, where somebody has worked out which side of the table is worth their time, and spends the whole night on it.
>
> By nine o'clock a dentist who came out for a nice evening has been sold to three times, and she isn't coming back.
>
> So the rule is flat. Nobody pitches. If it starts, I stop it, in the room, while it's happening.

**Close:**
> If you meet someone worth talking to, tell me after and I'll ask them for you.

**On-screen text:** `No pitching` at 0:00. `I stop it in the room` at 0:22.
**Shot:** Sitting. This is the most serious of the fourteen and it should be delivered flatly.
**SHARE:** the dentist at nine o'clock is the specificity. Do not generalise her to "a guest".

## S-04. Clinicians never pay
**Post:** week 3 slot

**Hook:**
> Clinicians don't pay to come to my dinners. Not now, and not later when there's a business here.

**Body:**
> People assume free is a launch promotion. It isn't. It's permanent, and it's written down.
>
> A dentist, a pharmacist, a physio, a researcher. Every one of them attends at no cost, forever.
>
> The reason is unromantic. The second you charge the people everyone else came to meet, they stop coming, and then there's nothing to sell to anyone else either.

**Close:**
> So it's free. Permanently. That's the whole policy.

**On-screen text:** `Clinicians attend free. Permanently.` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Anywhere. Delivery should be matter of fact, not generous.
**SHARE:** simple, and it is the single clearest thing this business believes.

## S-05. I stopped promising the seating
**Post:** week 3 slot

**Hook:**
> I used to tell people I spent hours on the seating plan. I've taken that off everything.

**Body:**
> It was true. I did spend hours on it. It was also the best line on the page, and I pulled it.
>
> Here's why. Free dinners run a high no-show rate. Everyone's in a clinic all day and things come up. So the room that arrives isn't the room that registered, and the seat I chose for you three days ago is next to an empty chair.
>
> The most credible promise on the page had become the least reliable thing on it.

**Close:**
> So now I promise what I control. No agenda, nothing to prepare, and you're home by nine.

**On-screen text:** `I pulled my best line` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Sitting, quiet.
**SHARE:** admitting you removed your own strongest claim is the most trust-generating thing on this list.

## S-07. I don't tell you how many people came
**Post:** week 3 slot

**Hook:**
> I'll never tell you how many people were at one of my dinners.

**Body:**
> Every event in the world leads with a number. Two hundred attendees. Forty founders. Sold out.
>
> The number is the least useful fact available. A room of two hundred where nobody needed to meet anybody is worse than a table of nine where two people did.
>
> So I report composition. Who was in the room, by role. A dentist, a community pharmacist, two researchers, someone building software for clinics.

**Close:**
> Who was there, not how many. It's a smaller claim and it's the true one.

**On-screen text:** `Composition, not headcount` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Anywhere.
**SHARE:** takes a position against a universal industry habit, which is the shareable shape.

## S-08. A dentist and a pharmacist
**Post:** week 4 slot

**Hook:**
> A dentist and a pharmacist will describe the exact same patient completely differently, and both of them are right.

**Body:**
> That's the entire reason I keep these rooms mixed.
>
> A dentist sees a mouth, a history and a person who has been putting something off for two years. A pharmacist sees the same person as a list of eleven medications and someone who hasn't picked up two of them.
>
> Neither one is wrong. Neither one has the other half.

**Close:**
> You only find that out if they're close enough to argue about it over dinner.

**On-screen text:** `Same patient. Two people.` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Sitting. This one benefits from being a little animated.
**SHARE:** the most genuinely interesting thing on the list, and it is about the guests, not about you.

## S-09. More than a year to reach a patient
**Post:** week 4 slot

## S-10. Why I ask everyone the same question
**Post:** week 4 slot. Launches the series

**Hook:**
> I ask every single person at these dinners the exact same question.

**Body:**
> What do patients get wrong about your field.
>
> Nobody sees it in advance and nobody prepares. Ninety seconds, off to one side, while everyone else is eating.
>
> They keep the footage. All of it, theirs outright, to put on their site or their ads or nowhere at all.
>
> Twenty people answering one question is a series. One person answering twenty questions is just an interview.

**Close:**
> The answers go up here, one at a time.

**On-screen text:** `The same question. Every time.` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Anywhere.
**SHARE:** sets up the series so the Question posts arrive with context rather than cold.

## S-11. Nothing leaves the table
**Post:** week 4 or 5 slot

**Hook:**
> Nothing said at one of my dinners gets repeated anywhere. Including by me.

**Body:**
> No recording of the room, no notes going out, no write-up with quotes in it, nothing named on a website.
>
> It sounds like a small rule. It's the load-bearing one, because it's the only reason a hospital procurement lead will tell you what actually happens inside a purchase, in front of a founder trying to sell into it.
>
> Take that away and everyone talks in press releases for three hours.

**Close:**
> Boring rule. Kept absolutely.

**On-screen text:** `Nothing leaves the table` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Sitting, low key.
**SHARE:** simple, and it answers the unspoken question a senior person has before accepting.

## S-12. Somebody asked me for the guest list
**Post:** week 5 slot

**Hook:**
> Somebody offered to pay me for the guest list last month. The answer was no, and it's always no.

**Body:**
> I understand the ask. They want to know who's in the room, and they want to follow up afterwards. That's a normal thing to want.
>
> But everyone at that table came on the understanding that nobody is anybody's prospect. Sell the list once and that's finished, permanently, and every clinician in the GTA hears about it inside a month.
>
> What I will do is make introductions. I ask the person first. If they say no, that's the end of it and it doesn't come back as a maybe.

**Close:**
> Consent first, every time. It converts better anyway.

**On-screen text:** `The answer is always no` at 0:00.
**Shot:** Anywhere.
**SHARE:** refusing money is the most entertaining thing a business with no revenue can credibly do.

> **Check before filming.** Only run this if it happened. If it did not, cut it. A composite anecdote is the one thing on this page that would genuinely damage the brand.

# Format C: the artifact. Voiceover over your own hands

# Held for your approval

## S-19. Three doctors said yes
**Not scheduled.** See flag 4 in the calendar. This runs only if you say so.

**Hook:**
> Three doctors accepted for my second dinner. On the night, none of them came.

**Body:**
> Not one cancelled. They just didn't arrive.
>
> And I've thought about that a lot, because it isn't a recruitment problem. They said yes. Something between yes and Wednesday was stronger than the invitation, and those two things have completely different fixes.
>
> A clinic runs late. A kid gets sick. An evening you're not paying for is the easiest thing in the world to drop.

**Close:**
> So I stopped trying to get more yeses and started working on why the yes doesn't hold.

**On-screen text:** `Three said yes. None came.` at 0:00.

**Why it is the strongest thing you own:** it is specific, it is a failure, it is counterintuitive, and it is about a problem every person who has ever organised anything recognises. That is four of the five SHARE letters in one story.

**Why it needs your sign-off:** it publicly documents that clinicians do not turn up, to an audience of clinicians you are trying to get to turn up. It breaks no rule in `CLAUDE.md`. It is a judgement call about whether candour or recruitment wins, and that is yours.
