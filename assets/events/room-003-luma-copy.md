## Registration questions

Keep the two already in place. Add one.

```
What company do you work for?
What is your job title?
What is the one question you would want to put to the rest of the table?
```

**The third question is the instrument.** It is the cheapest screen available. Anyone who will not spend sixty seconds on it will not spend an evening in traffic, and the answers set the seating plan and the opening question before anyone arrives.

## Approval message

Sent by Luma the moment a seat is confirmed.

```
Your seat is held.

Closer to the date I will send you the composition by role, and who you
are seated beside and why.

If anything changes, tell me early and I will give the seat to someone
on the waitlist.
```

## Declining a request

For service providers, agencies and anyone selling into the room.

```
Thank you for the request. This table is composed around clinicians,
researchers and founders, and I keep the commercial side of the room
deliberately small so that nobody at the table is being sold to.

I run larger rooms where that balance works differently. May I put you
on the list for the next one.
```

No price appears here. No tier language. Per `CLAUDE.md` rule 2, the commercial conversation happens on a call and never in anything a member reads.

## The one risk in this copy, stated plainly

`data/verified-stats.md` forbids publishing a fixed seat count, because "40 seats" against a room that held 16 overclaims.

**"The table seats eight" is a different kind of statement.** It describes a physical table, not an expected turnout. If six people arrive, the table still seated eight and nothing was claimed about attendance. That framing also satisfies the brand rule that scarcity is a property of the format rather than a pressure device.

**What must never be written:** "eight executives", "eight CEOs", or any promise about who fills the remaining four seats. That is a composition promise, and a composition promise that misses is the one outcome that damages the relationship with the guest who matters most.
