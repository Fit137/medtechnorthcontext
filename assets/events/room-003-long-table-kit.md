> **Public copy is final and ready to publish.** Private copy is DM-only and must never appear on Luma, LinkedIn, or in any image. Format decision: `ADR-023`. Format detail: `docs/17-event-formats.md`.

> **Date maths:** twenty days from 7 August 2026 lands on **Thursday 27 August 2026**. Swap it if you want the Wednesday pattern of room 001.

## 1. How the fallback actually works

The requirement was a page that survives the room changing shape in the last five days. It does, and the reason is a single design choice:

**The public copy describes the evening. It never describes the audience.**

Nothing on the page requires a clinic owner to be present. Dinner happens, a portrait station runs, everyone answers one question. That is true whether the room is twelve practice owners, six owners and six founders, or mostly researchers and builders.

| Day | What you do | What changes on Luma or LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 10 | Hard outreach to clinic operators, message A | Nothing |
| 11 to 15 | Keep working operators, open message B to non-owner clinicians | Nothing |
| 16 to 20 | If the owner count is short, open messages C and D to founders and researchers | **Nothing** |

The switch is private. Same title, same description, same images, same posts. Only the DM changes, and the seating plan on the night.

**Why the portrait works for every group.** A clinic owner wants a headshot. So does a founder, a researcher, a pharmacist who does not own the pharmacy, and a physiotherapist five years out. Nobody in any of these groups has a current photo of themselves they like. It is the rare offer that does not need to be re-argued per segment.

**The one thing that must stay true regardless of mix:** the room is not majority service providers. Cap them the same as always (`docs/03-icp-and-segments.md`). A room of six vendors and four clinicians fails whichever way the copy reads.

## 2. The one question

**"What's the one thing people outside your work always get wrong about it?"**

This is the load-bearing choice in the whole kit. It passes on four counts:

- **Anyone in health can answer it cold.** A dentist, a founder, a lab scientist, a pharmacy owner. No preparation, no expertise gap
- **It makes the speaker look knowledgeable**, so they will post the clip. That is the postability test from `docs/17-event-formats.md`
- **It is warm.** It sounds like something a person would ask you at dinner, not something a research panel would ask
- **It survives the ICP switch**, which no operator-specific question does

Series name if you want one: **One Thing**.

## 4. Event description (Luma). Final copy, publish as written

```
Once a month I put a small group of people who work in health around one table in Mississauga.

Dentists, pharmacists, physiotherapists, researchers, people building health software. Some run clinics, some run companies, a few do both. No programme, no slides, nobody presents and nobody pitches. Just dinner with people who get what you do without you having to explain it first.

Two things happen that don't happen at a normal dinner.

There's a photographer set up in the corner with proper lighting. Whenever you feel like it, you wander over and sit for five minutes. You get a real portrait, not a phone photo, and the files land in your inbox a few days later. They're yours. Put them on your website, your profile, wherever you want.

And everyone gets asked the same question on camera. What's the one thing people outside your work always get wrong about it? Ninety seconds, two takes, nothing to prepare. You get that clip as well, captioned, ready to post if you feel like it.

That's the whole evening. Eat, talk, sit for a photo, answer a question, home by nine thirty.

No cost, and dinner's covered. If you'd rather not be filmed, tell me at the door and no camera goes near you. You'll still get fed.

Thursday 27 August, 6:30 to 9:30 pm, Mississauga. Register and I'll send you the address.

Ali
```

**Why it reads human, in case you want to edit it later:** it opens with a fact rather than a hook, it uses contractions, sentence lengths run from three words to thirty, there is no closing summary, and it never once says what the evening will mean to you. The last line before the logistics is a joke about still getting fed. Keep something like it.

**Do not add:** a headcount, a past-event photo, a testimonial, the word sponsor, or a line about limited spots.

## 5. Private outreach ladder

Four messages, one event. Send order matters more than wording.

### Message A. Clinic and practice owners. Days 1 to 15

```
Hi [name], I run a small dinner in Mississauga once a month for people who work in health. Twelve or so around one table, no talks, nobody selling anything.

The reason I'm writing to you specifically: I bring in a photographer and a proper lighting setup, and every practice owner in the room gets a professional portrait and a short filmed piece about their own work. Five minutes for the photo, ninety seconds for the film. You get all of it a few days later and it's yours outright. Website, ads, profile, whatever you want. No cost and no catch.

The rest of the table is people like you, running clinics across the GTA, plus a few researchers and people building software for practices. Nobody pitches you. That's a rule, not a hope.

Thursday 27 August, 6:30 to 9:30, dinner's covered.

If it's a yes I'll hold you a photo slot. If the evening doesn't work, I do these at people's own clinics too, and honestly the footage is better there.
```

That last line matters. It converts a decline into the Tier 2 on-site feature from `docs/17-event-formats.md` instead of losing the person.

### Message B. Clinicians who don't own the practice. Days 11 to 20

```
Hi [name], I run a small dinner in Mississauga once a month for people who work in health. Around twelve people, one table, no talks and no pitching.

There's a photographer there with proper lighting, so you get a real headshot out of it, files sent to you a few days later, yours to keep. And everyone gets asked the same question on camera: what's the one thing people outside your work always get wrong about it. Ninety seconds, nothing to prepare.

Mostly I think you'd enjoy the table. Dentists, pharmacists, physios, researchers, a few people building health software. It's the rare room where you don't have to explain your job before you can talk about it.

Thursday 27 August, 6:30 to 9:30. No cost, dinner's on me, and it stays free for clinicians permanently.

### Message C. Founders and operators. Days 16 to 20

## 6. Image generation prompts

**Two hard rules before you generate anything:**

1. **No people, no crowds, no dinner-party scenes.** An image of a full table implies attendance at a past event, which `CLAUDE.md` rule 3 forbids and which you cannot evidence. Objects, rooms and typography only
2. **Nothing that reads as stock photography.** The craft bans in `docs/07-brand-and-voice.md` are absolute. Push every prompt toward drawn, illustrated or graphic, never toward a photograph of a generic professional

### Direction 1: The Empty Table

*Warm, hand-drawn, non-corporate. This is the one that matches the friendly register best.*

```
A single long dining table drawn in confident hand-inked line work, seen from a slight three-quarter angle. Set simply: plates, water glasses, a jug, a couple of small dishes, a folded napkin. At the far end of the table, a camera on a tripod and a single softbox light on a stand, drawn in the same line style, pointed at an empty stool. No people anywhere in the frame. Warm cream paper background with visible tooth. Ink lines in near-black. One accent colour only, a deep crimson, used on two or three small objects. Flat, no gradients, no blur, no lens effects. The feeling of a good illustrated cookbook or a letterpress dinner invitation, not a corporate event graphic. Generous empty space in the upper third for typography.
```

### Direction 2: The Portrait Corner

*Quieter, more intriguing. Best for the story format and the carousel cover.*

```
A quiet corner of a room set up for portraits: one wooden stool, a single softbox light on a stand tilted toward it, a camera on a tripod, a coiled cable on the floor. Plain wall behind. Nobody in the frame, the stool is empty and waiting. Rendered as a limited-palette screen print: three flat colours only, cream, deep ink, one crimson accent, with visible halftone texture and slight registration offset. Strong directional light shape falling on the wall. No photographic realism, no gradients, no blur, no lens flare. Calm and warm rather than clinical. Room at the top of the frame for a headline.
```

### Direction 3: Typographic Only

*Cheapest, most on-brand, and the one that will look best in a LinkedIn feed at thumbnail size. No image model needed if you use the Claude Design prompt in section 7 instead.*

```
A typographic poster. The sentence "What's the one thing people outside your work always get wrong about it?" set large in a confident modern serif, filling most of the frame, broken across five or six lines, ragged right. Deep ink text on a warm cream field. One short line beneath in small letter-spaced capitals. A single thin crimson rule under one word. Nothing else in the frame, no illustration, no decoration, no icons. Letterpress feel, slight ink texture at the edges of the letterforms. Confident and quiet.
```

### Sizes to render each direction at

| Use | Ratio | Pixels | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luma cover | 1:1 | 1080 × 1080 | Renders as a rounded square in the sidebar |
| LinkedIn feed | 4:5 | 1080 × 1350 | House master format, matches `assets/social/launch-campaign.md` |
| Carousel page | 4:5 | 1080 × 1350 | Every slide, same ratio |
| Story | 9:16 | 1080 × 1920 | Keep all copy inside the middle 70% vertically |
| Link preview | 1.91:1 | 1200 × 627 | Only if you share the Luma link as a raw URL |

Append the ratio to whichever generator you use. Generate direction 1 first and see whether the warmth survives; if it looks twee, switch to direction 3, which cannot fail.

## 7. Prompt to paste into Claude for design

Everything below the line is the prompt. Paste it as one block.

---

Build a social asset set for a small monthly dinner in Mississauga called **The Long Table**, run by MedTech North. Output every asset as its own frame, individually exportable as PNG. Do not put them on a shared canvas.

**What the evening is, so the design has the right temperature:** twelve or so people who work in health sit around one table. No talks, no slides, no pitching. A photographer is set up in the corner and everyone gets a proper portrait plus a ninety-second filmed answer to one question, all of which they keep. It is a dinner, not a conference. The design has to feel like a warm invitation from a person, not an event brand.

**This is a deliberate departure from the MedTech North institutional register.** Keep the craft standards, drop the formality.

**Design system**

- Palette: warm cream field (roughly #F4EFE6), deep ink text (#16191d), one accent crimson (#c41230) used sparingly and only for a single emphasis per frame. No other colours
- Type: a confident serif for headlines, set large and ragged right. A plain sans for support copy and footers. Real typographic hierarchy, at least a 4:1 size ratio between headline and support
- **Craft bans, absolute:** no emoji, no icon fonts, no stock icon libraries, no stock photography, no CSS blur, no gradients, no drop shadows, nothing left at a default size. Any illustration must be drawn as SVG line work
- **Never depict people, faces, or a populated room.** Empty table, empty stool, objects, light. This is a legal and honesty constraint, not a style preference
- Texture is allowed and encouraged: paper tooth, slight halftone, letterpress ink edges. This is what stops it reading as corporate
- 8px grid. One dominant element per frame

**Assets to build**

1. **Luma cover**, 1080 × 1080. Drawn line-art long table with a camera and softbox at the far end, empty stool. Title across the upper third
2. **Announcement frame**, 1080 × 1350. Typographic. Headline: *Dinner, portraits, and one good question.* Support: *Mississauga, Thursday 27 August*
3. **The question frame**, 1080 × 1350. The question set as the entire image, five or six lines, ragged right, one word underlined in crimson
4. **The portrait frame**, 1080 × 1350. Drawn stool, softbox and tripod in line work. Headline: *There's a photographer in the corner.* Support: *Five minutes. You keep the files.*
5. **Story version**, 1080 × 1920, of assets 2 and 3, with all copy inside the middle 70% vertically
6. **Carousel**, eight frames at 1080 × 1350, copy supplied below, typeset exactly as written

**Carousel copy, typeset exactly, do not rewrite or add words**

1. *Dinner, portraits, and one good question.* / Mississauga, Thursday 27 August
2. *Once a month I put a small group of health people around one table.*
3. *No slides. Nobody presents. Nobody pitches.*
4. *There's a photographer set up in the corner.*
5. *Five minutes, proper lighting. The files are yours a few days later.*
6. *And everyone gets asked the same question.*
7. *What's the one thing people outside your work always get wrong about it?*
8. *Ninety seconds. Nothing to prepare. You keep that too.* / Link below

Frames 1 and 7 carry the most visual weight. Frame 7 is the payoff and should be the largest type in the set. Frames 2, 3, 5 and 8 are quiet by comparison. A viewer swiping should feel the rhythm change at 7.

**Footer on every frame:** the wordmark small on the left, "Mississauga" on the right, in the plain sans at small size. Nothing else.

## 8. Social content kit

Six posts across twenty days. All ICP-agnostic, all survive the fallback. Publish as written.

### Post 1. Day 1, announcement

```
I'm running the next dinner on Thursday 27 August in Mississauga.

Same as always: about twelve people who work in health, one table, no talks, nobody selling anything to anybody.

One change this time. I'm bringing in a photographer with proper lighting. Everyone who wants one sits down for five minutes and gets a real portrait, files sent over a few days later, yours to keep and use however you like.

And I'm asking everyone the same question on camera. Ninety seconds, nothing to prepare.

What's the one thing people outside your work always get wrong about it?

I've wanted to ask a room that for a while. Dentists, pharmacists, physios, researchers, people building software for clinics, they all have an answer and none of the answers are the same.

Dinner's covered, there's no cost. Link's in the comments if you want a seat.
```

### Post 2. Day 5, the question

```
I asked a clinical pharmacist last year what people get wrong about her job.

She said everyone thinks she counts pills. She'd spent that morning catching a drug interaction that would have put someone in hospital.

That's the question I'm putting to everyone at the dinner on the 27th. On camera, ninety seconds, and you keep the clip.

Not because it makes good content, although it does. Because most people in health spend their whole career being slightly misunderstood by everyone outside it, and almost nobody gets asked about it directly.

Mississauga, Thursday 27 August. Link in the comments.
```

> **Check before posting.** Post 2 refers to a real conversation. If that exchange did not happen, either replace it with one that did or cut the first two paragraphs and open at "That's the question". Do not invent the pharmacist.

### Post 3. Day 9, the portrait

```
Something I've noticed doing these dinners.

Almost nobody who works in health has a photo of themselves they actually like. The one on the clinic website is six years old. The LinkedIn one is cropped out of a wedding.

It's a small thing and it isn't a small thing. That photo is the first impression for every patient checking you out before they book, every founder deciding whether to email you, every journalist looking for someone to quote.

So there'll be a photographer at the dinner on the 27th with real lighting. Five minutes, you keep the files, no cost and nothing attached to it.

Thursday 27 August, Mississauga. Link in the comments.
```

### Post 4. Day 14, carousel caption

```
What happens at the dinner on the 27th, in eight slides.

Short version: you eat, you talk to people who understand your job, you sit for a portrait, you answer one question on camera, you go home by nine thirty with a headshot and a clip that belong to you.

Mississauga, Thursday 27 August. Link in the comments.
```

### Post 5. Day 17, works whichever way the room went

```
The table for the 27th is coming together and it's a good mix. Practice owners, a couple of researchers, people building software that ends up in clinics.

That mix is the entire point. A dentist and a pharmacist will describe the same patient completely differently and both of them are right, and you only find that out if they're sitting close enough to argue about it over dinner.

A few seats left. Mississauga, Thursday 27 August, no cost, dinner's covered.

## 10. What never appears in public copy or images

- A headcount, past or expected. Two rooms have happened and the first held 16 (`data/verified-stats.md`)
- Photos of a previous event, or any image containing people
- The words sponsor, sponsorship, partner tier, exhibitor, booth, lead access (`ADR-007`)
- Any professional title used as a label: no Chief, Fellow, Chair, Consultant, Specialist (`CLAUDE.md` rule 5)
- A claim that physicians attend. None has yet
- Limited spots, last chance, filling fast, or any countdown
- Em dashes, en dashes, exclamation marks

## 11. One flag on voice

This kit deliberately breaks the register in `docs/07-brand-and-voice.md`, which says write as you would to a hospital chief of staff and warns against "community" as the framing. That was the right call for the website and it is the wrong call for a dinner invitation, which is why this copy sounds like a person rather than an institution.

Everything else in that document is honoured exactly: no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no urgency devices, no banned words, no headcounts, no scarcity as pressure.

**If this warmer register becomes the standing voice for event copy rather than a one-off, it needs its own ADR.** Do not let it drift into the site by accident.
