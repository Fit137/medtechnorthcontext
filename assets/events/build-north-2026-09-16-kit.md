## 1. The name

**Build North.** Published as **Build North. The MedTech North Table #3**.

It is short, it survives at thumbnail size, it works as a hashtag and a wordmark, and it is a verb and a direction rather than a slogan. "Build in Canada" was the alternative and it is weaker: longer, harder to set typographically, and it reads as a campaign somebody else already owns.

Edition numbering is standing practice (`ADR-024`). Cancelled room 003 did not consume a number (`ADR-025`), so this is **Table #3**.

Brand discipline is unchanged. The brand appears in the title, in the image footer, in slide one, and in the first line of a direct message. Not in the body of a human sentence.

## 2. What the public page is for

Same as room 003, with one change. The page is a landing surface, not an acquisition engine. Somebody reads it after your message reaches them, checks that it looks real, and buys.

**The change: this page now has to close a sale, not just confirm one.** A free page needed to look legitimate. A paid page needs to answer "what am I getting for $125" in the first six lines. That is what section 5 leads on and it is why the three minutes are stated before anything else.

## 3. The format

**Every seat gets the same three minutes.** Twenty seats, sixty minutes, one question asked of everyone in the same words.

> **What did you build this year, and what would make the next one easier to build here?**

No slides, no decks, no demos, no product names on a screen. Nobody can buy a longer slot and nobody gets a shorter one. Then the floor closes and the evening goes back to being dinner.

**Why identical slots and not a speaker line-up.** A line-up splits the room into featured and attending, which ranks guests, which `CLAUDE.md` rule 6 forbids and which is unrecoverable once anyone notices. `ADR-023` reached the same conclusion for a different reason and settled on one identical question for everyone. That principle carries here.

**Why this is not stage time being sold.** The ticket buys a seat. Every seat carries the same three minutes, so the money buys nothing that anyone else's money does not also buy. The moment one person can pay for six minutes, the rule is gone and so is the room.

**Running order is drawn at the table on the night.** Not sequenced by segment, not led off by the investors, not closed by the founders. Drawn. That is the cheapest possible guarantee against an implied hierarchy and it takes ten seconds to demonstrate in front of everyone.

**Timing.**

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 6:30 | Doors, dinner is out |
| 7:20 | The floor opens. Three minutes each, drawn order |
| 8:25 | Floor closes |
| 8:25 to 9:00 | Dinner and conversation |

Sixty minutes of floor is the hard part. Twenty people run long unless somebody runs it properly. Use a visible timer on the table, say at the start that you will cut people off warmly, and cut the first person off warmly so the room believes you.

**The question is also the year's structured question.** `ADR-019` requires every dinner to carry one, because five rooms answering the same question is an insight brief and five rooms answering nothing is five meals. Capture the answers the same night while they are legible.

## 8. Image generation prompts

**Four hard rules:**

1. **No people, no crowds, no dinner scenes.** An image of a full table implies attendance you cannot evidence (`CLAUDE.md` rule 3)
2. **No flags, no maple leaves, no hockey, no beavers, no mountains.** The red is the only national cue and it is already the brand colour
3. **No logos, no wordmarks generated in the image.** Type is added at layout. Image models mangle letterforms
4. **No construction clichés.** No hard hats, no cranes, no gears, no lightbulbs, no handshakes

Palette for all directions: ink `#16191d`, red `#c41230`, warm off-white. Red appears on one element only, never as a field.

### 13b. The venue, and this is the serious one

**Rooms 001 to 003 were run in the dining lounge of Ali's condo building. Written permission for commercial use has never been requested, and `HANDOFF.md` records the assumption that it is prohibited.**

Selling twenty tickets changes that question materially. A free gathering of guests is arguable. A ticketed event with a Stripe account attached is a commercial event held in a residential amenity space, and it is much harder to characterise as anything else if the board asks.

Second exposure, from the same file: **no commercial general liability insurance is in force.** It is listed as the highest-priority unmitigated risk, and it now has paying customers attached to it.

Third, and smaller but real: the residential venue already cost a confirmed speaker at room 001 on legitimacy grounds. This room's guests are founders, investors and clinicians paying up to $300.

**Recommendation: book a private dining room instead, and let the ticket revenue pay for it.** At the published ladder the room grosses $3,750 before any service provider seat. The financial model carries a restaurant seat at $105 against $55 partner-hosted (`ADR-021`), so twenty seats out is roughly $2,100. The room pays for its own venue with margin left, and that removes all three exposures at once. If you keep the condo, get the board's written position first and get an insurance quote today, because the lead time on insurance is the longest thing on this list.

### 13c. The runway

**Seven selling days for a paid room, from a standing start, with room 003 cancelled on this same list three weeks ago.**

Room 003's failure was late confirmations on a free room, and tickets fix exactly that. But they fix the signal, not the volume. Twenty paid seats in seven days needs roughly fifty direct messages sent in the first forty-eight hours, and the page will not do it for you.

**The decision to make today, not on the 14th:** if fewer than ten seats are sold by end of Monday 8 September, move the date rather than run a thin room. `ADR-025` is unambiguous on this, and a room that composes badly costs more than a room that moves. Moving on the 8th is an ordinary scheduling note. Moving on the 14th is the second cancellation in a month, to the same list.
