# Room 003 venue branding. What to print, where to put it, what it costs

> **For: The MedTech North Table #3. Wednesday 26 August 2026, 6:30 to 9:00 pm, Mississauga.**
> Companion to `assets/events/room-003-long-table-kit.md`, which owns the copy and the outreach.
> This file owns the physical objects in the room.
>
> **Written 19 August 2026. Seven days to the door.** Every recommendation below is scoped to that, which rules out anything with a proof cycle, a die, a foil block or an international shipment.

---

## 0. The brief, restated

Get the mark into photographs taken in a private dining room, on a small budget, without making a dinner look like a trade show.

Two audiences take photos in that room. Ali or a photographer takes wide shots of the table. Guests take phone photos of their own plate, their own drink, and the person across from them. The second group is more valuable and almost always ignored, because a sign on an easel behind the room does nothing for a photo taken at the table looking across it.

**So the spending priority inverts the obvious one.** Table surface first, wall second.

## 2. The constraint that decides the whole list

`ADR-023` and the room kit both hold the same promise: nothing is asked of the guest. No agenda, nothing to prepare, nothing to present. The room kit's image rules go further and ban depicting cards, boards, notes and pens, on the grounds that a pen on a table contradicts the offer.

That rule was written for illustrations. It applies with more force to the real table.

**Therefore, from the existing catalogue in `assets/design/printables-and-merch.md`:**

| Item | Verdict for room 003 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| B1 place card | **Print** | Hospitality, not homework. Helps people use each other's names |
| B3 menu card | **Print** | People photograph menus without being asked to |
| B7 napkin band | **Print** | The cheapest object that makes a table look composed |
| B6 coaster | **Print if time allows** | Sits under the thing already in every photo |
| B2 composition card | **Cut** | Describes the room's makeup, which `ADR-024` says the free track never promises |
| B4 conversation cards | **Cut** | A deck of prompts is an agenda in a box |
| B5 introduction slip | **Cut** | A form a guest fills in at dinner. This is the clearest breach of the promise on the list |
| D1 nomination card | **Print, but hand it at the door on the way out** | On the table it is an ask. In a coat pocket at 9pm it is a gift |

Place cards carry the name only. No job title, no company, no group marker for this room. The marker system belongs to the composed paid track, and printing it here would imply a curation promise the free dinner does not make.

## 4. Placement map

Camera sits at the doors, above table height, looking down the table. Plan for that shot and for the phone shot taken across the table.

```
        DOUBLE DOORS                       FAR WALL
   [11] framed poster, leaning
   [8]  nomination cards, stacked
        on the shelf. Not on the table
              |
              v
   ============================================   <- SLAT WALL, right side
   |  [3] band   [3] band   [3] band   [3]    |      Leaning poster sits at the
   |  [1] name   [1] name   [1] name   [1]    |      FAR end of this wall so it
   |  [6] can    [6] can    [6] can    [6]    |      clears the pendant lights
   |                                          |
   |         [4] Table #3 tent                |
   |         [7] banded catering box          |   <- centre of the table, where
   |         [2] menu cards                   |      food and drink already sit
   |                                          |
   |  [1] name   [1] name   [1] name   [1]    |
   |  [9] coaster  [9] coaster  [9] coaster   |
   ============================================
              ^
        [5] house rules card, framed,
        near end of the table.
        Largest object in the door shot

                          [ SOFA + RUG ] <- portrait station.
                                            NO BRANDING IN THIS FRAME
```

**Four placement rules for this specific room:**

1. **Nothing goes above table height in the middle third of the room.** The three pendant lanterns hang low and they are the best feature in the photograph. Anything that competes with them loses.
2. **The leaning poster goes at the far end of the slat wall**, so it appears in the top corner of the door shot rather than behind a guest's head.
3. **The warm wood and warm light in this room will drag any print towards yellow.** Set everything as ink on white, or white reversed out of ink. A cream or off-white stock will read as dirty against the oak. `assets/design/design-system-tokens.md` already struck cream from the system, which happens to be the right call here for a second reason.
4. **Repetition beats size.** Fourteen small marks at fourteen settings puts the brand in more frames than one large sign ever will, and it does it without anybody noticing they are being marketed to.

## 5. The portrait station is not a branding surface

The room has a photographer and a lighting setup, and the portrait is a gift. Guests take those files and use them on their own websites, profiles and ads. That is the entire private lever the outreach messages are built on.

**A visible logo behind a guest's head destroys the value of the gift.** A clinic owner cannot use a headshot with somebody else's brand behind them, so they will not use it, so the lever stops working from room 004 onward.

Keep the sofa corner clean. Brand the surrounding room instead, which is where the behind the scenes shots get taken anyway.

## 6. Artwork rules, short version

Full specification lives in `assets/design/printables-and-merch.md` sections 2, 5 and 6. For a seven day turnaround, the short list:

- **Palette.** Ink `#16191d`, white, red `#c41230` for one emphasis per item. Cool, never cream.
- **Type.** `--font-display` for the one headline, `--font-ui` for everything else. Never name a typeface in a design prompt, reference the token.
- **Bleed** 3mm all round, or 0.125in on North American trim sizes. **Safety margin** 5mm.
- **Text black is K100 single channel.** Never rich black on type this small.
- **Outline all fonts.** Export PDF/X-1a for flat process work at these sizes.
- **The mark.** Use the compact version below 18mm. On a napkin band and a can wrap, use the single line version, because a full mark at that width will fill in on a digital press.
- **Clear space** equals the height of the up-triangle, and the trim edge counts as an intrusion.
- **Print one set on a home printer at actual size before ordering.** The small mark test in section 8 of the printables doc exists precisely for jobs like this one.

**Copy on every item obeys the standing bans:** no sponsor language, no headcount, no testimonial, no professional title used as a label, no em dashes, no exclamation marks, no scarcity device. Edition number is fine and is standing practice under `ADR-024`.

## 9. What this leaves for later

Nothing here needs re-buying. The frame, the poster and the sticker artwork carry to room 004. The can wrap, napkin band, menu and place card artwork become templates with an editable date and edition number, which is what section 5 of the printables doc means by a `Variable` layer.

## 10. What not to make, and why

| Do not make | Reason |
|---|---|
| Name badges or lanyards | Turns a dinner into a conference in one object. Place cards do the same job better |
| Anything with a QR code on the table | It is an instruction, and nothing is asked of the guest |
| A step and repeat backdrop | Wrong format, wrong budget, and it would sit exactly where the portrait station goes |
| Printed agendas or programmes | There is no programme. That is the offer |
| Anything naming a company other than MedTech North | `ADR-007`. No sponsor language appears in anything a member reads |
| A sign stating how many people are at the table | `CLAUDE.md` rule 3 |
| Custom printed napkins or custom glassware | Minimum orders and lead times are both longer than seven days |
