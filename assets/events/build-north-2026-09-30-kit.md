## 1. The name and the series word

**Build North**, published as **Build North: A Celebration of Canadian Health Tech**.

Short, works at thumbnail size, sets as a wordmark, and it is a direction rather than a slogan. "Build in Canada" is longer, harder to set, and reads as somebody else's campaign.

**Both "Table" and "Session" come off the title.** Table reads as a dinner and Session reads as work, and the evening is neither. The subtitle now carries what the room is for, which is the job a subtitle should do.

**The edition number moves out of the title.** `ADR-024` says number every edition publicly and that still holds, so the number goes in the first line of a direct message and in the footer of any printed piece. It is off the Luma title because "No. 3" next to "Celebration" reads as bureaucracy. The alternate title in section 4 carries it for anyone who wants it there. Edition numbering continues unbroken: room 003 was cancelled without consuming a number (`ADR-025`), so this is the third.

Brand discipline is unchanged. MedTech North appears in the title, the image footer, slide one, and the opening line of a direct message. Not in the middle of a human sentence.

## 2. What the public page is for

The page is a landing surface, not an acquisition engine. Somebody reads it after your message reaches them, checks that it looks real, and registers.

**What changed now that there is a price.** A free page only had to look legitimate. This one has to make somebody want something in the first three lines, and what they want is to be heard. So the page opens on the recognition and puts the logistics at the bottom, which is the reverse of how the last three pages were built.

## 3. How the evening runs

**The offer is recognition.** That is what the page leads with and it is what the evening delivers. Everybody in this room is doing work that almost nobody claps for, and for three hours they get heard by people who understand what it cost. Everything else in the design serves that.

**The stage.** Every person in the room gets three minutes, same clock, order drawn on the night.

> **What are you building, and why did you decide to build it here?**

No slides, no decks, no demos. Nobody can buy a longer slot.

The second half of the question is the one that matters. Anybody can list a product. Saying out loud why you chose the harder market is the part that gets recognised, and it is the part that makes twenty answers add up to an argument about the country rather than twenty updates.

**The rest is a celebration and it should look like one.** Food and drinks out from the start, not held back until the programme ends. Music low but on. No lectern, no head table, no name badges with tiers on them.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 6:00 | Arrivals. Food and drinks are out |
| 6:40 | Open. Five minutes, the question and the two rules |
| 6:45 | **The stage.** Three minutes each, drawn order |
| 7:50 | Done. The rest of the evening is the room |
| 9:00 | Close |

**The two rules are said aloud at the open and nowhere else.** Nobody sells anything to anybody, and nothing said in the room gets repeated outside it. The rules block came off the public page because it read as a list of restrictions on an invitation, which is the wrong first impression for a celebration. The rules themselves have not changed and one of them is in the description as a sentence rather than a heading.

**Sixty-five minutes for twenty people is the hard part.** Use a visible timer, say at the open that you will cut people off warmly, then cut the first person off warmly so the room believes you. Run long and you eat the celebration, which is the thing people paid for.

**Capture it.** Photograph the room and film the three minutes if you can staff it, because this is the first room that generates material worth keeping and every guest owns their own clip. Consent in writing before anything is published, per section 13a. If the budget will not carry a photographer at this ticket price, do it on a phone on a tripod rather than not at all.

**Why every slot is identical.** A speaker line-up splits a room into featured and attending, which ranks guests. `CLAUDE.md` rule 6 forbids it and `ADR-023` reached the same conclusion for room 003 by a different route. Drawing the order at the table is ten seconds of work and it settles the question in front of everyone.

**Why this is not stage time being sold.** The ticket buys a seat. Every seat carries the same three minutes, so the money buys nothing that anyone else's money does not also buy. The moment somebody can pay for six minutes, the rule is gone and so is the room.

**The question is also the year's structured question.** `ADR-019` requires every room to carry one, because five rooms answering the same question is a publication and five rooms answering nothing is five evenings. "Why did you decide to build it here" is that question, and twenty answers to it are worth more than any panel on the same subject. Write them down the same night.

### 5a. Lines that are load bearing. Do not cut them

| Line | Why it stays |
|---|---|
| "a celebration of the people who choose Canada" | The whole event in nine words, and the line the title is built from |
| The three-line list | Builders, capital and talent, each named by the choice they made. Cutting any one of them shrinks the room |
| "shortens the road from a Canadian idea to a Canadian patient" | The patient is the reason any of this matters, and this is the only line that says so |
| "why did you decide to build it here?" | The second half of the question is what makes this Build North |
| "Everyone speaks, everyone listens" | Carries the no-selling rule without naming it, which is what keeps the page positive |
| "I read every one, and if the room is full for your group I'll refund you that day" | `ADR-026` requires it. A sold seat is not an entitlement to attend, and the refund is the mechanism |
| The sources line | Rule 4 |

**Do not add:** a headcount from a past room, a past-event photo, a testimonial, a logo, the word sponsor, a tariff figure, or a line about limited spots.

### 5b. Three notes on the brief

**On the celebration.** The word is in the title and in the first line, and then the page stops saying it and starts being it. Three minutes each, everyone heard, food out, room open. A page that keeps announcing that it is a celebration is a page nobody believes.

**On "exclusive".** The word cannot go on the page. `docs/07-brand-and-voice.md` allows it once, only in the literal sense of category exclusivity, and it names "limited spots" as the failure. Twenty is stated as a fact and the price ladder does the rest.

**On recognition without implying a credential.** Rule 7 forbids implying accreditation, endorsement or outcomes. Nothing says honoured, award, or recognised leader. The promise is three minutes and twenty people listening.

### 5c. Structures banned on sight

Ali's rules, 4 and 15 September. All three survive rewrites unless you go looking.

**1. Via negativa. Defining the evening by what it lacks.** No "nobody pitches", no "no slides", no "there's no agenda". The reader should never have to assemble the offer out of absences. Where a rule matters, state the positive behaviour: "everyone speaks, everyone listens".

**2. "It is not X, it is Y", and every variant, including "X, not Y".** It reads as a writer performing a reveal. Say the thing and drop the half you were going to negate.

**3. Three parallel clauses in a row, especially "the X, the Y, the Z".** Two is fine, four is fine, three in matching shape is the tell. The three groups on this page are set as a list rather than a sentence for exactly that reason: an enumeration reads as information, a triad reads as rhythm.

### 5d. What the warmth pass held on to

First person throughout, contractions where they fit, times written the way people say them, and no closing summary. The page ends on the address and the food, which is where a real invitation ends.

## 8. Image generation prompts

**Four hard rules:**

1. **No people, no crowds, no audience shots.** An image of a full room implies attendance you cannot evidence. Rule 3
2. **No wordmarks or logos generated in the image.** Type is added at layout. Image models mangle letterforms
3. **No dinner imagery and no construction clichés.** No plates, no cutlery, no hard hats, no cranes, no gears, no lightbulbs, no handshakes
4. **Flag references are in, hockey and beavers and mountains are out.** Ali's call, 4 September. The flag is used as geometry and as a leaf, never as a costume

Palette throughout: ink `#16191d`, red `#c41230`, warm off-white.

**The one thing that will ruin any of these: two different reds.** The Canadian flag red is roughly `#EF3340`, the brand red is `#c41230`, and side by side they look like a printing error rather than a decision. **Every prompt below builds the flag out of `#c41230` only.** A guest reads the shape as Canadian. A designer reads the colour as MedTech North. Nobody sees a clash.
