# MedTech North — Site Map, Hierarchy & Content

**Version 3.0.** Supersedes the single-page site. Written for Claude Code, to be built against the live repo.

---

## Two non-negotiables

**No sponsorship, anywhere.** Not a section, sentence, nav item, footer link, FAQ answer or metadata string. Partnership and monetisation live in private documents shared on calls. The site faces one direction: the people who join the room.

**Copy is sparse because the visuals carry the argument.** Every surface below is a headline, a short passage, and a note on what the visual must do. If a section only works because of its paragraph, the visual is underbuilt.

The four ICP pages are reached from one wayfinding surface on the homepage and from the footer. They are deliberately absent from the top nav, so nobody is asked to categorise themselves before they understand what this is.

**Footer:** wordmark, one line, four ICP links, The Rooms, Directory, Register, contact, LinkedIn.

---

## Hierarchy principle

The homepage argues about the country. The ICP pages argue about the reader. Nothing on the homepage addresses a segment, and nothing on an ICP page restates the national argument at length.

# HOME

Eight surfaces. Anyone from any group should finish it feeling the same thing.

## H1 · Hero

**Headline:** Canada invents faster than it adopts.

**Support:** Our hospitals, labs and companies build things the world eventually uses. Canadians often get them last. This is a table for the people who can change that.

**Action:** Register interest

**Visual carries:** the composed table at rest, ambient. The signature object.

## H2 · The paradox

**Headline:** We are not short of invention.

**Figures, rendered inside the visual rather than set as text:**
- 8th largest medical device market in the world
- 1,500+ medtech companies
- 6,000+ life sciences companies, 4th most active biotech sector in the OECD
- $344 billion in annual health spending, 12.1% of GDP

**Support:** A new device takes more than a year to reach a Canadian patient, and that is the best case. Many take several years. Some never arrive.

Three quarters of Ontario's academic hospitals name procurement rules and directives as a major hurdle to adopting innovation. That is the system describing itself.

**Closing line:** The invention is finished. The adoption isn't.

**Sourcing, small:** Medtech Canada, ISED, Invest in Canada, CIHI, Council of Academic Hospitals of Ontario.

## H3 · Why now

**Headline:** Something changed in December.

**Support:** On 16 December 2025 the federal Buy Canadian policy took effect, with health and pharmaceuticals among five named strategic sectors. Ontario passed its own act five days earlier. In June the threshold for Canadian preference fell from twenty-five million dollars to five.

Preference for Canadian health technology now exists in policy. The relationships that would turn preference into adoption do not.

## H4 · The room

**Headline:** Small rooms, composed by hand.

**Support:** Four groups, seated deliberately. Builders and validators in balance. No stage, no slides, no badge scanning. What is said at the table stays there, so people can talk plainly about procurement, regulation and money.

## H5 · The rules

**Headline:** Rules we don't bend.

**Nobody buys a microphone.** No one pays for stage time or a speaking slot. The room is not for sale.

**No one here is anyone's prospect.** Clinicians aren't pitched. Founders aren't sold to. Everyone at the table is a peer.

**Nothing leaves the table.** Chatham House by default, in every room, without exception.

**Composition over headcount.** We'll tell you who was in the room by role. We won't tell you how many, because it isn't the point.

## H6 · What's at stake

**Headline:** Three things happen when the room doesn't exist.

**A patient waits.** Something that already works elsewhere takes years to arrive here, or never does.

**A company leaves.** A Canadian founder raises the next round abroad, moves the team, and the return on public research money lands in another health system.

**A hospital buys a stranger.** The domestic product was never in the room, so the contract goes to the vendor who was.

**Closing:** None of that gets solved by better technology. It gets solved by the right people meeting early enough for it to matter.

## H7 · Find your seat

Wayfinding, not messaging. Four cards, name and one line. No benefit copy, no pitch.

| Card | Line |
|---|---|
| Clinicians & researchers | Physicians, dentists, pharmacists, nurses, allied health, scientists |
| Builders | Companies building health technology, at every stage |
| Policy & government | Federal, provincial, regional and health system |
| Students & graduates | Trainees and early career, across every discipline |

## H8 · Register

**Headline:** Ask for a seat.

**Support:** Every request is read by a person. Tell us who you are and what you're working on.

**Fields:** name, email, LinkedIn, "I am a…" (all four groups plus sub-segments), organisation and role, one or two sentences on what you're building, researching, funding or deciding.

**Submit:** Send my request

**Confirmation:** Received. A person will read this. If the next room is a fit you'll hear from us with the date and who's at the table. If it isn't, you'll hear from us anyway.

# /CLINICIANS

**Register note:** clinical and academic vocabulary. Point of care, workflow, care pathway, evidence generation, scope of practice, translational, multidisciplinary.

**Composition note for the build:** this page must not read as physician-first. The room that actually assembles is dentists, clinical pharmacists, physiotherapists, allied health and researchers. Physicians are one discipline among many here, not the headline.

## C1 · Hero

**Headline:** You see it after the design is frozen.

**Support:** By the time most health technology reaches you, it's a finished product and a vendor meeting. The decisions that would have made it fit your workflow were made eighteen months earlier, by people who never watched you work.

## C2 · What the room is for

**Headline:** Input early enough to change something.

**Support:** Founders bring what they're building at the stage where it can still move. You say where it breaks in real conditions. That conversation usually happens after launch, in a procurement meeting, when nobody can change anything.

**Three lines:**
- Technology at the point where clinical input still alters it
- Peers from institutions and disciplines you rarely share a table with
- No pitching, in either direction

## C3 · Who's at the table

**Headline:** Clinical and academic, broadly defined.

**Support:** Dentists. Clinical and community pharmacists. Physiotherapists, occupational therapists, respiratory therapists, radiation therapists, speech-language pathologists and dietitians. Nurses and nurse practitioners. Physicians and surgeons across every specialty. Medical laboratory technologists and diagnostic imaging professionals.

Clinical trialists and translational researchers. Biomedical and academic scientists. Health services researchers, epidemiologists and health economists.

Hospital and practice leadership: chiefs, program directors, medical affairs, quality and safety.

## C4 · How you attend

**Headline:** As our guest. By nomination.

**Support:** There is no fee at any tier, in any year, permanently. Access comes through nomination by someone already in the room, or by request, and every one is reviewed individually.

Free here means chosen, not discounted.

**Actions:** Request an invitation · Nominate a colleague

## C5 · The rules that apply to you

Reuse the four site rules, led by "no one here is anyone's prospect."

# /BUILDERS

**Register note:** commercial and operating vocabulary. Runway, traction, pilot, clinical champion, procurement cycle, reimbursement pathway, regulatory strategy, commercialisation.

## B1 · Hero

**Headline:** Eighteen months for one meeting.

**Support:** That's what it can take to reach the clinical lead who decides whether your product lives. She isn't hiding. She works forty minutes away. There's just no room where the two of you end up.

## B2 · What you get

**Headline:** The people who actually decide.

**Support:** A clinical champion who'll tell you where your product breaks in practice. Someone who understands how their organisation really buys, as opposed to how the tender document says it buys. An investor who has seen this pathway before.

**Three lines:**
- Seating built around what you're working on
- Introductions made personally within the week
- Peers hitting the same regulatory and procurement walls

## B3 · Who's at the table

**Headline:** Every sector, every stage.

**Sectors:** Medical devices and diagnostics. Digital health and health IT. Biotech and therapeutics. Pharmaceuticals. Clinical AI and decision support. Remote monitoring and virtual care. Laboratory tools and life science instrumentation. Dental technology. Mental and behavioural health. Femtech, agetech and paediatric health.

**Models:** B2B, B2B2C, provider-facing, payer-facing, direct to consumer, and the hybrids that fit none of those.

**Stages:** Pre-seed and seed. Series A and B. Growth and scale-up. Established companies opening a Canadian operation, and international companies entering the market.

**Roles:** Founders and co-founders. CEOs, COOs and commercial leads. Heads of clinical, regulatory and market access. Business development.

## B4 · How membership works

**Headline:** By application.

**Support:** Membership is deliberately small and application-based. It takes about ten minutes and asks what you're building, what you need from the room, and what you bring to it.

| Guest | Invitation only, for people who aren't selling into this sector |
| Chapter | Every room in your home province |
| National | Every chapter, plus the summit |
| Circle | Everything, everywhere, plus a hand in shaping the rooms |

**No prices on this page.** Four scope statements, nothing more. Details are covered in the application conversation.

**Action:** Apply

# /POLICY

**Register note:** system and public sector vocabulary. Capacity, procurement pathways, health technology assessment, value-based procurement, interoperability, jurisdictional, evidence-informed, domestic capacity.

## P1 · Hero

**Headline:** The barrier reaches you as a report, two years late.

**Support:** By the time a procurement obstacle appears in an evaluation, the founder hit it two years ago and the clinician has stopped mentioning it. At this table they're two seats away, and it's Chatham House, so they'll say it plainly.

## P2 · What changed

**Headline:** The policy moved. The pathways didn't.

**Support:** Buy Canadian took effect in December 2025 with health and pharmaceuticals among five strategic sectors. The Buy Ontario Act received royal assent days earlier. The federal threshold fell to five million dollars in June 2026.

Preference now exists. A hospital can prefer a Canadian supplier and still not know who they are, and a founder can qualify for that preference and never meet a procurement lead.

**Honest note, kept on the page:** whether these measures shift enough volume is genuinely debated. That debate is a reason to build the pathways, not to wait for them.

## P3 · Who's at the table

**Headline:** System, not just government.

**Support:** Federal: Health Canada, the Public Health Agency, ISED, CIHR, federal procurement.

Provincial: ministries of health, Ontario Health and its regional structures, provincial agencies and Crown bodies.

Regional and municipal: public health units, Ontario Health Teams, economic development.

Health system: hospital procurement and supply chain organisations, shared services, clinical program leadership.

Assessment and regulation: health technology assessment, regulatory affairs, privacy and data governance.

## P4 · How you attend

**Headline:** As our guest, and never as a target.

**Support:** No fee, permanently. Attendance is by invitation, and the rules are written for you before anyone else. Nobody buys stage time. Nobody is anyone's prospect. Nothing leaves the table.

**Action:** Request an invitation

# Content rules for the build

**Never appears on this site:** sponsor, sponsorship, partner tier, anchor, category-exclusive, exhibitor, underwriter, booth, media rights, lead access, or any sentence describing how the organisation earns money.

**Never claimed:** past events, member counts, testimonials, attendance figures, outcomes, endorsements, accreditation, professional credit. One room has happened. The copy must not imply more.

**No fixed seat count anywhere.** "Small rooms" and "composed by hand" carry the scarcity. A specific number becomes a claim to defend.

**Every figure** traces to the sourced set in H2 and P2.

**No regulated titles** applied to anyone who does not hold them: Resident, Attending, Chief, Fellow, Chair, Registrar, Consultant, Specialist, Practitioner, Associate.

**Voice:** executive throughout. No exclamation marks, no urgency devices, no second-person hype. Scarcity is stated as a property of the format, never as pressure. The word "exclusive" appears nowhere.
