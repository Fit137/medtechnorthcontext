# Executive Table Outreach: messaging

> **INTERNAL.** Copy for working `data/prospects/executive-table-targets.csv`. Room context: `docs/17-who-belongs-in-the-room.md` (option F). Page copy: `assets/events/room-003-luma-copy.md`.
>
> **No pricing, no tier language, no commercial vocabulary anywhere in these messages.** Per `CLAUDE.md` rule 2, that conversation happens on a call and never in anything a guest reads. **No headcount claims and no track record.**

---

## The four rules these drafts follow

Drawn from the audit of the outreach that went to the first executive (`data/prospects/smile-digital-health.md`, section 3).

1. **The first ask is cheap.** No question requiring an essay. A senior person answers what takes thirty seconds and ignores what takes ten minutes.
2. **The frame never changes mid-thread.** They are invited as a guest and they stay one. Nothing in the first three messages positions them as a buyer.
3. **Nothing is promised that cannot be delivered.** Composition is described as what is confirmed, never as what will be assembled for them.
4. **Every message contains a graceful exit.** Counterintuitively this raises reply rates, and it protects the relationship on a no.

---

## Volume plan: send in waves, not in a batch

Four seats are open and there are 28 targets. **Sending all 28 creates a decline problem**, which is a worse outcome than sending fewer. Over-inviting a table of eight means turning senior people away after they said yes.

| Wave | When | How many | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Today | Top 6 by priority | 4 acceptances |
| 2 | Day 4 | Next 6 | 4 acceptances |
| 3 | Day 8 | Remainder | 4 acceptances |

Space sends across the day rather than firing them in one sitting. The confirmed-composition line gets stronger with every acceptance, so later waves carry a better message than earlier ones.

## Track A. Founder-led chief executives (the peer group)

### A1. LinkedIn connection note, 300 character limit

```
[First name], I convene a small table in Mississauga. Eight seats, clinicians
and founders, no stage, no slides, nobody pitching. Confirmed so far: the CEO
of a health data platform, a professor of health sciences, a community
pharmacist. [Date]. Want the details?
```

### A2. First full message, after connecting or by InMail

Subject, if email: `A table of eight in Mississauga, [date]`

```
[First name],

[ONE SPECIFIC LINE. See the bank below. Verify it before sending.]

I convene a small table in Mississauga. Eight seats, one conversation,
composed across clinicians, researchers and founders.

No stage, no slides, nobody presents and nobody pitches. Seating is built by
hand beforehand. What is said at the table stays at the table.

Confirmed so far: the chief executive of a health data platform, a professor
of health sciences, a community pharmacist, and a founder working inside
hospital medical education.

[Date], [time]. Private lounge in Mississauga, dinner provided, no cost.

If it would be useful I will send you the composition and who you would be
seated beside. If the timing is wrong, say so and I will not come back to it
this year.

Ali
```

### A3. Single follow-up, day 5 or 6. Never a third.

```
[First name], closing the table this week. If the timing is wrong I will keep
you in mind for the next one.
```

## Track B. Health system and public buyers

Different framing entirely. A hospital chief executive is approached by vendors constantly, and the thing that earns a reply is an explicit guarantee that this is not that.

### B1. The nomination request, sent first

**To the registrant who works in medical education at Trillium.** This is the highest-value message on the page and it goes out before anything cold.

```
[First name], thank you for taking a seat.

One thing I would value your read on. I am holding a seat for someone senior
on the hospital side, because the table is currently weighted toward builders
and I would rather it were not.

Is there anyone inside Trillium you would want at a table like this? If there
is, and you are comfortable making the introduction, it would go a great deal
further than anything I could send cold. If not, no difficulty at all.
```

### B2. Cold approach to a system chief executive, if no nomination arrives

```
[Name],

[ONE SPECIFIC LINE. Verify it before sending.]

I convene a small table in Mississauga. Eight seats, clinicians, researchers
and founders, one conversation.

Two rules matter for a seat like yours. Nobody presents, and nobody sells.
Every guest is told who is at the table before they accept, and what is said
there stays there.

Confirmed so far: the chief executive of a health data platform, a professor
of health sciences, a community pharmacist, and a founder working inside
hospital medical education.

[Date], [time]. Ten minutes from Credit Valley, dinner provided, no cost.

If a seat is not right for you, is there someone on your team you would send
instead.

Ali
```

**The last line is the important one.** It converts a decline into an acceptance one level down, which for this room is nearly as valuable and considerably easier to get.

## Track C. Presidents and general managers of multinational subsidiaries

**Rank these last and expect a slower reply.** Per gate 1 in `docs/11-corporate-outreach.md`, what they own is a Canadian marketing plan rather than a company, so a private dinner reads to them as a marketing decision rather than a peer invitation.

```
[Name],

[ONE SPECIFIC LINE.]

I convene a small table in Mississauga. Eight seats, clinicians, researchers
and founders, no stage and no pitching.

The reason I am writing to you rather than to your marketing team is that this
is not a marketing slot and there is nothing to buy. It is a seat at a dinner.

Confirmed so far: the chief executive of a health data platform, a professor
of health sciences, a community pharmacist, and a founder working inside
hospital medical education.

[Date], [time]. Dinner provided, no cost.

If it is not for you, I will leave it there.

Ali
```

The second paragraph exists because without it the message is forwarded to field marketing and dies there.

## The specificity bank

**One line proving you know who they are is the single largest driver of executive reply rate.** Verify each before sending, because a wrong detail is worse than none.

| Target | Line to verify and use |
|---|---|
| Dave or Mike Wessinger, PointClickCare | Their work connecting hospital and long-term care records across Ontario regions |
| Karli Farrow, Trillium Health Partners | The Peter Gilgan Mississauga Hospital build |
| Sachin Aggarwal, Think Research | Order sets and clinical content deployed in Ontario hospitals |
| Michael Millar, Verto Health | Patient flow and data integration across providers |
| Aaron Leibtag, Pentavere | Extracting clinical detail from unstructured records |
| Tomi Poutanen, Signal 1 | The Unity Health Toronto deployment |
| Albert Tai, Hypercare | Clinical communication inside hospitals |
| Liran Belenzon, BenchSci | Scale reached in life sciences AI |
| Matthew Anderson, Ontario Health | The federated provincial data utility presented in 2026 |
| Gaby Bourbara, AstraZeneca Canada | The size of the Mississauga operation |

**Never open with flattery.** One factual sentence about their work, then the invitation.

## What must not appear in any of these messages

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| The confirmed guest's name or company | No permission has been requested. Role only, until it is held in writing |
| Any price, tier, package or sponsorship word | `CLAUDE.md` rule 2 |
| A headcount promise, or "eight executives" | A composition promise that misses is the one unrecoverable outcome |
| Member counts, attendee totals, past-event claims | `CLAUDE.md` rule 3 |
| "Limited seats", "spots filling", any countdown | Scarcity is a property of the format, never pressure |
| Exclamation marks, dashes, or hype | `docs/07-brand-and-voice.md` |

## On an acceptance

Send the Luma link, then run the confirmation ritual in `docs/17-who-belongs-in-the-room.md` section 9. The third registration question does the screening. Do not let an acceptance sit unconfirmed: the gap between a yes and a seated guest is where the 40 to 60% goes.

## On a decline

```
Understood, and thank you for the straight answer. I will come back to you
when the next one is composed. If there is someone you would put in the room
instead, I would welcome the name.
```

Every decline is a nomination opportunity, and this is the cheapest one available.
