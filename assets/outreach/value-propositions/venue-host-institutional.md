# Segment value proposition: institutional venue host

**Status:** draft, written for campaign 1. **Zero live conversations.** Every claim below is untested.
**Scope:** a named person at an organisation that owns multi-room space with in-house AV and has a mandate reason to host at no charge. Regional innovation centres, universities and colleges, economic development and investment attraction bodies, industry associations. Titles: Director of Programs, Director of Partnerships, Director of Industry Engagement, Executive Director, Sector Lead, Associate Dean.

Do not reuse this wording for a corporate marketing owner, who is being sold a room rather than asked for a building. See `assets/outreach/value-propositions/corporate-room-funder.md`. Do not reuse it for a clinician under any circumstances.

Campaign, kill numbers and timing: `docs/20-outreach-campaign-sequence.md`, campaign 1. Incentive model: `docs/19-host-incentives-and-the-media-floor.md`. Named list: `data/venue-host-targets.md`.

## What this segment is actually asking

Four questions, in this order, usually unstated:

1. **Is this a rental enquiry.** If it is, it goes to whoever handles bookings and dies there.
2. **What does it cost me, in money, staff time and risk.**
3. **What do I write in my report about why I said yes.**
4. **Who is going to be in my building, and will I be embarrassed by them.**

Question 3 is the one most messages never answer, and it is the one that decides. This person is not buying and does not have a budget conversation to have. They have an internal justification to construct, and the message either hands it to them or makes them build it alone.

## The proposition

Their facility is busy for a day with exactly the audience their mandate names, at no cost, and they end the week holding material they could not have commissioned.

The mechanic: we bring the people, the programme, the production and the insurance. They bring the rooms. Neither side invoices the other.

## What they get, in plain terms

- Their name alongside ours on the day
- Their team in the rooms as participants
- On-record interviews with practising clinicians and operators, filmed in their building, cut for their channels as well as ours, theirs to use
- Their category held for the day, so no direct competitor of theirs is in the building
- Two nominated guests
- A written summary of the day, publishable by both

## What they do not get, and must be told in the first message

- No attendee data. No list, no export, no contact details, no badge scanning, at any point, for anyone
- No view of the guest list before the day
- No stage time, no deck, no demo
- No approval over individual guests. They may nominate two. We compose
- No promised commercial outcome of any kind

**Saying this first is not a defence, it is the offer.** It is what makes the room worth being in, and a host who reads it early understands faster than one who has to be talked down from expecting a list.

## The three things that must be true in the first message

1. **Ask for a window, never a date.** Facilities say yes to two weeks and no to one Thursday
2. **State the nomination cap before they ask.** A host who expects to fill forty seats with their own invitees has bought a different product, and finding that out in week four costs the date
3. **Name one specific thing about their building or their mandate.** Without it the message is a mail merge, which is the exact objection this network is supposed to be immune to. See `ADR-015`

## Send-ready message

Subject lines, in order of preference. Plain, specific, no question marks:

```
[Facility name], one Thursday in November
A day of small rooms, in your building
Your November calendar, and a room of clinicians
```

```
[Name],

[ONE RESEARCHED SENTENCE. Their facility, their mandate, their new programme, their
reporting year. If this sentence could be sent to another organisation unchanged, it
is not finished.]

I run MedTech North, a small invitation network for Canadian health innovation. We
seat clinicians, researchers, founders and operators in the same rooms, kept small
enough that people actually talk. The third of these runs in Mississauga this month.

In November I want to run several of them in one building on one day. Six to eight
rooms, around thirty people each, each on a different question, each composed by hand.
Clinicians, researchers and students attend at no cost, permanently.

I am asking for the building, not for money. A Thursday inside a two week window in
November, whichever suits your calendar. We bring the programme, the production, the
catering and the certificate of insurance.

What you would have afterwards: your name alongside ours on the day, your team in the
rooms, and the interviews we film in your building. Practising clinicians and operators
talking about their own work, shot properly, cut for your channels as well as ours,
yours to use however you like.

Three things I want to be clear about now rather than later. You would not receive
attendee data of any kind, you would not see the guest list before the day, and nobody
gets stage time. You can nominate two guests. I compose the rooms.

If it is worth twenty minutes, I will come to you and walk the floor plan.

Ali
MedTech North
[registered business name, mailing address]

If you would rather I did not write again, reply with one word and I will not.
```

### Swap paragraph, university and college

Replace the "what you would have afterwards" paragraph with:

```
What you would have afterwards: your name alongside ours, your faculty and students in
the rooms with people who hire them, and the interviews we film on your campus.
Practising clinicians and operators talking about their own work, shot properly, cut
for your channels as well as ours. It is industry engagement that produced something
you can show, rather than something you have to describe.
```

### Swap paragraph, economic development and investment attraction

```
What you would have afterwards: your name alongside ours, and the interviews we film
in your building. Founders and clinicians on record about what is being built here and
what it takes to reach a patient. That is material aimed at exactly the audience you
are trying to attract, and it is difficult to produce any other way.
```

### Follow-up 1, day 4

```
[Name], one practical question rather than another pitch. Does [facility] have six
rooms that can each hold thirty, plus somewhere everyone can gather once. If the answer
is no, I will stop taking your time. If it is yes, I would like to walk it.
```

### Follow-up 2, day 10, only if follow-up 1 went unanswered

```
[Name], a different angle on the same ask. The part of this most useful to you is
probably not the day, it is what we film in your building: named clinicians and
operators on record about their own work, cut for your channels. That is a hard thing
to commission, because a crew is easy to book and the participants are not.

Still looking for a Thursday in November. If it is not you, is there someone at
[organisation] who would be the right person to ask.
```

### Follow-up 3, day 18, closing the loop

```
[Name], closing this out so it stops appearing in your inbox. If a day like this is
ever useful to [organisation], I will be running them through next year and I will
keep you posted rather than pitch you. If you can point me at one other building in
[city] that would suit, that would be the most useful reply I could get.
```

## Openers that were tried and rejected

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| "We are looking for a venue partner" | Partner is on the `CLAUDE.md` rule 2 list and reads as sponsorship. It also sounds like a rental enquiry, which is the fastest route to the wrong department |
| "An opportunity to reach 200 healthcare decision makers" | An attendee total we cannot claim, per rule 3, and it is exposure language on a segment that is not buying exposure |
| "Support Canadian medtech innovation" | Charity register. It also opens on the national interest argument, which belongs in the third paragraph, never the first. See `docs/19-host-incentives-and-the-media-floor.md` section 5 |
| "Limited dates available" | Urgency device, forbidden in `docs/07-brand-and-voice.md`, and transparently untrue when we are the ones asking |
| "Canada's leading health innovation network" | A claim with nothing behind it. Two rooms have been held |
| Opening with the media package | It is the consideration, not the ask. Leading with it makes the message sound like we are selling video production |
| "Would you be open to a quick chat" | Asks for a meeting, which tests nothing. Campaign 1 exists to find out whether they will give a building, so the message has to ask for a building |

## Guardrails applied

- No sponsorship, partner tier, anchor, exhibitor or booth language, per rule 2 and `ADR-007`
- No attendee totals, member counts, past-event photographs or logos, per rule 3
- No named reference until written permission is held, per `ADR-017`
- No figure that does not trace to `data/verified-stats.md`. There are no figures in the copy at all, which is the safest version
- Clinicians, researchers and students at no cost, stated as permanent and never as a launch rate, per `ADR-002`
- No regulated professional title used as a label, per rule 5
- No segment described as more valuable than another, per rule 6
- No implied accreditation or promised outcome, per rule 7 and `docs/08-legal-and-compliance.md`
- Sender identification, mailing address and a working opt-out in every message, per section 7 of `docs/20-outreach-campaign-sequence.md`
- No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no urgency devices

## Untested, and what would settle it

| Claim | How it gets settled |
|---|---|
| That media alone is sufficient consideration for a building | Campaign 1. A pattern of replies asking for a lead count first is the falsifier |
| That "I am asking for the building, not for money" prevents the rental-desk redirect | Count how many replies come from a bookings address rather than the named person |
| That the nomination cap survives first contact | Watch for it being renegotiated at the site visit. Every renegotiation attempt goes in the log |
| That a two week window converts better than a fixed date | Not worth an A/B at this volume. Revisit if campaign 1 stalls at the calendar stage |
| That six rooms of thirty is a realistic facility ask | The first three site visits answer this, and if it is consistently four rooms rather than six, the day shrinks and `ADR-025` gets amended |
| Whether the sample reel changes the reply rate | Compare the 20 sends before 2 September with the 40 after. Crude, but it is free |
