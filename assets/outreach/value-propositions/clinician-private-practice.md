# Segment value proposition: practising clinician, private or community practice

**Status:** draft, tested against one live conversation. Not generalised.
**Scope:** a regulated health professional in private or community practice, allied health or
dental or pharmacy, outside the GTA, approached directly and asked to take a directory
listing. Not employed by a hospital, not a researcher, not a founder, no prior relationship
with MedTech North.

Do not reuse this wording for a hospital clinician, an academic, a policy contact, a student
or a founder. Each of those hears a different question behind "what is this." See the
library README for how a new segment gets added.

---

## What this segment is actually asking

Three questions, in this order, usually unstated:

1. Am I about to be sold something.
2. Does this send me patients.
3. What does it cost me, in money and in time.

An answer that does not settle all three in the first message gets a polite no, or silence.

## The proposition

She is asked before the design is frozen, by the people who will hand her the tool in three
years, and it costs her nothing.

The directory is the mechanic that delivers it. The listing makes her findable by discipline,
so a founder or researcher working in her area can ask for an introduction, which she then
approves or declines.

## What she gets, in plain terms

- Findable by discipline, by people building in her area
- Introductions requested through her, approved by her
- Her profile carries what she speaks on and what she wants to be asked about
- Invitation to the rooms when composition calls for her discipline
- Nomination rights for colleagues
- No fee, in any year, permanently
- No pitching in either direction, and Chatham House in the room

## What she does not get, and must be told

- Patients. This is not a patient-facing listing and it does not generate referrals.
- Speaking slots, panels or events supplied by us. Her profile lists what she speaks on. It
  does not promise an invitation to speak.
- Any commercial outcome, credit, certification or endorsement.

## Send-ready message

> My work is getting Canadian health technology in front of clinicians earlier. Most
> companies here spend eighteen months on a device or an app and meet their first clinician
> at the point of sale, when the design is already frozen.
>
> The directory is how that gets fixed. Anyone building or researching in your area can find
> you by discipline, see what you actually work on, and ask for an introduction. You approve
> it or you don't. Your profile is also where you set out what you speak on and what you want
> to be asked about, so when a panel or a room needs your discipline, it points at you.
>
> No fee for the clinical side, in any year. Nobody pitches you. And it won't send you
> patients, that isn't what it's for.

### Longer variant, when she has asked a real question and the thread is unhurried

> Happy to keep it short.
>
> MedTech North is a network for the people who build health technology in Canada and the
> people who use it on patients. Most companies here design a device or an app for a year
> and a half, then meet their first clinician at the point of sale, when nothing can be
> changed anymore. I put those two groups in the same room earlier than that.
>
> On the directory, you would be listed by discipline, name and practice. That is how the
> network gets searched. When a founder or a researcher is working on something that touches
> your patients, they find you there and ask what would break in a real clinic day.
>
> What that gives you:
>
> - Your name in front of the people building the tools you will be handed in three years,
>   at the stage where what you say still changes them
> - An invitation to the rooms when composition calls for your discipline. No fee, in any
>   year, permanently. The clinical side never pays
> - The ability to nominate colleagues you would want at the table
> - Nobody sells to you. No pitching in either direction, and nothing said in the room
>   leaves it
>
> Two honest notes. This is not a patient-facing listing and it will not send you patients.
> And we are early: one room has been held, in the Toronto area, so being listed now means
> being findable before the network fills in.

## Openers that were tried and rejected

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| "My goal is to accelerate Canadian technology development" | A mission statement, not an answer. This segment has no stake in the abstraction. The concrete version, first clinician met at the point of sale, does the same work and lands. |
| "You can share your expertise and speaking engagements, panels and invite-only events" | Reads as a promise to supply speaking slots. Off one held room that is an outcome claim. Rewritten so the profile lists what she speaks on. |
| "Facilitate easier matchmaking between your expertise and the right opportunity" | Vague, restates the introduction mechanic already stated better, and "opportunity" reads as a job board. |

## Guardrails applied

- Free is permanent and a property of who she is. Never a discount, trial or launch rate.
- No member counts, testimonials or implied roster. One room, 16 people, stated plainly or
  not at all.
- No sponsorship, partner tier or exhibitor language in anything a member reads.
- No regulated titles used as labels.
- Rooms have run in Mississauga only. Never imply a room in her city.
- No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no urgency devices.

## Untested, and what would settle it

- Whether "it won't send you patients" reads as honest or as a reason to decline. Watch the
  reply rate on the next five sends before treating it as settled.
- Whether the introduction mechanic means anything to her before any introduction has
  actually been made through the directory.
- Whether an out-of-region clinician stays engaged with a listing when no room is reachable.
