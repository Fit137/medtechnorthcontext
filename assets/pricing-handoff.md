# MedTech North Membership Pricing

**One line:** Four tiers on a sliding scale, free forever for the people who create the room's value and priced by scope for the people who capture it, sold per member per year in Canadian dollars.

---

## Strategic rationale

**Who pays and who does not.** The free tier exists because clinicians, researchers, academics, students and policy leaders are the reason every other tier is worth buying. They give feedback, early adoption, beta testing and their evenings. Charging them, even a token amount, would remove the exact signal the paid tiers are paying for. Guest is free permanently, in every phase, and that is a design rule rather than a launch promotion.

**Denominator: per member, per year.** Value arrives as seats in rooms, and rooms run on an annual cadence of four quarterly dinners plus a summit. Pricing per event would have priced attendance and destroyed the belonging story. Annual also matches the composition planning cycle, so a member's renewal date and their seat allocation move together.

**Basis: value-based, floored by event cost.** Prices are set from the value of guaranteed proximity plus a redeemable rate card, then checked against the real cost of a dinner seat. The Attending tier captures 19.4 percent of the quantified annual value it delivers, which sits in the healthy 10 to 30 percent band and leaves four fifths of the surplus with the member.

**Why Attending is the Hero.** Resident at $290 is deliberately close to what four dinner tickets cost at the door, so joining is cheaper than attending. Attending at $790 is Resident plus $500, and the summit pass alone is $395 of that, with two out-of-province dinners ($140) and the exhibitor rate on top. The upgrade math does the selling. Chief at $2,950 is 3.7 times the Hero and anchors the ladder so Attending reads as reasonable.

**Deviation to watch.** The standard split is entry 5 to 20 percent, hero 60 to 80, anchor 5 to 20. The planning split here is Resident 45, Attending 40, Chief 15, because in an events business the entry tier carries real recurring value rather than being a stripped software plan. If Resident exceeds 60 percent of paid members after two quarters, Attending is underbuilt, not overpriced.

---

## The headline finding

At typical redemption with **unsponsored** dinners, the cost to serve an Attending member is $442 and the 60 percent margin floor is **$1,105**. The Hero price is $790, which sits below that floor.

With **sponsored or partner-hosted** dinners, the cost to serve drops to $210 and the floor to **$525**. The Hero then clears at 73.4 percent margin.

The operating rule that follows: **no dinner goes on the calendar without either a per-dinner sponsor at $3,000 to $7,500 or a donated partner venue.** Membership revenue is margin on top of a sponsor-funded programme, never the thing that pays for the food. At launch volumes the sponsorship needed per dinner is $3,200, which lands inside the band already in the business model.

---

## Tier names

**Recommended: Guest, Resident, Attending, Chief.** It borrows the hospital career ladder, so every clinician reads the hierarchy instantly, and Attending is a genuine double meaning: the senior clinical rank and the person who attends. Confirm with three clinicians first that a founder holding the title Attending does not read as appropriation.

Fallbacks: **Guest, Seat, Table, Host** (sits on the brand language already used across the site and the keynote) or **Guest, Chapter, National, Founding Circle** (purely descriptive). Keep Guest in all three. It is the most important word on the page because it frames free as hosted status rather than a discount.

## Savings scenarios for the page

| Scenario | The public way | As a member | Saves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toronto bootstrapped founder, **Resident**: four Toronto dinners plus the summit | $675 | $485 ($290 + $195 summit) | **$190, 28%** |
| Montreal founder exhibiting, **Attending**: four Montreal dinners, two Toronto dinners, summit, one booth | $3,315 | $2,240 ($790 + $1,450 booth) | **$1,075, 32%** |
| Vancouver scale-up, **Chief**: eight dinners nationally, both summits, booths in both cities | $6,350 | $3,850 ($2,950 + $900 second booth) | **$2,500, 39%** |
| Toronto physician, **Guest**: two dinners as an invited guest plus the summit | $535 | $0 | **$535, 100%** |

The cleanest line for the Resident block: **four dinner tickets at the door cost $280. Membership is $290 and includes all four, plus the cohorts, the directory, the introductions, the partner benefits and a member rate on everything else.** You pay roughly the price of the dinners you were already attending and the rest of the network comes free.

The line for the Guest block: **you will never be charged, in any phase, permanently. Your presence is what the room is built around.**

## Perks worth adding that were not on the original list

Fourteen of the thirty perks in the model cost nothing marginal to deliver, which is how the value stack reaches four to five times the fee without eroding margin.

The strongest is the **partner benefits bundle**: cloud credits, an hour of IP or regulatory counsel, a banking offer, negotiated as part of category partnerships. It costs nothing and can exceed the membership fee on its own. Assumption to validate: $2,000 of stated annual value per member.

Others: seat-preference requests (tell us who you need to meet, and we seat you accordingly, which costs only labour and is the single most differentiating perk in the stack), a physical member card presented at the table as a ritual object, guest passes so members bring colleagues into the room as an acquisition mechanic, cohort moderator status for Attending and above as free recognition, co-hosting rights and summit programme input at Chief, and a founding member price lock for the first 100.

## Terms the page must carry

- **Composition clause.** Every seat at every tier, including Chief, is subject to the balance of the room. If a dinner needs clinicians in the remaining seats, no membership tier overrides that. This protects the product and naturally caps redemption.
- **Monthly guard.** A monthly member becomes eligible for an included dinner seat after 60 days of continuous membership, or pays the $35 member rate before that. Without this, someone joins for $29, takes a $70 seat and cancels.
- **Guest eligibility.** No commercial product or service being sold. Reviewed at application, not self-declared. A Guest who founds a company or joins a vendor moves to a paid tier at renewal with a 90 day grace period.
- **Refunds and transfers.** Non-refundable, but a dinner seat transfers to a colleague free with 72 hours notice. Transfers keep rooms full and cost nothing.
- **No-shows.** Two unattended reserved seats in a year moves a member to the waitlist for the following quarter.
- **Tax.** Register for a GST/HST number before the first membership is sold. The $30,000 small supplier threshold is crossed in year one. Quebec requires separate QST registration. Confirm the treatment of memberships versus event admissions with a Canadian accountant; this model is not tax advice.

## Notes for design

- Highlight the **Attending** column. Flag it "Most popular" and give it the raised card treatment.
- The **Guest** column is first, not last, and should not read as the cheap option. Give it equal visual weight and let the copy carry the status ("Always our guests").
- Billing toggle: monthly and annual, with annual showing "2 months free" (the discount is 17 percent at both Resident and Attending).
- Chief uses "Talk to us" rather than a checkout button, since it is a conversation tier.
- Show the founding rate as a banner above the table with a live counter of seats remaining out of 100, not as a fourth price row.
- Put the savings scenarios directly under the table. The Resident scenario is the one that converts.
- Assumptions a founder should confirm before this goes live: the $2,000 partner benefits value, the market band figures (chamber $400, association $950, peer network $3,500), the summit cost of $200 per attendee, and the blended dinner seat cost of $80 once venues are booked.
