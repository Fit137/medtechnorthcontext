# ADR-018: No summit in year one. Run a day of rooms instead

**Status:** Accepted

## The numbers

`docs/06-financial-model.md` assumes a summit of **380 attendees** producing $90,000. That figure is a stretch target, not a base case, and the risk register already flags summit revenue as the weakest line in the model.

**Realistic first summit: 120 to 200 attendees.** 250 with two or three strong distribution partners.

Anchors:
- **Toronto Health Innovation Week draws 1,000+ in total** ✅, across a full week of interconnected events including MaRS Impact Health, the Bloom Burton Health Investment Conference and the BioLabs UofT opening. That is the combined output of several established organisations. One first-time day by a solo operator is a fraction of it.
- **Free in-person events run 40 to 60% no-show. Paid events run 10 to 30%** ✅.
- MedTech North has held two rooms, the first with 16 people. There is no list.

## Why not a summit

| Reason | Detail |
|---|---|
| **It attacks our own position** | `data/competitors.md` places us as small, balanced, neutral and provable, against event companies whose scale compromises curation. A summit with exhibitors and a stage is their format, entered with none of their assets |
| **Our own scoring already rejected it** | The 50-person promotional briefing scored **6.30, lowest of every corporate product** in `docs/05-monetization.md`, because large promotional formats are where trust erodes fastest |
| **The language is banned** | Exhibitor and booth are on the `CLAUDE.md` rule 2 list |
| **Cash risk is inverted** | Venue deposit, AV and catering minimums are all paid before a ticket sells. Roughly $50,000 to $70,000 committed up front at 150 attendees. This is the standard way event businesses die |
| **We cannot charge the scarce side** | Rule 1. Clinicians attend free, so the clinical half of the room carries free-event no-show economics, and a thin room in front of paying exhibitors is worse than no room |
| **No insurance, no incorporation** | Both required before a venue contract |

## Decision

**Run a day of rooms.** Six to eight composed rooms in one venue on one day, each a different specialty, each with its own funder. Attendance of 150 to 250 across the day, but no single room over 35.

| | Summit | Day of rooms |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | One ticket line plus exhibitors | 6 to 8 funded rooms at $7.5K to $15K each, so $45K to $120K |
| Downside if it underfills | Fixed costs already spent | Sell four rooms, run four |
| Contradiction with the moat | Direct | None. No room exceeds 35 |
| Exhibitors | Needed | None. A funder co-hosts a room instead of renting a table |
| Speakers on stage | The product | Facilitators inside rooms |

Same scale in the pitch, same revenue, a fraction of the risk, and it is the format that has already worked twice.

## What must be true before a real summit is reconsidered

1. Six or more rooms run, so an actual list exists
2. Three or more paying funders willing to act as references
3. Liability insurance in force and the entity incorporated
4. Someone other than the founder running operations
5. A funnel table from at least three rooms, so attendance can be forecast rather than hoped for

Revisit at the end of year one against those five, not against ambition.
