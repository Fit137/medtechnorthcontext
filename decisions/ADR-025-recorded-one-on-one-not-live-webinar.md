# ADR-025 — The interview series is recorded one-on-one, not a live webinar

**Status:** Proposed. Awaiting Ali's confirmation. Analysis in `docs/18-interview-series-strategy.md`.

## Context

An online interview series was proposed, released as video, with two stated objectives: an audience that actually watches, and guests who require no chasing. The open question was live webinar versus recorded one-on-one, and which ecosystem segment supplies guests who are eager, welcoming, unpaid and fast to confirm.

MedTech North has no publishable audience. Two rooms have been held, no member counts exist, and `CLAUDE.md` rule 3 forbids anything that reads as a track record. Every view in year one therefore arrives through a guest's own following.

## Decision

**Season one is recorded one-on-one, released as video. No live webinar.**

Three structural reasons, none of them about production quality.

1. **A live session has a visible failure mode.** Free-attendance events here run 40 to 60% no-show, which is why the guarantee ladder guarantees half of a stated target. A thin live room is witnessed by the one person whose opinion of the property matters most, and in a small sector that is unrecoverable.
2. **A live session displays a track record that does not exist.** Registration counts and session archives are exactly what rule 3 forbids. A recording makes no headcount claim, so the rule costs nothing.
3. **A recording cannot fail in public.** A weak interview is never released. A weak webinar has already happened.

**Recorded roundtables of two or three guests enter at month three**, once a back catalogue exists to show a prospective guest. That format scores second and carries the highest repurposing yield in the set. It is sequenced, not skipped.

## Guest selection

**Practice owners lead season one:** community pharmacy owners and dental practice owners first, physiotherapy and rehabilitation clinic owners next. They score highest because they are the only segment where the four convenience requirements coincide with authority and with an audience corporate buyers already pay to reach.

**Ecosystem support organisations are a distribution partner, not the guest.** They carry the highest self-distribution score in the map and they are also listed as competitors in `data/competitors.md`.

**No season is built on physicians.** Zero have attended either room, three accepted for room 002 and no-showed, two more withdrew for structural reasons. `ADR-012` settled the equivalent question for events.

## The premise that was wrong

That eagerness identifies a good guest. It identifies the opposite. Service providers, seed-stage founders and students score a perfect 10 on eagerness, on appearing for free and on requiring no chasing, and all three sit in the bottom half of the map. Needing exposure is the inverse of having an audience. The four convenience requirements are kept, and satisfied inside a segment that also carries authority, rather than used as the selection criterion on their own.

## Carried from ADR-023 without change

- **The scarce side receives, the abundant side works and pays.** The ask is a produced feature the guest owns outright, not a request for their time
- **The closing question is identical for every guest, in every episode.** One person answering twenty questions is an interview. Twenty people answering the same question is a format that compounds and yields a monthly compilation from footage already shot
- Guests own their footage. Any MedTech North use needs separate written permission
- Every interviewee approves their cut. No patient information in any frame. Per-college advertising rules checked before the first shoot

## Consequences

- Filming the 8 to 12 clinician interviews already required for the insight brief (`HANDOFF.md` next action 10) produces the brief, a gift for the guest and a publishable asset from one conversation
- Views are not the success metric. Named target companies reached, guests who post unprompted, nominations per episode and interviews converted to room attendance or a corporate conversation are
- Publishing guest footage on the MedTech North site is **not** authorised by this ADR. Rule 3 currently forbids it and reversing that needs its own record

## Open, and deliberately not decided here

Whether founders appear on camera at all. `ADR-023` gives them no stage time in the room, and airtime online while denied it in the room makes a public rule look negotiable.
