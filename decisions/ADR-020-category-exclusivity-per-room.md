# ADR-020: Every funded room is category-exclusive, and we enforce it

**Status:** Accepted

## The defect this fixes
`ADR-019` created a paid service provider seat at $350 without saying what a funder gets that a seat buyer does not. That made the funder fee look like a 21x markup on the same access. It also meant a co-host could pay $25,000, open their own building, and watch a competitor walk in for $350.

Both are real. Neither is acceptable.

## Decision

**No direct competitor of the funder or co-host is admitted to that room, at any price.**

The rule is ours and we apply it. The funder does not see names, does not approve individuals, and does not curate. We run a conflict check the way a law firm does, on category, before any commercial seat is confirmed.

**A seat buys attendance. The funder fee buys the category for that room.** A seat can be refused outright if the category is taken, and the money is returned.

## Why this is the right premium
Attendance is a commodity. Anyone can buy it, so it is priced like a commodity. Category exclusivity is the only thing in the room that exactly one company can hold, which makes it the only thing that can carry a premium. Everything else a funder receives (shaping the question, the composition report, the welcome, consented introductions, first refusal) is additive. **Exclusivity is the reason the price is different in kind rather than in degree.**

## Price change
Service provider seats move from $350 to **$500**. They are the highest-extraction non-funder seat and $350 underpriced them. It also widens the gap to the funder fee, which is the confusion this ADR exists to remove.

## Two operational leaks to close
1. **Hide the guest list.** Luma shows attendees to each other by default. Turn it off. Registration is by invitation with the list hidden. Otherwise a $500 seat buys the attendee list we tell funders nobody gets
2. **The never list is about what we provide, not what happens.** We never supply a list, an export or contact data, to anyone, ever. We do not pretend to stop people talking in a room. Say it that way, because the honest version survives scrutiny and the overclaim does not

## Enforcement on conduct
Pitching from the floor or working the room ends the invitation permanently. A four-seat cap and a curated invitation make that a real cost. This protects the funder as much as the clinicians.

## What this does not change
The co-host still never sees the guest list before the room, never approves individuals, and never receives attendee data. Category exclusion is a rule we enforce, not a favour we grant, and it must never become a route to curation.
