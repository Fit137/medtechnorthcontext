## Entity structure

**Current state:** SaaS Relaunch exists with an Ontario business name, a BIN, and a GST/HST number. **Unverified whether it is a sole proprietorship or a corporation.** Check for articles of incorporation. Everything below depends on which.

### Can MedTech North run under the existing entity?

**Technically yes if sole proprietorship.** A GST/HST number attaches to the legal person, not the trade name. Every registered business name sits on top of the same person, so the existing number covers any activity invoiced under any of them.

An invoice reading **MedTech North**, address, existing GST/HST registration number, date, purchaser name, description, total and HST amount is compliant. A client can claim their input tax credit from it. **Cost to get there: $60.**

### The lines that force incorporation

| Line | Why |
|---|---|
| **Liability** | 25 to 50 people in rooms with catering in venues you do not own. A claim reaches personal assets. This is the real gap |
| **Corporate procurement** | A company paying $20K–100K runs vendor onboarding. Some processes require a corporation |
| **Brand separation** | Invoices, contracts and bank details leak the entity name to sponsors and clinicians |
| **Outside money** | Investment, co-founder, chapter host with a stake. None works in a sole proprietorship |
| **Tax** | At projected revenue, incorporation likely wins via the small business deduction |

**Timing:** run the first two or three free rooms under a registered business name. Incorporate before whichever comes first: signing any sponsorship or corporate services agreement, running an event in an external venue, or collecting money at any scale. Realistically month two or three.

## Minimum viable set

1. **Register "MedTech North" as a business name.** $60. Do this now regardless of the incorporation decision
2. **GST/HST.** Existing number covers a sole prop trade name. Incorporating creates a new legal person needing its own account
3. **Separate business bank account**
4. **Commercial General Liability, $2M–5M.** ⚠️ **The one item that cannot be skipped.** External venues demand a certificate, corporate procurement demands it, and it is the answer if the condo board asks questions
5. **Invoice template** carrying the GST/HST registration number. Corporate AP bounces invoices without it
6. **Ticket and registration terms:** refunds, transfers, no-shows, code of conduct, composition clause
7. **Privacy policy and PIPEDA-compliant consent flow.** Already collecting personal data through the directory
8. **Bookkeeping from day one.** Vendor, date, amount, HST, purpose on every receipt

## The condo amenity question

**Assumption, unverified:** the building prohibits commercial use of the amenity.

**Gating attendance on paid subscription makes this worse, not better.** The test is not whether money changed hands on site. It is whether the amenity functions as an input to a business. Making room access something people paid for is the clearest possible evidence of commercial use.

**Second exposure:** if membership promises access to rooms held in a space held under personal-use rights, and the board revokes privileges, money has been taken for something undeliverable.

**The risk that actually costs money is insurance.** If someone is injured, the building's policy may decline for unpermitted commercial use, and personal home insurance excludes business activity.

**Also:** running a business from a residential address falls under municipal home occupation rules, which typically restrict client visits and traffic.

### Actions

1. **Read the declaration, rules and amenity booking agreement.** Currently working from assumption
2. **Request written permission** from the property manager. Describe accurately: private professional gatherings, no admission charged, no sales on site, resident-hosted. Offer a certificate of insurance. Asking in month one from transparency is a different conversation from explaining in month four after a complaint
3. **Never make the venue identifiable in published media.** No exterior shots, no building name, no address in any listing. Location is "Mississauga" or "Square One District". Address sent privately after RSVP
4. **Get CGL in force before the next event**

**Delivery catering you arrange yourself is safer than a staffed catering crew.** Vendors with staff on site is where "resident gathering" starts looking like "event business." If alcohol is ever served, check whether a Special Occasion Permit applies.

**The problem mostly solves itself:** the first two or three rooms are free and ungated. Gating only arises from month four, which is when external venues arrive anyway.

## Claims that must never appear

- **Regulated titles for non-holders:** Resident, Attending, Chief, Fellow, Chair, Registrar, Consultant, Specialist, Practitioner, Associate
- Implying MedTech North is a professional association, accreditor or continuing education provider
- Suggesting attendance confers professional credit, accreditation or certification
- Any clinical, medical, diagnostic or product efficacy claim
- Promising a commercial outcome: securing funding, closing deals, winning contracts, gaining hospital adoption
- Implying clinicians, institutions or regulators endorse any company or MedTech North
- "Accredited", "certified", "approved", "official" about MedTech North or its members
- Anything about member data conflicting with PIPEDA consent requirements

**Careful words:** "verified" and "vetted" are fine describing your own review process. Not fine in any way implying third-party or regulatory validation.

## Honoraria mechanics

- Fees for services generally trigger a **T4A obligation above $500 per recipient per year**
- Many physicians have professional corporations, which is cleaner because they invoice you
- **Ask each participant which applies before paying anyone**
- Collect proper invoices from vendors with legal name, address and GST/HST number. Screenshots will not survive an audit
