# PART A. The frame

## What the night has to do

Every attendee takes the stage for five minutes and answers three questions in the same order:

1. What are you working on.
2. Why did you choose Canada for it.
3. What is the one word you want the rest of the room to hear about building here.

Attendees are not all founders. Professors, academics, consultants, managers and corporate operators are in the room, and the theme is building in the general sense: what you are making, and why you are making it here. A research programme, a hospital service line, a curriculum, a clinic, a fund and a company all qualify.

The deck is one file, one slide per person, plus an opening frame and a closing wall.

## The single structural idea

**Slide 1 is the legend for every slide that follows.** The title slide places the three questions as three labels in the exact positions they will occupy on every attendee slide. The room learns the template once, then reads twenty slides fluently without being told again. Nobody has to explain the format twice, and no speaker has to remember the order, because the order is on the wall behind them.

This is what makes the deck do work rather than decorate the night.

## Five rules the deck enforces, and what each one is for

**1. Every slide is the same slide.** Identical geometry, identical type sizes, identical space. The professor's slide and the pre-seed founder's slide are structurally indistinguishable. `CLAUDE.md` rule 6 forbids ranking one segment above another, and `ADR-023` already rejected featuring some guests and not others on the grounds that it splits a room into featured and attending and is unrecoverable once noticed. The deck is where that split would happen first. So: no bigger slide for a bigger name, no extra slide for anyone, no exceptions.

**2. No logos, for anyone.** A logo slide favours whoever has a design team. A corporate mark next to a PhD student's plain name creates the hierarchy rule 1 exists to prevent. Organisations appear as typeset words at the same size for everyone.

**3. Three filled fields, no more.** The slide carries the person's name and title, one line on what they are working on, and their word. "Why Canada" is a label with nothing under it, deliberately, because that answer is the reason they are standing up. If it is printed, the audience reads it and the speaker becomes redundant.

**4. The slide is the anti-pitch device.** Five minutes with a microphone is an invitation to pitch, particularly for founders, and the house rules are that nobody buys a microphone and no one here is anyone's prospect. A slide with three visible fields and no room for a product shot makes departing from the format obvious to everyone watching, including the speaker. No ask from the stage, no raise announcement, no pricing, no demo, no roadmap. Say this once at the top of the night and then let the slides hold the line.

**5. Order carries no status.** Print the running order, and print the rule that produced it, for example alphabetical by first name. An unlabelled order looks curated, and a curated order ranks people. A visibly arbitrary one does not. Interleave segments rather than grouping them, which also stops the night ending on a block of students at the point when the room is tired.

## Running time, which is the real risk

Five minutes each plus roughly thirty seconds of handover is the honest arithmetic. Total stage time above 75 minutes loses an audience that has already worked a full day, and no single block should run past 25 minutes.

| Attendees speaking | At 5 min each | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 12 | 55 to 66 min | Five minutes. Two blocks, one break |
| 13 to 16 | 77 to 88 min | Five minutes in three blocks, or four minutes in two |
| 17 to 22 | 93 to 121 min | Four minutes, three blocks |
| 23 or more | Not viable | Three minutes, stated plainly at the top |

Decide the length once the list is final, then tell Claude Design the number, because the on-slide timer is built from it. Do not decide it on the night: a five-minute slot announced and then privately cut to three is the one thing that makes a speaker feel demoted.

## Slide architecture

| # | Slide | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-roll | Holding slide, loops as people arrive | The three questions, on the wall, before anyone is called. People rehearse without being asked to |
| 1 | Build in Canada | Title, date, venue, and the three labels in their permanent positions |
| 2 | Why here, in figures | The case for Canada, verified figures only, sourced on the slide |
| 3 | How this runs | Three questions, the slot length, four house rules, the on-the-record line |
| 4 | Running order | Names and the rule that ordered them |
| 5..N | One per attendee | Identical geometry. Name, title, organisation, one line on the work, the word |
| Blocks | Break slide between blocks | Carries the next three names so nobody wanders off before their slot |
| N+1 | The word wall | Every word from every person, one composition |
| N+2 | The ask | One line, in red. The only red on a content slide |
| N+3 | The word wall again | Held on screen for the rest of the evening |

The person slides carry no visible numbering. A no-show then gets skipped in silence rather than leaving an obvious gap, which matters: three physicians accepted for room 002 and none arrived.

## Anatomy of a person slide

```
  MEDTECH NORTH                                    [ marker ]

  FIRSTNAME LASTNAME
  Title, verbatim · Organisation, verbatim
  ────────────────────────────────── the seam ──────────────
  WORKING ON        One line, written by you, twelve words at most

  WHY CANADA        (label only, nothing under it)

  THE WORD          ONE WORD, set large

  ·  ·  ·  ·  ·                                   five minutes
```

**The marker** is one of the four brand group markers, drawn as SVG, same size and same position on every slide: gear for founders and operators, medical cross for clinicians, researchers and academics, rising bars for capital and corporate, columns for policy and public sector. Shape only, never colour, which is both the accessibility rule and the brand rule. It tells the room what kind of building this is without ranking it.

**Titles are verbatim.** `CLAUDE.md` rule 5 forbids Resident, Attending, Chief, Fellow, Chair, Registrar, Consultant, Specialist, Practitioner and Associate as labels we assign. Use what each person wrote for themselves. If their real title contains one of those words because they hold it, it is their title and it stands. Never invent one, never level anyone up or down, never write "Consultant" as a category for someone who called themselves something else.

**The five dots** are the timer, sitting on the seam, one per minute, filling as the slot runs. A countdown clock is an urgency device and the brand does not use those. Dots on the seam are a property of the format. The last dot fills red, which is the only honest use of the ask colour here: at minute five the ask is the stage back.

## The word, and why it is the point

The word is the artifact of the evening. It is the only thing the night produces that can be photographed, posted and attributed without touching the track record rule, because it is the attendees' words about Canada rather than any claim about MedTech North. No headcount is visible on the wall. No caption counts the contributors. Composition over headcount holds even here.

Collect words two ways. Pre-collect tonight from whoever replies, which is what Part C is for, and type the rest in live: the deck's word fields are editable, and the wall reads from the slides, so typing a word on a person's slide puts it on the wall automatically with no second pass.

## One decision to make before you open the doors

The stage segment and the rest of the evening cannot both be Chatham House. "Nothing leaves the table" is a house rule, and a word wall posted afterwards breaks it unless the stage segment is explicitly carved out.

Say this at the top, in one sentence: the five-minute slots are on the record and may be photographed or filmed, everything after is not, and anyone who would rather not be recorded should say so and will not be. Then the wall is publishable and the room is still protected. Handle it in the message tonight as well, so nobody is asked to decide with a camera already pointed at them.

## What must not appear anywhere in this deck

- Attendee counts, room numbers, "our third room", totals across events, any past-event photograph or gallery
- The words sponsor, sponsorship, partner tier, anchor, exhibitor, underwriter, booth. A paying company is a co-host, and not on a slide unless one exists and has agreed to it
- Miro, by name or mark. Written permission is not held. See `ADR-017`
- The four public LinkedIn testimonials. Permission to quote has not been requested
- The ~70% outreach reply rate. Anecdote grade until it is reconstructed from send records
- Any figure that is not in `data/verified-stats.md`
- The 76% procurement figure. It is from 2016, it carries an age warning, and this deck does not need it

## What this deck is for

A single HTML deck for a live event on Wednesday 30 September 2026. Every attendee takes the stage for {{SLOT_MINUTES}} minutes and answers three questions in the same order. Each attendee has exactly one slide.

Build the skeleton and slide 1 now. Per-person content arrives separately as a list. The deck must accept it by duplicating one commented template block, with no restyling.

**Match the MedTech North partner deck in this project. Treat slide 1 of that deck as the reference surface: same palette, same type tokens, same restraint.** If anything here conflicts with the project's design system, the design system wins.

## Format and delivery

- Each slide is one `<section class="slide">` at 1920 x 1080. One HTML file, presented full screen from a browser
- **It must run with no network.** No CDN, no remote fonts, no external requests. It will be presented from a laptop on a venue projector
- Keyboard navigation: right arrow and space advance, left arrow goes back, `Home` returns to slide 1, `F` toggles full screen
- A print stylesheet that exports one slide per landscape page, as a PDF fallback for the night
- Type and colour come from tokens. No raw hex and no raw px in slide code

## Writing rules, absolute, no exceptions

- **No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere**, including in ranges. Write "four to six", never with a dash. Commas, colons, full stops
- No exclamation marks. No urgency devices, no countdowns, no "spots", no "don't miss"
- No second-person hype. No rhetorical question openers
- Banned words: leverage, unlock, synergy, seamless, streamline, empower, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionize, best-in-class, robust, elevate, transform, landscape, foster, delve, testament, navigate used figuratively, realm
- Register is executive. Write as you would to a hospital chief of staff
- Never imply professional credit, accreditation, endorsement or any promised commercial result
- Do not write the words sponsor, sponsorship, partner tier, anchor, exhibitor, underwriter or booth anywhere in this file
- Do not state or imply how many people are in the room, how many rooms have been held, or any total across events

## Colour discipline, and it is tighter than usual

Red `#c41230` is the ask, and in a deck there is nothing to click, so red marks the one ask of the night and nothing else.

- Red appears on exactly two content slides: the ask line on slide 1, and the closing ask slide
- Red appears nowhere on any person slide, except the final timer dot
- Everything else is ink and the gray family. The system is cool. No cream, no beige, no warm neutrals
- One emphasis per surface, maximum

Twenty near-identical ink slides followed by one red line is the argument. Restraint is doing the work.

## The seam

The brand mark is an up-triangle and a down-triangle meeting at a line, and that seam is the structural rule of the system. In this deck the seam sits at the same vertical position on every single slide: under the title on slide 1, under the name on every person slide, and it carries the timer dots. It is the one element that must not move by a pixel between slides, because the whole deck reads as one object only if it holds.

## Craft, absolute

No emoji. No icon fonts. No stock icon libraries. No stock photography. No photographs of people at all. No CSS blur. No gradients. No drop shadows. Nothing left at a default size. All illustration drawn as SVG. Entry animations 300 to 500 ms, objects hold when they arrive, nothing loops or pulses.
