## Slide 3 — The model in one picture

**Title:** How the money actually works

**Diagram labels (four flows into one table):**
- Guests flow in free — clinicians, researchers, academics, students, policy
- Members pay to be near them — founders, operators
- Sponsors pay for the composition — one partner per category
- The programme funds itself — sponsorship covers the room

**Two call-out figures anchored to the diagram:**
- Membership: **29%** of revenue
- Sponsorship: **50%** of revenue

**Bottom line:** Membership qualifies the room. Sponsorship monetises it. Neither works without the free tier at the centre.

## Slide 5 — What Finding 1 means

**Title:** Three consequences, and none of them is a pricing problem

**Three statements, generous spacing, no chart:**

1. **Growth comes from cities and cadence, never from selling harder into a full chapter.** A chapter at its ceiling is done, and that is by design.

2. **Membership can never be the main revenue line.** A 40-seat room that gives away half its seats caps out. This is exactly why the sponsorship architecture exists.

3. **A waitlist is a feature.** It is the scarcity proof that prices the sponsorship. An oversubscribed room is worth more to a partner than an open one.

**Title:** LTV, and why you should not trust it yet

| Tier | Annual contribution | Retention (assumed) | Lifetime | LTV | CAC | LTV:CAC | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resident | $220 | 65% | 2.9 yrs | $629 | $80 | 7.9x | 4.4 mo |
| Attending | $580 | 75% | 4.0 yrs | $2,320 | $250 | 9.3x | 5.2 mo |

**Caveat (same type weight as the table, not a footnote):** Retention carries no history. Every LTV here is a projection until two renewal cycles have run. ## Slide 13 — The risk register

**Title:** What we know is soft

**Four High-severity risks (most prominent), plus the register:**

| Risk | What breaks | Severity | De-risk before it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retention is unproven | Every LTV rests on assumed renewal | High | Run two renewal cycles; report actuals |
| Summit revenue is the weakest line | $90K–$110K on one blended assumption | High | Rebuild seat by seat before booking a venue |
| Sponsor attach rate | Needs 80% of dinners sponsored by year 2 | High | Sell four dinner sponsors before scaling |
| Capacity misread as a growth problem | Selling into a full chapter kills composition | High | Hold the ceiling; add cities, not seats |
| Guest supply | If guests don't come, members leave | High | Track guest:payer ratio; pause paid acquisition below 1.2:1 |
| Founder concentration | One person does curation, seating, selling | High | Chapter hosts by year 2; document the method |
| Category partner ladder | Year 3 assumes 5 partners; 1 exists in plan | Medium | Close one, publish its report, sell the next |
| Venue cost inflation | Restaurant-only pushes seat cost to $105 | Medium | Two venue partners per chapter before it opens |
| Tax and registration | GST/HST threshold crossed in year 1 | Medium | Register before the first membership is sold |
| Price is too low | LTV:CAC above 7 on every tier | Low | Hold through year 1, test Attending up at renewal |

**Bottom line:** The four High-severity risks share one root: nothing here has happened yet. Presented that way, the model is credible.

## Slide 14 — Assumption register

**Title:** Ranked by how much damage a wrong answer does

| Assumption | Current | If wrong by | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor attach rate | 80% by year 2 | Down to 50% | Dinner programme swings to a $24,000 cost |
| Partner-hosted share | 50% | Down to 25% | Blended seat cost rises $80 → $92.50 |
| Summit ticket and booth revenue | $90,000 (yr 2) | Down 40% | $36,000 straight off net contribution |
| Resident retention | 65% | Down to 40% | LTV falls $629 → $367 |
| Average dinners attended | 2.3 of 4 | Up to 3.2 | Chapter ceiling falls 35 → 25 members |
| Blended CAC | $220 | Up to $400 | $17,000 additional cost in year 2 |

**The counter-intuitive one, called out:** Higher attendance *lowers* the chapter ceiling, because engaged members consume more of a fixed seat supply. Success on engagement is a constraint on membership growth, not relief from it.

## Slide 15 — What we build next

**Title:** Turning this into a full financial model

**Five items:**

1. **Monthly cohort engine.** Members join on rolling dates; annual memberships create deferred revenue. Model cohorts by join month with per-cohort retention.
2. **Cash timing.** Sponsorship arrives in lumps, dinner costs are paid on the night, memberships are collected up front. Build a 13-week cash view.
3. **Seat-by-seat summit build.** Replace the single blended line with pass types, booth tiers and sponsor cheques.
4. **Chapter unit model.** Make one chapter a repeatable P&L so expansion is modelled as adding units.
5. **Actuals capture.** After dinner one, replace assumed seat cost, guest-to-payer ratio and attendance with measured figures.

**Bottom line:** Three dinners of real data will move this model more than any amount of further modelling.
