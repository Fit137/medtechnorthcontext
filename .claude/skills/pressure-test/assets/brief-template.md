# Pressure Test Brief

**What this is.** The starting point for a pressure test: a short document describing what the business is, what it rests on, and what has actually happened. It exists so the interview can start from real gaps rather than from orientation questions the founder has answered a hundred times.

**It is allowed to be bad.** Three honest lines beat two polished pages. Blanks are useful, because a blank says where the interview should go. If there is no brief at all, the interview will produce one, and that version is usually better than a brief written alone.

**The one rule:** section 4 holds only what has happened, with dates and numbers. Intentions go in section 5. Almost every weak pressure test traces back to a brief where those two were mixed.

Delete this preamble when filling it in.

---

## 1. What the business does

Five lines, plain, no pitch language. What it is, who it is for, what changes for them.

## 2. The transaction

Who hands over money. How much. How often. What they get for it. If nobody has yet, say that here.

## 3. The core claim

One or two sentences in the form: **we believe X, therefore Y.**

Example: *We believe mid-size distributors cannot get accurate delivery estimates from their carriers, therefore they will pay for a layer that produces them.*

## 4. What has actually happened

Facts with dates and numbers. Money received, contracts signed, users active, deliveries completed, people hired. Where a number came from a model rather than a measurement, mark it.

Write "nothing yet" if that is the answer. It is a completely normal answer and it changes what gets tested, not whether the exercise is worth doing.

## 5. What is assumed

The founder's own list, before anyone else's. What has to be true for the plan to work that has not been demonstrated. Three to ten lines.

## 6. What is already decided

Decisions that are settled and not up for debate: market, model, pricing structure, things tried and rejected. This stops the exercise relitigating closed questions and spending attention where it cannot change anything.

## 7. Constraints

Runway in months. Time available. People. Money. Anything non-negotiable, including personal constraints.

## 8. The decision this should inform

What will actually change based on the output. What are you choosing between right now.

If the honest answer is "nothing specific, I want to know what I am missing", write that. It is a legitimate reason to run this and it changes how the register is ordered.
