The structure

How the money actually works

Guests flow in free
Clinicians, researchers, academics, students, policy. The gravity.
Members pay to be near them
Founders and operators. Membership is 29% of revenue.
The room
40 seats, composed and vetted
Sponsors pay for composition
One partner per category. Sponsorship is 50% of revenue.
The programme funds itself
Sponsorship and donated venues cover the room.
Membership qualifies the room. Sponsorship monetises it. Neither works without the free tier at the centre.

Finding 1, continued

Three consequences, and none is a pricing problem

01

Growth comes from cities and cadence

Never from selling harder into a full chapter. A chapter at its ceiling is done, and that is by design.

02

Membership can never be the main line

A 40-seat room that gives away half its seats caps out. This is exactly why the sponsorship architecture exists.

03

A waitlist is a feature

It is the scarcity proof that prices the sponsorship. An oversubscribed room is worth more to a partner than an open one.

Roadmap

Turning this into a full financial model

1 · Monthly cohort engine
Members join on rolling dates; annual memberships create deferred revenue. Model cohorts by join month with per-cohort retention.
2 · Cash timing
Sponsorship arrives in lumps, dinner costs are paid on the night, memberships collected up front. Build a 13-week cash view.
3 · Seat-by-seat summit build
Replace the single blended line with pass types, booth tiers and sponsor cheques.
4 · Chapter unit model
Make one chapter a repeatable P&L so expansion is modelled as adding units.
5 · Actuals capture
After dinner one, replace assumed seat cost, guest-to-payer ratio and attendance with measured figures.
Three dinners of real data will move this model more than any amount of further modelling.
