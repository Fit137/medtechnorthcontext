## The firmographic screen

Run every candidate through this before it earns a call.

| Dimension | Winning profile | Disqualifier |
|---|---|---|
| Canadian revenue | $3M to $250M, or a subsidiary holding its own Canadian P&L | Global budget only, no Canadian line |
| Canadian headcount | 20 to 500 | Under 10, meaning no budget. Or a pure rep office |
| Commercial base | GTA or Golden Horseshoe, inside 90 minutes of Mississauga | No Canadian commercial presence at all |
| Who signs | Canadian marketing, professional relations or market development lead holding $10,000 discretionary | Global brand team. Procurement-first culture |
| Existing spend | Already funds study clubs, evening education, association meetings, trade booths | Has never sponsored anything |
| Regulatory path | No PAAB submission. Light or no MLR | Prescription drug or vaccine promotion |
| Their buyer | The person in the room owns the purchase decision | A hospital procurement committee owns it |
| Sales motion | Direct to practice or clinic owner, cycle under 90 days | Multi-year enterprise RFP |
| Stage | Series A to C, or a mature company defending a channel that is getting worse | Pre-revenue |
| Trigger | A dated 2026 regulatory or funding change moved their market | No timing pressure |
| Onboarding | Pays against an invoice plus a certificate of insurance | Requires a long MSA and a 90-day vendor review |

**The single highest-signal indicator:** a company that has a professional relations, clinical education, market development or KOL function **and no community of its own**. They already rent access to clinicians. You are a better landlord than what they are renting from now.

**The strongest disqualifier, and the easiest to miss:** a company that says yes immediately and wants a table, a booth, stage time or a list. Those are all purchases of the thing that cannot be sold. Screen for it in the first conversation, not the third.

## Tier A. Dental. Close these for the next room.

**Why this tier at all.** The CDCP is the largest expansion of federal health coverage in Canadian history: more than 6.5 million Canadians covered, roughly $13 billion over five years, 2026-27 benefit year opened 1 July 2026, benefit grids revised 1 April 2026 with preauthorization changes alongside. Dentists own their practices, so the room holds decision-makers rather than influencers. Dental industry marketing runs on study clubs, so the budget line already exists. And almost nothing in dental requires PAAB.

### A1. Practice-management, claims and billing software

**Why they are the most likely buyer on the entire list.** The CDCP made claims processing the number-one operational complaint in Ontario practices, and it changed again in April 2026. These vendors are in a land grab for practice owners right now, their buyer is exactly who is in the room, the cheque is a marketing manager's decision, and there is no regulatory review of any kind.

| Company | Base | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ABELDent ⬜ | Ontario | Canadian signature authority, no parent-company review. Verify current base and marketing lead |
| MaxiDent ✅ | Canada | Canadian provider, 40+ years, full practice-management suite |
| ClearDent ✅ | Canada | Cloud practice management, all practice sizes and specialties |
| Dentitek ✅ | Canada | Practice management with scheduling, automated billing, analytics |
| Smilepass ✅ | Toronto, Ontario | AI dental insurance verification, patient payment financing, digital treatment plans. Local, small, founder-reachable |
| Henry Schein One Canada (Dentrix) ✅ | Canada / US parent | Larger budget, slower US approval path |
| Curve Dental ⬜ | US with Canadian presence | Same trade-off as above |
| Adit, Oryx, Dental Intelligence ⬜ | US | Verify Canadian commercial presence before spending time |

**Approach the Canadian ones first.** ABELDent, MaxiDent, ClearDent, Dentitek and Smilepass clear gate 1 outright. The US-parented options carry bigger budgets and a slower signature, which is the wrong trade for a first deal.

### A2. Dental group practices and DSOs

**Why.** Their interest is recruitment and retention of dentists and hygienists, funded from employer-brand budget, with no regulatory review at all and the shortest approval path in the tier. On-thesis enough to be defensible in the room.

dentalcorp ⬜ (Toronto) · 123Dentist ⬜ · Altima Dental ⬜ · Dental Corporation of Canada ⬜

### A3. Practice finance, accounting and insurance

**Why they score highest on speed and probability of anything here.** Scotiabank Healthcare+ runs dedicated banking programmes for dentists and for pharmacists, covering students, associates and practice owners, including practice acquisition and equipment financing ✅. The room is literally their customer acquisition target. No regulatory review, regional marketing budget, and a $3,000 to $7,500 cheque is routine for them.

Scotiabank Healthcare+ ✅ · RBC Healthcare ⬜ · BMO ⬜ · MNP professional practice group ⬜ · Baker Tilly ⬜ · CDSPI ✅ (dental financial services body that already funds association CE and wellness initiatives) · ROI Corporation and other practice brokerages ⬜

**The trade-off, stated plainly.** Highest probability and fastest signature in the document, weakest strategic fit. A bank is off-thesis for a health innovation network. Use this group to fund a room, never to define one, and cap it at one per room. If practice finance becomes the recurring identity of the programme, the programme has quietly become something else.

### A4. Dental AI, imaging and intraoral scanning

**Why.** Radiograph AI and scanning companies sell direct to practice owners and already use evening education as their primary channel. Their product needs explaining, which is exactly the case where a room of fifteen beats a hall of five hundred.

Pearl ⬜ · Overjet ⬜ · VideaHealth ⬜ · Denti.AI ⬜ (verify Toronto base) · 3Shape Canada ⬜ · Medit ⬜ · SprintRay ⬜ · Planmeca Canada ⬜ · DEXIS / Envista ⬜

### A5. Aligners, implants and materials

**Why.** The most sponsorship-native category in Canadian dentistry. Their entire clinician marketing function is built on study clubs and CE.

Align Technology Canada ⬜ · Straumann Canada ⬜ · Nobel Biocare ⬜ · Dentsply Sirona Canada ⬜ · Ivoclar ⬜ · Kulzer ⬜ · SDI ⬜

**Caution.** The largest of these will ask for stage time and product presence, because that is what they buy everywhere else. That is precisely what cannot be sold. Lead with the never-list rather than defending it later.

### A6. Dental distribution

Henry Schein Canada ⬜ · Patterson Dental Canada ⬜ · Sinclair Dental ⬜

Rep-level relationship budgets exist and are discretionary, so the money is real. The risk is that they will push for a booth or a table, which turns the room into a vendor floor and triggers exactly the failure `data/events/2026-07-29-patient-journey-jam.md` records as correctly avoided at the first event. **Sell them the brief, not the room**, unless they accept the never-list without negotiation.

## Tier B. Pharmacy. Open now, close months three to six.

**Why this tier at all.** As of 1 July 2026 Ontario pharmacists can assess and prescribe for nine additional minor ailments, moving from 19 to 28, with five more planned for early 2027 to reach 33. Six additional publicly funded vaccines were added. Over 99% of Ontario pharmacies participate in the minor ailments programme and 2.4 million assessments have already been delivered. This is the largest workflow change in Ontario community pharmacy in a decade.

### B1. Pharmacy clinical services software

**The fastest movers in the whole tier**, because their product roadmap *is* the trigger event.

| Company | Base | Note |
|---|---|---|
| MAPflow ✅ | Canada | Minor-ailment prescribing platform, 2,200+ pharmacies, released virtual minor-ailment updates for rural Ontario pharmacies. Their entire product is the 1 July change |
| MedEssist ✅ | 108 College St, Toronto | Pharmacist-founded, seed funding from Staircase Ventures and Hyperplane VC, Google for Startups. Access to Care programme has onboarded 100 Ontario pharmacies. Founder-reachable in one hop |
| TELUS Health (Kroll, PharmaClik Rx) ✅ | Canada | Minor ailments modules with pre-populated fee-for-service claims. Large, so slower, but Canadian signature |
| Fillware, Nexxsys, WinRx ⬜ | Canada | Verify current ownership and marketing function |

### B2. Pharmacy banners and ownership groups

**Why.** As scope expands, recruiting and retaining pharmacist-owners becomes the competitive question between banners. Marketing budgets are Canadian and discretionary.

| Group | Ontario scale | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Guardian / IDA / Remedy'sRx ✅ | 852 Ontario locations, ~58% of the national network | Under the McKesson Canada retail banner network |
| Pharmasave ✅ | 486 Ontario locations, ~53% of its national network | |
| Whole Health Pharmacy Partners ✅ | 200+, Ontario-based | Established 2016, fastest-growing independent banner. Smallest and therefore fastest |
| Shoppers Drug Mart / Loblaw ⬜ | Largest in Canada | Slowest by an order of magnitude. Note one first-room attendee already works inside Shoppers, which is a warm path worth using for intelligence rather than a pitch |

### B3. Point-of-care testing and diagnostics

**Why.** Expanded assessment scope makes point-of-care testing the enabling product. Abbott's ID NOW platform returns Strep A results in under fifteen minutes and is already used in provincial pharmacy programmes ✅. Abbott, bioMérieux, Cepheid and Roche all hold miniaturised molecular tests suitable for pharmacy use ✅.

Abbott (ID NOW, Afinion) ✅ · Roche Diagnostics Canada ✅ · bioMérieux ✅ · Cepheid ✅ · QuidelOrtho ⬜ · BD ⬜ · Siemens Healthineers ⬜

Real Canadian commercial teams and real budgets. Medium speed. Device marketing carries meaningfully lighter review than drug promotion.

### B4. Consumer health and OTC

Haleon Canada ⬜ · Kenvue ⬜ · Bayer Consumer Health ⬜ · Reckitt ⬜ · Jamieson ⬜ (Toronto)

Pharmacist recommendation is their entire channel, and minor-ailments expansion directly reshapes it. MLR exists but is lighter than prescription. Month four to six.

### B5. Vaccine manufacturers

GSK ⬜ · Pfizer ⬜ · Merck ⬜ · Moderna ⬜ · Sanofi ⬜ · AstraZeneca ⬜

Six newly publicly funded vaccines in Ontario pharmacies (tetanus, whooping cough, diphtheria, RSV, shingles, pneumococcus) is a commercial event of real size, and pharmacist education is a budget these companies already spend heavily on.

**They are the largest cheques available and the slowest path to them.** PAAB, full MLR, global standard operating procedures often stricter than Canadian minimums, and vendor onboarding that will take longer than the review itself. **Do not build the next room on them.** Open the conversation now, sell them the brief rather than a room, because a research purchase clears far faster than an event sponsorship, and let the room follow in month six once the vendor readiness pack exists.

## Do not sell to, yet

| Group | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Hospital enterprise vendors** (Epic, Oracle Health, Philips, GE HealthCare) | No hospital decision-makers in the room. They would be buying the room you do not have |
| **Anything whose buyer is a physician** | Zero MDs have attended. Selling physician access you cannot compose is the fastest available way to destroy the property |
| **Venture funds** | They want deal flow. The first room held two founders |
| **Professional services selling to founders** (law, accounting, regulatory consultancies, CROs) | They will say yes faster than anyone and buy the wrong thing. Service providers are a capped segment for a reason: eight in a room of sixteen is a vendor floor with a few clinicians standing in it. Cap at one per room and hold the cap when a cheque tests it |
| **Government and agency partners** (OBIO, CAN Health, MEDTEQ+, MaRS, RIC Centre, Invest Ontario, Toronto Global) | These do not sponsor, they contract. Separate motion, separate track: $15,000 to $60,000 delivery engagements, three to six months to first cash. See `data/competitors.md` |
