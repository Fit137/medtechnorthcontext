## The screen. Seven checks, all of them

| # | Requirement | How to verify in under five minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Space for six to eight concurrent groups of roughly 30, plus one room the whole day can gather in** | A published floor plan, a room-booking page, a conference or events page, or photographs of a past multi-track day at their own address |
| 2 | **In-house AV and staff who run it** | An events page listing AV, or a facility with a named coordinator. If we would have to hire AV, the cash risk `ADR-018` was written to avoid comes straight back |
| 3 | **A mandate or budget reason to host at no charge** | Ecosystem or community language in their own description, a utilisation target, a partnership office, a reporting obligation, or an under-used facility |
| 4 | **No procurement path required for a yes** | Nothing is being bought, so the test is whether one named person owns the calendar. If the answer involves a vendor form before a date, they are a slow host |
| 5 | **Category-neutral to the day's topics, or willing to let the topics be set** | Same orthogonality rule as `ADR-016`. A host whose product is the subject converts the day into their sales meeting |
| 6 | **A weekday available inside a two-week window in October or November 2026** | **Ask for a window, never a date.** Facilities say yes to windows and no to dates |
| 7 | **Will accept a certificate of insurance and a one-page agreement** | Standard. It is also the reason CGL insurance is on the critical path in `docs/20-outreach-campaign-sequence.md` |

**Check 1 disqualifies more candidates than the other six combined**, and it is the check most corporate briefing centres fail. A briefing centre is usually one impressive room and two small ones. A day of rooms needs six. Screen for it first.

### Disqualifiers

Nothing bookable without a rental agreement. A venue whose business model is renting the space, since we are not a customer and the conversation is a price negotiation we do not want. A host whose product is the subject of a room. Anywhere requiring a security or facilities charge that we would carry. Any city where the scarce-side gate below has not been passed.

### The scarce-side gate, and it is not negotiable

**Do not sign a host in any city until practising clinicians in that same city have replied.** A donated building in Vancouver is worthless if the room seats nine, and the failure is public, expensive in airfare and unrecoverable in reputation.

**The gate:** at least 12 positive replies from practising clinicians or practice owners with that city's postal codes, before a host agreement is signed there. This tests H6 in `docs/18-hypothesis-risk-and-falsification.md` before it can hurt.

**The corollary that shapes the whole campaign: the city is an output, not an input.** Run the host campaign in every corridor at once and let the first qualified yes that also clears the clinician gate choose where we fly.

## The seven archetypes, ranked by how fast a yes arrives

Ranked on decision latency and on whether a free yes is structurally available, not on prestige.

### 1. Regional innovation centres, incubators and accelerators

**Why they rank first.** Convening is what they are funded to do. They report to a public funder on organisations engaged and events held, and those are the units of their annual report. They have multi-room space with AV, a person who owns the calendar, and no procurement path for a partner event because no money changes hands. The media package is proof of activity for the exact report they are already writing.

**Approach:** Director of Programs, Director of Partnerships, Executive Director, Health Sector Lead, Community Manager.

**Named, Ontario:** MaRS Discovery District ⬜ (Toronto, purpose-built for this) · Communitech ⬜ (Kitchener) · RIC Centre ⬜ (Mississauga, already named in `docs/12-convening-as-a-service.md` as a sourcing route) · Innovation Factory ⬜ (Hamilton) · Haltech ⬜ (Halton) · Spark Centre ⬜ (Durham) · Invest Ottawa and Bayview Yards ⬜ · TMU DMZ ⬜ · Creative Destruction Lab at Rotman ⬜ · JLABS Toronto ⬜ (health-specific, check category stake given the parent)

**Named, rest of Canada:** Centech ⬜ and District 3 ⬜ (Montreal) · adMare BioInnovations ⬜ (Montreal and Vancouver) · Foresight and Innovate BC ⬜ (Vancouver) · Platform Calgary ⬜ · Health City ⬜ (Edmonton) · North Forge ⬜ (Winnipeg) · Volta ⬜ (Halifax) · Ignite ⬜ (Fredericton) · Genesis Centre ⬜ (St John's)

**The watch item:** an innovation centre will want to fill seats with its own portfolio companies. That is founders, and the room needs the scarce side. Cap nominated seats in writing in the first message, per section 8 of `docs/19-host-incentives-and-the-media-floor.md`.

### 2. Universities and colleges with health programmes

**Why they rank second.** They own exactly the building the day needs: lecture theatres, breakout rooms, in-house AV, catering, parking, and staff who run events weekly. Autumn is term time, so the building is staffed and lively. They are category-neutral to every clinical topic, they carry legitimacy no corporate host can buy, and the footage is recruitment content and industry-engagement evidence at once.

**Why they are not first.** Two to six weeks slower, because the yes usually needs a faculty or partnership office as well as a facilities booking, and academic calendars are set early.

**Approach:** Director of Partnerships or Industry Engagement, Dean or Associate Dean of a health faculty, Director of Continuing Professional Development, Executive Director of a research institute.

**Named, GTA and Ontario:** McMaster ⬜ (a professor there posted one of the four public testimonials, which makes this the warmest academic door available) · University of Toronto Temerty ⬜ · Toronto Metropolitan University ⬜ · George Brown College ⬜ (dental and dental hygiene programmes, directly relevant to the dental thesis in `docs/11-corporate-outreach.md`) · Humber ⬜ · Sheridan ⬜ · Seneca ⬜ · Centennial ⬜ · Conestoga ⬜ · Michener Institute ⬜ (UHN) · University of Waterloo ⬜ (already a cited source in `data/verified-stats.md`) · Western ⬜ · Queen's ⬜ · uOttawa ⬜

**Named, rest of Canada:** McGill ⬜ · Concordia ⬜ · Université de Montréal ⬜ · UBC ⬜ · SFU ⬜ · BCIT ⬜ · University of Calgary ⬜ · University of Alberta ⬜ · Dalhousie ⬜

### 3. Economic development and investment attraction bodies

**Why they rank here.** Their performance is measured on landed companies and investor touchpoints, which is a reporting currency rather than a revenue one. They are also the best route into the US inbound line, because they hold lists of foreign companies actively evaluating Canada, which `docs/12-convening-as-a-service.md` already identifies. Several have their own event space; those that do not will co-promote, which is worth almost as much.

**Approach:** Director of Investment Attraction, Sector Lead for Life Sciences or Health, Director of Partnerships.

**Named:** Invest Ontario ⬜ · Toronto Global ⬜ · Invest Mississauga ⬜ (already a cited source, and the corridor argument below is theirs) · Invest Ottawa ⬜ · Montréal International ⬜ · Invest Vancouver ⬜ · Calgary Economic Development ⬜ · Edmonton Global ⬜ · Waterloo EDC ⬜ · Hamilton EDO ⬜ · Peel and Halton economic development ⬜ · Trade Commissioner Service ⬜ · US Consulate General Toronto commercial service ⬜ (inbound line only) · Canadian American Business Council ⬜ · Toronto Region Board of Trade ⬜ · Mississauga Board of Trade ⬜

### 4. Industry associations and cluster organisations

**Why:** member value is their product and an autumn day of rooms is member value delivered. Some have space, most have distribution, and distribution is the second most useful thing a host can supply. **The caution:** several run their own competing events and will read this as encroachment. Approach as co-promotion first and venue second, and expect a slower, more political conversation than the ranking suggests.

**Named:** Medtech Canada ⬜ · Life Sciences Ontario ⬜ · OBIO ⬜ · BIOTECanada ⬜ · CAN Health Network ⬜ · LifeSciences BC ⬜ · Montreal InVivo ⬜ · BioNova ⬜ · BioAlberta ⬜

### 5. Hospital innovation units and research institutes

**Why:** unmatched legitimacy and the clinicians are already in the building. **Why they rank fifth:** the slowest approval on the page, research ethics and communications review both plausible, and real category stake once a room touches procurement or clinical systems. Excellent for one composed room inside the day, poor as the host of the whole day at this notice.

**Named:** UHN Techna and OpenLab ⬜ · Trillium Health Partners ⬜ (Mississauga) · Unity Health ⬜ · Sunnybrook ⬜ · SickKids ⬜ · Sinai Health ⬜ · Hamilton Health Sciences ⬜ · William Osler ⬜ · CHEO ⬜ · The Ottawa Hospital ⬜ · MUHC ⬜ · Vancouver Coastal Health Research Institute ⬜ · Providence ⬜

### 6. Professional services and law firms with a life sciences practice

**Why:** they own purpose-built convening facilities, they have partner-level discretionary budget, they have no stake in a clinical conclusion, and thought leadership is what they buy instead of leads. The Deloitte Greenhouse in Toronto exists specifically to convene C-suite groups for sessions lasting hours to two days ✅. EY opened Canada's first wavespace in the EY Tower ✅.

**The specific risk, unchanged from `data/cohost-targets.md`:** their model is client entertainment, so they will want the room filled with their clients. State the nomination cap in the first message. Also hold the service-provider cap: a hosting firm gets one or two seats, not eight.

### 7. Corporates with briefing centres, banks, and medtech Canadian headquarters

**Why they rank last as venue hosts and first as funders.** This is the group `data/cohost-targets.md` was built for, and they are the right people to pay for a room. Approach them for the funded rooms inside the day, and take the building only if the floor plan genuinely supports it.

## Corridors, and why the GTA still leads even when travel is free

The founder's willingness to fly changes the target list. It does not change the arithmetic underneath it.

**Ontario holds roughly 46.8% of Canada's medical device companies and more than 1,300 medical device firms** ✅. **Mississauga is the second largest medtech sector in the country behind Toronto, with over 500 life sciences businesses, more than 27,000 skilled workers, and over $1.2 billion in life sciences research and development foreign direct investment across five years** ✅.

So the correct reading of "I will travel anywhere" is not that geography stopped mattering. It is that **the host campaign should be sent everywhere at once and the clinician gate should decide where we land.** The GTA remains the highest prior by a wide margin, and a non-GTA yes has to clear a higher bar precisely because there is no list there.

| Priority | Corridor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mississauga and the GTA** | Density, the existing list, no travel cost, and the corridor facts above |
| 2 | **Toronto downtown** | Professional services, banks, MaRS, universities, and the venue the US inbound line needs, since `docs/16-product-architecture.md` already sells Toronto to buyers who fly in |
| 3 | **Hamilton, Waterloo, Kitchener** | McMaster, Communitech, Innovation Factory, and a day trip rather than a flight |
| 4 | **Ottawa** | Public sector adjacency, Invest Ottawa, federal reporting calendars |
| 5 | **Montreal** | Second largest device cluster at roughly 22.3% ✅, strong institutional hosts, and a language decision that has to be made deliberately rather than discovered on site |
| 6 | **Vancouver** | Real cluster, weakest list, highest travel cost |
| 7 | **Calgary, Edmonton, Halifax, Winnipeg** | Only against a host who clears the gate and offers something the others do not |

## What to record for every candidate

Same discipline as `data/prospects/schema.md`. `unknown` is a valid answer and a guessed field is worse than a blank one.

`org` · `archetype` · `city` · `rooms_available` · `plenary_capacity` · `av_in_house` yes/no/unknown · `named_contact` · `contact_title` · `mandate_signal` (the reason a free yes is available) · `category_stake` orthogonal/adjacent/conflicted · `nomination_cap_accepted` yes/no/unknown · `window_offered` · `status` · `evidence` (URL per non-obvious claim)

When a candidate reaches `qualified`, add it to `data/prospects/companies.csv` so it is scored against the three products as well. A venue host today is frequently a room funder next quarter, and the two lists should not drift apart.
