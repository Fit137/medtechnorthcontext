## What a co-host actually is

Not a sponsor. A company that provides its own premises, pays a production fee, and receives co-host billing. MedTech North designs, composes, produces and facilitates. The client never sees the guest list before the room.

That definition is what makes the firmographic unusual. **Most of the companies with the most money to spend cannot co-host at all, because they have nowhere to put thirty people.** Space is the binding constraint, not budget, and it is the filter that most target lists miss.

---

## The two axes that define a good co-host

Every candidate sits somewhere on a grid, and only one quadrant works.

**Axis 1: audience relevance.** Does this company sell to, hire from, or serve the people who will be in the room? High relevance means they will pay. Low relevance means they will not, no matter how good the room is.

**Axis 2: category stake.** Does this company have a commercial interest in the *conclusions* the room reaches? High stake means clinicians read the room as a sales meeting and the asset degrades.

|  | **Low category stake** | **High category stake** |
|---|---|---|
| **High audience relevance** | **The target quadrant.** They want the room and have no stake in its conclusions | Sell them the brief, a session, or an advisory roundtable where the interest is declared. Never a co-host slot |
| **Low audience relevance** | Miro. Worked because of the client relationship, not the fit. Do not build a list here | Irrelevant |

Miro sat in the bottom-left, which is exactly why the relationship ran out of room: healthcare was never Miro's ICP, so there was no reason for the budget to grow. **The target is companies with Miro's neutrality and a healthcare ICP.** That combination is the entire brief.

## The firmographic screen

### Five must-haves. All five, no exceptions.

| # | Requirement | How to verify in under five minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A Canadian office with hostable space for 25 to 40 seated** | A named facility (innovation lab, briefing centre, experience centre, customer centre, Greenhouse, wavespace, town hall floor), or photographic evidence of a past event at their own address on LinkedIn |
| 2 | **A named healthcare or life sciences vertical in Canada** | At least one person on LinkedIn whose title contains both a healthcare word and an industry, vertical, or marketing word, located in Canada |
| 3 | **A field marketing, industry marketing, community or events function with Canadian budget** | Titles: Field Marketing Manager, Industry Marketing Lead, Head of Community, Events Marketing, ABM Manager, Customer Marketing Manager. Located in Canada, not the US |
| 4 | **A considered, relationship-led sales motion** | Contract values above roughly $50,000, sales cycles of three months or more, several stakeholders per deal. Self-serve and product-led companies will not pay for this |
| 5 | **No commercial stake in the room's likely conclusions** | Or a willingness to let the room's topic be set so that they have none |

**Requirement 1 disqualifies more candidates than the other four combined.** Screen for it first and stop wasting time on companies that would have to rent a venue, because at that point they are buying a sponsorship, not a co-host slot, and the price and the pitch both change.

### Scoring signals, each of which raises probability

| Signal | Why it matters | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **They already run their own executive dinners or roundtables** | Budget line exists, mechanic understood, internal justification pre-written | High |
| **A Canadian healthcare leader hired in the last 12 months** | New leaders need pipeline and internal visibility fast, and they buy to get both | **Highest single trigger** |
| **A Canadian healthcare product, vertical or practice launched in the last 12 months** | Same logic, with an announced commitment behind it | High |
| **Exhibited or sponsored at a Canadian health event in the last 18 months** | Proves both budget and intent, and tells you what they are currently paying for | High |
| **Their Canadian healthcare team is 2 to 15 people** | Large enough to hold budget, small enough that they cannot convene alone | High |
| **A published Canadian healthcare customer story** | They are past the "should we do healthcare" question | Medium |
| **Fiscal year ending within four months** | Unspent field marketing budget is the cheapest money in the world to win | Medium |
| **The Canadian office is new or recently expanded** | New space needs to be justified and filled | Medium |

### Disqualifiers

No Canadian office. Product-led or self-serve sales motion. Healthcare absent from their vertical list. Under roughly 50 Canadian employees, meaning no field marketing function exists. Their product is the room's subject. A pharmaceutical company promoting a specific molecule, which is a different product entirely and belongs in an advisory roundtable format.

### Size band

**200 to 20,000 Canadian employees**, or Canadian revenue above roughly $50M. For scale-ups, Series C or later with a Canadian office of 100 or more. Below that band there is space but no budget, or budget but no space.

## The five archetypes, ranked

Ranked on co-hosting specifically, meaning space plus budget plus healthcare relevance plus low stake.

### 1. Professional services with a life sciences or health practice

**Why they rank first: convening is already their marketing model.** These firms do not need to be sold on the format, only on the composition. They own purpose-built convening space, they have partner-level discretionary budget, they have zero stake in any clinical conclusion, and thought leadership positioning is what they buy instead of leads.

The Deloitte Greenhouse in Toronto exists specifically to convene C-suite groups for sessions lasting from hours to two days, and has hosted TD, the Toronto Blue Jays and Infrastructure Ontario ✅. EY opened Canada's first wavespace in the EY Tower ✅. These are not meeting rooms, they are facilities built for exactly this.

**Named:** Deloitte Canada ✅ · EY Canada ✅ · PwC Canada ⬜ · KPMG Canada ⬜ · Accenture Canada ⬜ · MNP ⬜ · BDO Canada ⬜ · Grant Thornton ⬜
**Law firms with life sciences or emerging companies groups:** Osler ⬜ · Torys ⬜ · Fasken ⬜ · McCarthy Tétrault ⬜ · Blakes ⬜ · Gowling WLG ⬜ · Norton Rose Fulbright ⬜ · Borden Ladner Gervais ⬜ · Bereskin & Parr ⬜ · Smart & Biggar ⬜

> **The specific risk with this archetype, and it is not the obvious one.** Their entire model is client entertainment, so they will want to fill the room with *their* clients. That breaks the rule the whole product rests on. The contract must say plainly: the co-host may nominate, MedTech North composes, and the co-host does not see the list in advance. If they will not accept that, they are buying a venue-and-catering arrangement and should be priced and named as one. Also hold the service-provider cap: a co-hosting firm gets one or two seats, not eight.

### 2. Horizontal platforms with a healthcare vertical

**The Miro shape, corrected.** They sell infrastructure, data, cloud, security or collaboration into healthcare. They have a Canadian healthcare vertical lead with a budget. They have executive briefing space. And they cannot get clinicians into a room on their own, because their brand is not clinical, which is precisely the gap being sold. Perfect orthogonality with genuine audience relevance.

**Named:** Microsoft Canada ✅ (Mississauga at 1950 Meadowvale Blvd, and a downtown Toronto headquarters at CIBC Square opened May 2022, with customer-facing technology displays including telemedicine) · AWS Canada ⬜ · Google Cloud Canada ⬜ · Salesforce Canada ⬜ (Health Cloud) · ServiceNow Canada ⬜ · SAP Canada ⬜ · Oracle Canada ⬜ (note: Oracle Health carries category stake in clinical systems topics) · IBM Canada ⬜ (Markham) · Cisco Canada ⬜ · Dell Technologies Canada ⬜ · Nvidia Canada ⬜ · Snowflake ⬜ · Databricks ⬜ · Palo Alto Networks ⬜ · Okta ⬜ · Zoom ⬜ · Docusign ⬜ · Atlassian ⬜ · Figma ⬜ · Notion ⬜ · Twilio ⬜ · Thomson Reuters ⬜ (Toronto) · OpenText ⬜ (Waterloo)

**Highest-probability sub-slice:** a company that hired a Canadian healthcare industry lead in the last twelve months. That person has a number to hit, no local community, and a budget. They are the single best-defined buyer in this document.

### 3. Financial institutions with a healthcare practice

**Why:** they acquire clinicians as customers, they own large downtown space, they have zero clinical stake, and their signature is fast. Scotiabank Healthcare+ runs dedicated programmes for dentists and pharmacists ✅, which means the room is literally their acquisition target.

**Named:** Scotiabank ✅ · RBC ⬜ (and RBCx) · BMO ⬜ · TD ⬜ · CIBC ⬜ (CIBC Square) · National Bank ⬜ · Desjardins ⬜ · Sun Life ⬜ (administers CDCP claims) · Manulife ⬜ · Canada Life ⬜ · Green Shield Canada ⬜ · CDSPI ⬜

**The trade-off, stated again because it will be tempting to forget:** highest speed, weakest thesis fit. A bank funds a room, it never defines one. Cap at one per room, and if practice finance becomes the recurring identity of the programme then the programme has quietly become something else.

### 4. Canadian health tech scale-ups

**Why:** their ICP is exactly the room, their founders sign in one call, and many have real offices. **Why they rank fourth:** category stake. Topic selection has to do real work here, and some of them are competitors to each other in ways that constrain who else can be invited.

**Named:** PointClickCare ✅ (Mississauga, one of Canada's largest homegrown technology companies) · League ⬜ (Toronto) · Maple ⬜ (Toronto) · Think Research ⬜ (Toronto) · Kii Health ✅ (formerly CloudMD, Toronto) · Greenspace Health ✅ (Toronto and Kelowna) · AlayaCare ⬜ (Montreal) · Dialogue Health ⬜ (Montreal) · WELL Health ⬜ (Vancouver) · Jane App ⬜ (North Vancouver) · MedEssist ⬜ (Toronto) · MAPflow ⬜ · Cloud DX ⬜ (Kitchener) · Novari Health ⬜ (Kingston) · QHR Technologies ⬜ · Myant ⬜ (Mississauga) · Baylis Medical Technologies ✅ (Mississauga)

### 5. Medtech and pharma Canadian headquarters

**Biggest budgets, most space, slowest process, highest stake.** Better suited to a clinical advisory roundtable, where commercial interest is declared openly and the format is built to handle it, than to an open co-hosted room.

**Named, and note the geography:** Boston Scientific ✅ · GE HealthCare ✅ · Baylis Medical ✅ · Myant ✅ (all Mississauga) · Roche Canada ⬜ · AstraZeneca Canada ⬜ · GSK Canada ⬜ · Amgen Canada ⬜ · Novartis Canada ⬜ · Sanofi Canada ⬜ · Johnson & Johnson Canada ⬜ (Markham) · Medtronic Canada ⬜ (Brampton) · Abbott Canada ⬜ · Stryker Canada ⬜ · Siemens Healthineers Canada ⬜ (Oakville) · Philips Canada ⬜ · Baxter Canada ⬜

## The geographic finding that should change the travel plan

**Mississauga is the second largest medtech sector in Canada, behind only Toronto** ✅. It holds over 500 life sciences businesses, more than 27,000 skilled workers, and attracted over $1.2 billion in life sciences research and development foreign direct investment over five years ✅. Named medtech companies based there include PointClickCare, Baylis Medical Technologies, Boston Scientific, Myant and GE Healthcare ✅. Microsoft Canada's long-standing Canadian office sits on Meadowvale Blvd in the same city ✅.

The founder already lives there. **The densest co-host corridor in the country is a fifteen-minute drive from the condo, and the strategy has been treating the condo as a liability without noticing that its postcode is an asset.** Work Mississauga and the GTA to exhaustion before booking a single flight.

Priority order for cities: **Mississauga and the GTA** first, by a wide margin. **Toronto downtown** second, for professional services and financial institutions. Then **Montreal** (AlayaCare, Dialogue, pharma), **Vancouver** (WELL, Jane App), **Ottawa** (federal and public sector adjacency), **Waterloo and Kitchener** (OpenText, Cloud DX, Communitech), **Calgary** last.

Travel only against a confirmed meeting with a qualified co-host, never speculatively. One booked co-host room pays for the trip several times over, and an unbooked trip is the fastest way to burn a month.
