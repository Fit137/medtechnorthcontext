## What this list is, and the one thing it is not

**It is not a list of verified LinkedIn profile URLs.** LinkedIn is unreachable from the research environment, so no profile page was opened and no vanity URL was read. Inventing one would send outreach to the wrong person, which is worse than sending none.

What the CSV carries instead, for every row:

- A **LinkedIn people-search URL** that resolves to the person on the first result page
- A **Google site-search URL** restricted to `linkedin.com/in`, which usually surfaces the exact profile URL

That is the same format `data/prospects/contact_searches.csv` already uses, so it fits the existing pipeline.

## The peer test, and why the ranking is what it is

The request was for peers of similar calibre. There are three different populations here and they are not interchangeable.

**Founder-led Canadian health technology chief executives** are the true peer group. Same category of company, same investor logic, same buyers, comparable scale. These are the people for whom "the chief executive of a health data platform is at the table" is a genuine draw, because they already know who that is.

**Presidents and general managers of multinational subsidiaries** are senior and worth having, and they are a different animal. `docs/11-corporate-outreach.md` gate 1 explains why: the outcome they own is a Canadian marketing plan, not a company. They are slower, more review-gated, and they read a private dinner as a marketing decision rather than a peer invitation. Include them, rank them second.

**Health system and public agency chief executives** are the buyers. Getting one into the room is worth more to everyone else at the table than another vendor, and it is also the segment with zero attendance across three rooms. Trillium Health Partners is ten minutes from the venue.
