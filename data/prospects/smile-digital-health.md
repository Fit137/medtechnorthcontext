## 3. Audit of the outbound message, and whether it cost anything

Three problems worth naming, and one conclusion that runs against them.

**Problem 1. The ask was expensive for a stranger.** "Who would you love to see on the table?" asks a CEO to write out his ideal customer profile, in a channel he does not read, for a room he has no evidence exists. The cheapest available response is to point at the concrete action instead, which is exactly what he did.

**Problem 2. The frame changed mid-thread.** At 9:48 he was invited as a guest. At 10:14 he was addressed as a buyer whose customers could be assembled for him. Senior people go quiet when an invitation converts into an offer inside twenty-six minutes. It also sits awkwardly beside our own rule that nobody in the room is anybody's prospect (`docs/07-brand-and-voice.md`). Composing to a brief is a legitimate paid product, and it is not the opening line to someone whose trust in the room's neutrality we also want.

**Problem 3. A promise we cannot currently keep.** "A composition that matches your ideal customers or partners" means, for this company, health system CIOs, payers and government. We have zero physicians, no health system relationships, and three MDs who no-showed. That sentence promised the hardest room in the business to the person least able to overlook a miss.

**Minor:** "feature Smile Digital Health's patient journey" is imprecise. A health data platform does not have an obvious patient journey, and "feature" leans toward exposure rather than composition.

**The conclusion, against all three.** He did not change the subject because of the message. He registered *between* those two messages, said yes to a future dinner before any of it, and closed with an ordinary courtesy. The likelier reading is a busy CEO with low LinkedIn attention taking the one action he could take in thirty seconds.

**Nothing needs repairing. The composition question is unanswered, not refused.** It needs re-asking in a channel he reads, in a form that costs him a yes or no rather than an essay.

## 4. Where it fails, and this part decides the strategy

`data/cohost-targets.md` names four screening traps. **Trap 3 applies here directly:** orthogonality is necessary and not sufficient, because a company can have no stake in the room's conclusions and still be the wrong co-host if nobody in the room is their buyer.

The room that actually assembles is dentists, clinical pharmacists, physiotherapists, researchers and students (`ADR-012`). **None of those people buys an enterprise FHIR platform.** A dentist does not procure a clinical data repository. The composed room contains close to zero of this company's economic buyers.

So the honest read on the standard co-host screen:

| Requirement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Canadian hostable space for 25 to 40 seated | **Unverified.** A suite in a College Street building. This is the binding constraint and the first thing to check |
| 2. Named Canadian healthcare vertical | **Pass, overqualified.** Healthcare is the whole company |
| 3. Canadian marketing or events function with budget | **Likely, unverified.** Check LinkedIn for field marketing, community or events titles held in Canada |
| 4. Considered, relationship-led sales motion | **Strong pass** |
| 5. No commercial stake in the room's conclusions | **Conditional on topic.** See below |

**On requirement 5.** Their product is orthogonal to a clinical specialty room. A room on the dental patient journey, or on pharmacy scope expansion, reaches no conclusion in which a data platform has a stake. That is safe.

A room on interoperability, health data, or AI readiness in Canadian health systems is **their own category**, and per the co-host table in `docs/12-convening-as-a-service.md` that combination is not safe as a co-host. Clinicians will read it as a sales meeting. It is a brief, a session, or a clinical advisory roundtable with the interest declared openly, and it is priced as one.

Expect them to want the data room. That is the room to decline as a co-host and sell as something else.

## 5. The asset here is not the cheque

The gap this business has never closed is physicians and health systems. Three physicians accepted for room 002 and all three no-showed. Zero MDs have sat in a room. The venue repels them and it cost a confirmed speaker.

**This company sells to hospitals, payers and governments for a living.** Their customer contacts are, precisely, the segment we cannot recruit. Their ecosystem contacts are health-tech founders, who are the other half of the table.

The nomination question is worth more than a first-year contract, and it has already been asked and not yet answered:

> "Who would you love to see on the table?"

**Follow up on that question before anything commercial is raised.** It costs nothing, it is the highest-converting channel available per `docs/10-growth-flywheel.md`, and a CEO answering it converts a registration into a supply line for the exact people we lack.

## 6. On giving him a free convening

The instinct is right in two ways and wrong in one, and the wrong part is the expensive one.

**Right: do not sell early.** At his seniority, with two rooms behind us, a price before he has seen anything ends the conversation. Holding back is correct.

**Right: never our building.** Their headquarters or nothing. `ADR-013` already names the client's own office as the answer to the venue problem, and `docs/08-legal-and-compliance.md` treats the condo as the highest unmitigated exposure.

**Wrong: free removes the one protection the product has.**

Four reasons, in order of how much they cost.

**1. A free room is the hardest room in the business.** Composing to their brief means health system CIOs, payers and government buyers. That is the composition we have never delivered once. Zero MDs have sat in a room and three no-showed. Offering to assemble that group, for free, at their office, with our name on the night, is the highest delivery risk available taken at the lowest possible price. If it underdelivers we have burned exactly the relationship the free room was meant to protect.

**2. Free has no brief, and the paid brief is the whole safety mechanism.** Per `docs/16-product-architecture.md`, the brief is what stops us signing a convening we cannot fill, and it is described there as our own protection rather than the client's. Remove the fee and the qualification step goes with it. We would carry all the delivery risk and none of the screening.

**3. Free anchors at zero.** The next conversation opens with "what changed?" A $25,000 convening after a free one of the same shape is a very hard step, and anchors are much easier to avoid than to undo.

**4. Free tells us nothing.** We do not know whether this company will spend anything on convening. A free room proves he will accept a gift, which is not information.

### What to give instead

Two things, both genuinely valuable, neither of which touches the price ladder.

**Give the brief away, and do not call it a brief.** The work is: here is exactly who we would put in the room and whether we can get them. Doing that for nothing costs a week of research, carries no delivery risk, needs no venue, and produces the ICP conversation he did not answer on LinkedIn, as a piece of work presented to him rather than a question asked of him. Most importantly it tells us privately whether his room is fillable **before** we promise it. If the honest answer is "we can reach twelve of these thirty", we discover that in a document instead of on the night. Do not price it and then discount it to zero, because that invites a comparison. Frame it as coming back to him with the list.

**Give him a seat and three introductions.** He comes to the next dinner as a guest, seated next to two people worth his evening, and receives three consented introductions afterwards with a composition report. That is the product experienced in full, at zero risk, and it is not a discount on anything.

**Then, and only then, the paid room at their headquarters.**

### On an external venue with him as partner

Third choice, and not yet. An external venue means we pay for it and carry public liability we do not have. No CGL is in force. Their headquarters or the existing venue until that certificate exists.

### One structural warning

"Feature Smile Digital Health on the night" and "compose a room of your ideal customers" cannot both be true while he is also presented to guests as a fellow guest. Pick one posture and state it. Either he attends as a guest in a neutral room, or he co-hosts a room composed to a brief, in which case every guest is told who is hosting before they accept, which is our own rule and is what keeps it clean. The failure mode is doing the second while describing it as the first. Clinicians detect that inside one evening.

## 7. The sequence

Timing matters more than the pitch. The mistake available here is treating a polite yes as demand.

**Move to email today.** He told us he reads LinkedIn sporadically, and he registered through the site, so we hold an address. That is the most useful thing he said. Short note, confirms the seat, no ask, one line of value.

**Re-ask the composition question as work, not as a question.** Not "who would you love to see". Instead: here are the people I am putting in the room, is there anyone here you would want to sit beside, and is there anyone missing. That costs him thirty seconds and it produces the same answer.

**Before the room.** Confirm attendance personally and close to the date. Free-attendance rooms run 40 to 60% no-show, and a 272-person CEO is the profile most likely to be pulled elsewhere. He was also late to reply the first time. Unconfirmed until seated.

**In the room.** No ask, no pricing, no tier language. Seating needs care in both directions: a large-vendor CEO can tilt a small room, and founders will work him if allowed to.

**T+48 hours.** Composition report, what came out of the room, and three consented introductions. This is the demonstration and it does the selling.

**T+2 to 3 weeks, only if the room landed.** Offer to come back with the list for a room at their office. No price attached to that offer.

**When the list is delivered.** If it is fillable, that is the moment a number is named, and the ladder starts at $25,000 (`docs/16-product-architecture.md`). If it is not fillable, say so and propose the room we can actually compose. Both outcomes preserve the relationship, which is the entire point.

**Not the $2,500 meetup, at any stage.** It sells exposure into a room where his buyers are absent.

### Nurture cadence after the dinner

He is a quarterly contact, not a monthly one. Only ever with something in hand: an introduction, a finding from a room in his category, a name he asked for. No check-ins. The ask that eventually matters is not "will you buy", it is "will you introduce me to two people", and it is only earned after something has been delivered.

## 8. What to verify before the commercial conversation

1. **Does Suite 401 hold 25 to 40 people seated?** Requirement 1 disqualifies more candidates than the other four combined. Ask plainly, or look for photographs of a past event at that address.
2. **Is there a Canadian-held field marketing, community or events role?** If the answer is no, this is a CEO discretionary spend rather than a budget line, which changes the size and the cadence.
3. **Have they run their own executive dinners or roundtables?** Highest-weight signal in the scoring model. If yes, the mechanic and the internal justification already exist.
4. **Any Canadian healthcare leader hired in the last twelve months?** Highest single trigger in the model.
5. **When does their fiscal year end?** Unspent field marketing budget is the cheapest money available.

## 9. Blockers this moves onto the critical path

A 272-person company with government and hospital customers has real vendor onboarding. Every item below is already on the `HANDOFF.md` not-done list and each one is now load-bearing rather than theoretical.

| Blocker | Consequence if unfixed | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| No registered business name | No invoice carrying a GST/HST number. Their accounts payable bounces it | $60 |
| No CGL certificate | Vendor onboarding stops, and any external venue is unavailable | $500 to $1,500/yr |
| No incorporation | Some procurement cannot set up a sole proprietorship as a vendor | $300 to $400 |
| No one-page agreement | Their legal team drafts from scratch, which costs weeks | Time |
| No composition report template | Nothing to deliver, so nothing to invoice against | Time |

Roughly $400 plus a premium. **Do this before the T+2 week conversation, not after it.**

## 10. Rules that do not bend here

- The name does not appear on the website, in social content, in a deck, or in anything a member reads. Not as a partner, not as a client, not as an attendee (`CLAUDE.md` rule 3, `ADR-007`).
- No sponsorship vocabulary at any point, including in private conversation. Co-host, brief, convening.
- He attends as a guest. Nobody in the room is his prospect and he is nobody's prospect (`docs/07-brand-and-voice.md`).
- One registration is not a track record. It does not become a claim, a counter, or a logo.
